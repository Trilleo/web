Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty: a single coin toss can't be predicted,
but a thousand of them follow rules you can calculate with. This course builds those
rules from the ground up. It starts with **events** — what can happen, and how likely
it is — then turns outcomes into numbers with **random variables** and their
distributions (binomial, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal), and finally studies
**pairs** of random variables: how they vary together, when they are independent, and
what the distribution of a sum is.

Every lesson starts from the idea, then the definitions and formulas, then worked
examples with real numbers. Play with the graphs — move a slider and watch a
distribution change — and finish with the quiz. Chinese terms sit beside the English
ones, so the lessons match the slides and the exam.

The course is still being taught: lessons follow the lectures up to 14 October 2026,
and new ones are added as the term goes on.
