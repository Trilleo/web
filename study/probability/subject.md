Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty: a single coin toss can't be predicted,
but a thousand of them follow rules you can calculate with. This course builds those
rules from the ground up. It starts with **events** — what can happen, and how likely
it is — then turns outcomes into numbers with **random variables** and their
distributions (binomial, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal), and studies **pairs**
of random variables: how they vary together, when they are independent, and what the
distribution of a sum, a maximum or a minimum is. **Expectation** and **variance** sum
a distribution up in a few numbers, and the **law of large numbers** and the **central
limit theorem** explain why averages settle down and sums become normal.

The second part is **statistics**: working back from a sample to the population it
came from. It introduces samples and the χ², t and F distributions, then **estimates**
unknown parameters (the method of moments, maximum likelihood, confidence intervals)
and **tests** claims about them.

Every lesson starts from the idea, then the definitions and formulas, then worked
examples with real numbers. Play with the graphs — move a slider and watch a
distribution change — and finish with the quiz. Chinese terms sit beside the English
ones, so the lessons match the slides and the exam.
