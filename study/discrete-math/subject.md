Computers deal in separate, countable things: bits, records, links, steps. Discrete
mathematics is the language for them. This course starts with **sets** — collections
and how to combine and count them — then **relations**, which say how the elements of
sets are connected (equal, smaller, related), and **functions**, which turn out to be
special relations and let us compare even infinite sets. The second half is **graph
theory**: dots joined by lines, a model for networks, maps and schedules, ending with the
classic questions of travelling every road once (Euler) or visiting every place once
(Hamilton).

Every lesson starts from the idea, then the definitions and theorems, then worked
examples. Look at the drawings — relation diagrams, Hasse diagrams, graphs — and finish
with the quiz. Chinese terms sit beside the English ones, so the lessons match the
slides and the exam.
