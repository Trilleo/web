This first lesson answers the question the whole course rests on: *what is Marxism?*
Most people have heard the name, but the course starts from a line of Hegel's,
{{zh 熟知并非真知}} ("what is familiar is not thereby truly known"): knowing the word is
not knowing the theory. Here you get the course map (what you will study and in which
order), the textbook's definition of Marxism in six points, what its "basic principles"
are, the three parts the theory is made of, and Marx's "two great discoveries". These
are the most quoted formulations of the introduction, and the exam asks for them
almost word for word, so the Chinese versions are given for you to learn.

## The course map

The course follows the textbook 《马克思主义基本原理》 (2023 edition, Higher Education
Press). It has an introduction and seven chapters, which fall into three groups. The
groups are not arbitrary: they are the three parts of Marxism itself, which this lesson
explains in its last sections.

| Part | Chapters | What it asks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marxist philosophy (马克思主义哲学) | 1 世界的物质性及发展规律; 2 实践与认识及其发展规律; 3 人类社会及其发展规律 | What is the world, how does it change, how do we know it, how does society develop? |
| Marxist political economy (马克思主义政治经济学) | 4 资本主义的本质及规律; 5 资本主义的发展及其趋势 | How does capitalism work, and where is it heading? |
| Scientific socialism (科学社会主义) | 6 社会主义的发展及其规律; 7 共产主义崇高理想及其最终实现 | How do socialism and communism come about? |

Marxist philosophy itself has two halves: {{term dialectical materialism | 辩证唯物主义}}
(chapters 1 and 2: matter, dialectics, knowledge) and
{{term historical materialism | 历史唯物主义}} (chapter 3: society). Together they are
what the course calls Marxism's **scientific worldview and methodology**.

::: callout pitfall "Materialism" is not enough
The in-class question asks what Marxism's scientific worldview and methodology is. The
answer is **dialectical materialism and historical materialism** together — not
"materialism" (old materialism existed long before Marx), and not just one of the two.
Watch for 辨证 on the slide: the character is 辩证.
:::

## Why study it: Marxism's reach

The course opens with the reasons it gives for studying Marxism. They are framed as
the course's own case for its subject; the exam rarely asks about them directly, but
they give you the examples teachers like to use.

**The size of the work.** The first Chinese edition of the *Collected Works of Marx and
Engels* (《马克思恩格斯全集》) runs to 50 volumes; the second is planned at 70. The
slides count over 2,000 articles, over 4,000 letters and some 32 million Chinese
characters, of which more than 100 pieces are joint work. The main works and their
years are listed in Lesson 1.2. (The slides also cite polls — the BBC's 1999 and 2005
listener polls and a 1999 Cambridge poll put Marx first as "thinker of the millennium" or
"greatest philosopher" — as a sign of his standing.)

**Marxism and the world.** The course names three kinds of influence:

- *Political*: after Marx, workers' and socialist parties were founded across Europe and
  the working class "stepped onto the stage of history". The
  {{term Second International | 第二国际}} (founded 1889) was the world organisation of
  these parties.
- *Economic*: Marx explained the nature of the capitalist mode of production and the
  roots of its economic crises; during the 2007–2008 financial crisis *Capital* sold out
  in Germany and elsewhere.
- *Intellectual*: Marx became part of mainstream Western thought, and a whole family of
  schools called {{term Western Marxism | 西方马克思主义}} grew from him (table below).

::: callout note The parties' dates on the slide
The slide lists parties with years (German SPD 1884, Austria 1890, Norway 1894…) that
don't match the usual founding dates: the German party was formed at Gotha in 1875 and
took the name SPD in 1890; the French SFIO dates from 1905 and the British Labour Party
took its name in 1906. The Second International ran from 1889 until it broke apart in the
First World War (the slide gives 1889–1916). The point the slide makes, that workers'
parties spread across Europe, stands.
:::

| Early Western Marxism | Lukács, Korsch, Gramsci, Bloch |
| Frankfurt School | Horkheimer, Adorno, Benjamin, Marcuse, Habermas, Schmidt |

**Marxism and China.** Marx's theory guided the Russian October Revolution of 1917,
which turned scientific socialism from a theory into a reality. In 1949 Mao Zedong,
looking back on 28 years of the Communist Party of China, wrote:
"十月革命一声炮响，给我们送来了马克思列宁主义" (the salvoes of the October Revolution
brought us Marxism-Leninism). The slide's arrow runs **Marxism → the Communist Party
of China → socialism with Chinese characteristics**, and the course quotes Xi Jinping's
2018 summary: the Party "can" and socialism with Chinese characteristics "is good"
because "马克思主义行，是中国化时代化的马克思主义行". How Marxism developed in China is
Lesson 1.3.

**Marxism and HIT.** The course's own university is its local case. In the 1950s HIT
built its teaching and research on Marxist philosophy and the dialectics of nature
(the "eight hundred heroes" spirit of its founding staff, under president 李昌, 1953–64).

::: example The "building-block" machine tool
In 1958 the Harbin 机联 machine factory built a modular ("积木式", building-block)
machine tool. In November 1960 HIT teachers and the factory published an article in
the *Guangming Daily* on "the law of contradictory motion inside machine tools"; it was
reprinted in *Red Flag*, caught Mao Zedong's attention, and he wrote to HIT asking for a
follow-up, published in 1961. What does it show about Marxism and engineering?
== The engineering problem ==
A big workpiece seemed to need a big machine. The modular design let small machines,
combined, do big jobs: "以小干大", using the small to do the big.
== The philosophy in it ==
The authors analysed the machine as a unity of contradictions and looked for the
**principal contradiction**: the relation between the size of the tool and the size of
the job. Grasping that one relation is what the design changed. (Contradictions and
the principal contradiction are Lesson 3.3.)
== The point for the course ==
The course uses the case to show materialist dialectics guiding real scientific and
engineering practice, not only books. The same idea returns in HIT's 2015 modular
self-reconfiguring robot (modules that connect and separate to change shape) and the
student 紫丁香 micro-satellite team, whose designs were tested by assembly, testing and
launch: practice tests the truth of a technical plan (Unit 4).
:::

**Marxism and the individual.** At 17, in his school-leaving essay
*Reflections of a Young Man on the Choice of a Profession* (1835), Marx wrote that if we
choose the profession in which we can most work for humanity, burdens cannot bow us
down, because they are sacrifices for all. The course uses it to ask what kind of life
you want.

## The six-point definition of Marxism

Now the definition. The textbook does not define Marxism in one line; it describes it
from six angles, each labelled on the slide with what it tells you. Learn the six
statements and their labels: a 简答题 can ask for them.

::: define Marxism | 马克思主义
The theory described from six angles:
1. a **scientific theoretical system** founded by Marx and Engels and continually
   developed by their successors (its creators and inheritors);
2. the theory of the **general laws of the development of nature, society and human
   thought** (its object of study);
3. the theory that **socialism must replace capitalism** and communism finally be
   reached (its main content);
4. the theory of the **liberation of the proletariat, of all humankind, and of the free
   and all-round development of every person** (its class character);
5. the **guiding ideology** of proletarian parties and socialist states (its class
   character);
6. the **guide to action** leading the people to create a better life (its social
   function).

> 马克思主义是由马克思和恩格斯创立并为后继者所不断发展的科学理论体系，是关于自然、社会和人类思维发展一般规律的学说，是关于社会主义必然代替资本主义、最终实现共产主义的学说，是关于无产阶级解放、全人类解放和每个人自由而全面发展的学说，是无产阶级政党和社会主义国家的指导思想，是指引人民创造美好生活的行动指南。
:::

Read the six points as answers to six questions: *who made it* (Marx, Engels and
successors — so "Marxism" includes Leninism and its Chinese developments, Lesson 1.3),
*what it studies* (the most general laws, so it is a worldview, not a specialised
science), *what it claims* (the course of history from capitalism to socialism and
communism), *whom it serves* (the proletariat and finally everyone), *what role it
plays* (a party's and a state's guiding ideology), and *what it is for* (action, not
contemplation).

::: callout note Two labels for "class character"
The slide labels both point 4 and point 5 阶级属性. Point 4 says *whose* liberation the
theory is about; point 5 says *who* takes it as their guiding ideology. Both answer
"which class does it stand for?", so the double label is not a mistake, just two sides
of one feature.
:::

The fourth point names Marxism's highest value goal. A past postgraduate exam
question (in the quiz) tells the story: in January 1894 the Italian socialist Giuseppe
Canepa asked Engels for a short motto for a new weekly, something to set against
Dante's description of the old era, "some rule, others suffer". Engels replied that he
could find nothing better than a sentence from the *Manifesto*:

> 代替那存在着阶级和阶级对立的资产阶级旧社会的，将是这样一个联合体，在那里，每个人的自由发展是一切人的自由发展的条件。

In place of the old society with its classes there will be an association in which **the
free development of each is the condition for the free development of all**.

::: callout key Marxism's value goal
The fundamental value goal Marxism pursues is **the free and all-round development of
every person** (人的自由而全面的发展), reached through the liberation of the proletariat
and of all humankind.
:::

## The basic principles: stand, viewpoints and method

The course is called *Basic Principles* of Marxism, so it is worth being exact about
what a "basic principle" is. Think of the difference between a doctor's general
knowledge of the body and one diagnosis made for one patient: the diagnosis can be
wrong for the next patient, the general knowledge stays. Marx and Engels made many
particular judgements about their own time (about particular countries, parties and
events); not all of them hold everywhere. The basic principles are the part that, in the
course's account, has been tested again and again and holds generally.

::: define Basic principles of Marxism | 马克思主义基本原理
A concentrated summary of Marxism's **stand, viewpoints and method**: the theory of
general truth established and repeatedly tested in practice as Marxism was formed,
developed and applied. They express Marxism's fundamental nature and overall character,
and, compared with particular judgements and conclusions made under particular
historical conditions, they have **universal, fundamental and long-term** guiding
significance.

> 马克思主义基本原理是对马克思主义立场、观点、方法的集中概括，是马克思主义在其形成、发展和运用过程中经过实践反复检验而确立起来的具有普遍真理性的理论。
:::

The three parts of that summary each have a definition.

::: define Basic stand | 基本立场
The fundamental footing and starting point from which Marxism observes, analyses and
solves problems: whose side it takes. In the course's account this is the stand of the
people, of the overwhelming majority.

> 马克思主义基本立场是马克思主义观察、分析和解决问题的根本立足点和出发点。
:::

::: define Basic viewpoints | 基本观点
Marxism's scientific understanding of the general laws of the development of nature,
society and human thought: a scientific summing-up of the results of human thought
and of the experience of social practice (for example: matter is primary; practice is
the basis of knowledge; the productive forces decide the relations of production).

> 马克思主义基本观点是关于自然、社会和人类思维发展一般规律的科学认识，是对人类思想成果和社会实践经验的科学总结。
:::

::: define Basic method | 基本方法
The ways of thinking and working, built on the worldview and methodology of
dialectical and historical materialism, that guide us to understand and change the
world correctly (for example: seeking truth from facts, analysing contradictions,
seeing things in their connections and development).

> 马克思主义基本方法是建立在辩证唯物主义和历史唯物主义世界观方法论基础上，指导我们正确认识世界、改造世界的思想方法和工作方法。
:::

| Part | Question it answers | Everyday picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic stand (基本立场) | From whose position do we look? | Where you stand decides what you see |
| Basic viewpoints (基本观点) | What do we know about how things are? | The map |
| Basic method (基本方法) | How do we think and work? | How to read the map and find a way |

::: callout key Grasp them as one
The slide's banner is the methodological requirement: **grasp the basic principles of
Marxism from the organic unity of its basic stand, viewpoints and method**
(从基本立场、基本观点、基本方法的有机统一中把握马克思主义基本原理). A stand without
viewpoints is only an attitude; viewpoints without method are dead formulas; a method
without a stand can serve any end.
:::

::: callout pitfall Principles are not every sentence Marx wrote
"Basic principles" ≠ "everything in the classics". Individual judgements made under
particular historical conditions can be outdated; the basic principles keep their
universal, fundamental, long-term guiding role. Exams build multiple-choice traps on
this: "every conclusion of Marxism applies forever" is wrong; so is "since some
conclusions are outdated, the basic principles are outdated too".
:::

## The three components of Marxism

What does the theory actually contain? Lenin's short article *The Three Sources and
Three Component Parts of Marxism* (1913) gave the classic division, and the textbook
keeps it. Each part plays a role in a single argument: philosophy gives the way of
seeing, political economy applies it to capitalism and proves the conclusion,
scientific socialism draws the conclusion for action.

::: define Three components of Marxism | 马克思主义的三个基本组成部分
**Marxist philosophy** (dialectical materialism — its materialism, dialectics and theory
of knowledge — and historical materialism), the **foundation** of the theory;
**Marxist political economy**, which scientifically reveals the nature of the
capitalist mode of production and the secret of capitalist exploitation, the theory's
**proof**; and **scientific socialism**, which shows the historical conditions and
mission of the proletariat's complete liberation, the theory's **goal**. The three are
an organic unity.

> 马克思主义哲学是理论的基础，马克思主义政治经济学是理论的论证，科学社会主义是理论的归宿。
:::

| Component | Its role (中文) | What it does | Textbook chapters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marxist philosophy | Foundation (理论的基础) | The worldview and method: dialectical and historical materialism | 1–3 |
| Marxist political economy | Proof (理论的论证) | Reveals the nature of capitalist production and the secret of exploitation | 4–5 |
| Scientific socialism | Goal (理论的归宿) | Shows the conditions and mission of the proletariat's liberation | 6–7 |

::: callout pitfall Components are not sources
The **three components** (philosophy, political economy, scientific socialism) are what
Marxism *is made of*. The **three sources** (German classical philosophy, English
classical political economy, British and French utopian socialism; Lesson 1.2) are what
it *grew out of*. They pair up one to one, which is why they are easy to mix up in a
multiple-choice question.
:::

## Marx's two great discoveries

Among all Marx's ideas, two are singled out as the ones that made socialism a science.
At Marx's grave in March 1883 Engels compared him to Darwin: as Darwin discovered the
law of development of living nature, Marx discovered the law of development of human
history, and also the special law of motion of capitalism, surplus value. In
*Socialism: Utopian and Scientific* (1880) Engels had already said that with these two
discoveries socialism became a science.

::: define Materialist conception of history | 唯物史观
Also called **historical materialism** (历史唯物主义). It reveals the general laws of
the development of human history, the role of the masses as the subjects of history,
and the great role of class struggle in class societies. It puts the pursuit of socialism
on a scientific understanding of the objective laws of social development, overcoming
the utopian socialists' basic defect: they did not understand the laws of history.
:::

::: define Theory of surplus value | 剩余价值学说
Also called the **theory of surplus value** (剩余价值理论). It reveals the secret of how
capitalists exploit workers (workers create more value than they are paid for, and the
capitalist keeps the surplus) and the fundamental opposition between the interests of
the proletariat and the bourgeoisie. It proves the proletariat's historical mission to
overthrow the old capitalist world and build a new socialist one, and shows the force
and the road for that change.
:::

::: callout key The two discoveries
**唯物史观和剩余价值学说是马克思一生的两个伟大发现。由于这两个伟大发现，社会主义实现了从空想到科学的飞跃。**
The materialist conception of history and the theory of surplus value are Marx's two
great discoveries; with them socialism made the leap from utopia to science
({{term scientific socialism | 科学社会主义}}). Either name for each is accepted in
answers (唯物史观 = 历史唯物主义; 剩余价值学说 = 剩余价值理论).
:::

Why exactly these two? Utopian socialists before Marx (Lesson 1.2) condemned
capitalism as unjust and drew pictures of a better society, but could not say *why*
it would come or *who* would bring it. The materialist conception of history answers
the "why": society changes by its own laws, so socialism is a necessity of history, not
a wish. The theory of surplus value answers the "who": exploitation sets the proletariat
against the bourgeoisie, so the proletariat is the force that will change society.

::: example From utopia to science
A student writes: "Marx was the first person to want socialism." Assess the claim with
what this lesson says.
== What is wrong ==
Socialist ideals are much older: Saint-Simon, Fourier and Owen criticised capitalism and
imagined new societies before Marx (Lesson 1.2), and older dreams of a just society go
back much further.
== What Marx added ==
What is new is not the wish but the **science**: the materialist conception of history
showed socialism as the outcome of the laws of social development, and the theory of
surplus value found the force (the proletariat) and the road to it.
== Conclusion ==
Marx did not invent socialism; with his two great discoveries he turned it from a
utopia into a science. The course's phrase is "从空想到科学的飞跃".
:::
