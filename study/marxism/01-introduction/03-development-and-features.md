Lessons 1.1 and 1.2 showed what Marxism is and how it was founded. This lesson follows
it forward: how Lenin developed it in a new era, how it was combined with China's
conditions in three "leaps", what the course says are its four distinguishing features,
why it says Marxism still matters today, and how the course asks students to study it.
The order of the leaps, the four features and the reason "Marxism works" are favourite
exam questions; the lesson ends with the course's discussion question on science and
philosophy.

The slide's rising arrow is the map of the whole development:
**the founding of Marxism → Leninism → Mao Zedong Thought → the theoretical system of
socialism with Chinese characteristics → Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese
Characteristics for a New Era.**

## Lenin and Leninism

**A new question.** From the 1870s to the early twentieth century capitalism changed:
free competition gave way to **monopoly**, and the proletarian movement met new
conditions. Russia entered the imperialist stage while keeping remnants of serfdom in
its economy and tsarist autocracy in its politics. The slide's question echoes the one
Marx faced: where is capitalism going, and where is Russia going?

::: thinker Vladimir Lenin | 列宁
dates: 1870–1924
works: *What Is to Be Done?* (1902); *Two Tactics of Social-Democracy in the Democratic Revolution* (1905); *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* (1908); *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism* (1916); *The State and Revolution* (1917)
Russian revolutionary and the founder of Leninism. In the course's account he summed up
the new experience of the Russian and international workers' movements, generalised the
new results of science, and fought mistaken currents of thought, and so developed
Marxism comprehensively. He led the October Revolution of 1917 and the first steps of
building socialism in Soviet Russia. His definition of matter is Lesson 2.2.
:::

His most quoted line, from *What Is to Be Done?*: "没有革命的理论，就没有革命的
行动" — without revolutionary theory there can be no revolutionary movement. Theory
guides action, which is why he developed it.

| Work | Year | What it contributed, in the course's account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Two Tactics of Social-Democracy in the Democratic Revolution* (《社会民主党在民主革命中的两种策略》) | 1905 | Summed up the 1905 revolution, criticised Menshevik opportunism, set out the theory of the proletariat's leadership in the democratic revolution |
| *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* (《唯物主义和经验批判主义》) | 1908 | Defended and developed Marxist philosophy against Machism, including the definition of matter |
| *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism* (《帝国主义是资本主义的最高阶段》) | 1916 | Analysed imperialism's nature, features, contradictions and trend: imperialism is capitalism's highest stage, so socialist revolution can win first in one or a few countries |
| *The State and Revolution* (《国家与革命》) | 1917 | Enriched the theory of the dictatorship of the proletariat and developed the Marxist theory of the state |

::: callout note How the slide pairs the books
The slide puts one box under two books (*Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* and *Two
Tactics*) about the 1905 revolution and the Mensheviks, and puts "the proletariat's
leadership in the democratic revolution" under *The State and Revolution*. The usual
account, used in the table, gives the leadership theory to *Two Tactics* and makes
*Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* Lenin's philosophical work against Machism.
:::

::: define Leninism | 列宁主义
Marxism of the era of **imperialism and proletarian revolution**
(帝国主义和无产阶级革命时代的马克思主义). Lenin combined the basic principles of
Marxism with Russia's concrete reality while leading the Russian revolution and
socialist construction, founded Leninism, and took Marxism to a new historical stage.
:::

The **October Revolution** of 1917, in the course's words, was a great practice
guided by Marxism that turned scientific socialism from theory into reality. After it
Lenin and the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks) explored how a country with a
relatively backward economy and culture could make the transition to socialism. After
Lenin's death Stalin led the Soviet party and people in continuing that work in the first
socialist country.

## Marxism adapted to China and the times

::: define Adapting Marxism to China and the times | 马克思主义中国化时代化
The process by which Marxism develops in China: combining **the basic principles of
Marxism with China's concrete reality** and **with China's fine traditional culture**
(the "two combinations", 两个结合), so as to answer the questions of China and of the
times. Its three historic leaps creatively enriched and developed Marxism.

> 马克思主义在中国的发展过程就是马克思主义中国化时代化的过程。
:::

::: law Marxism is a guide to action, not a dogma | 马克思主义不是教条，而是行动指南
Marxism must develop as practice changes. Its history is the history of Marx, Engels
and their successors developing it as times, practice and knowledge developed, and of
absorbing all the best results of human thought and culture.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Hold to the basic principles, but don't treat the
classics as a book of ready answers: combine the principles with concrete reality
(Lenin with Russia's, the Chinese Communists with China's) and with the culture of the
country, and develop theory as practice gives it new questions. Both mistakes are
possible: dogmatism (copying formulas regardless of conditions) and abandoning the
basic principles.

| Leap | Result | Main representative | Key point on the slides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First historic leap (第一次历史性飞跃) | Mao Zedong Thought (毛泽东思想) | Mao Zedong | The creative application and development of Marxism-Leninism in China; correct principles and experience of China's revolution and construction, proved by practice |
| A new leap (又一次飞跃) | The theoretical system of socialism with Chinese characteristics (中国特色社会主义理论体系) | Deng Xiaoping, Jiang Zemin, Hu Jintao | Deng Xiaoping Theory, the "Three Represents", the Scientific Outlook on Development |
| A new leap (新的飞跃) | Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era (习近平新时代中国特色社会主义思想) | Xi Jinping | The latest result: "contemporary Chinese Marxism, twenty-first-century Marxism" |

::: define Mao Zedong Thought | 毛泽东思想
The creative application and development of Marxism-Leninism in China: the correct
theoretical principles and summary of experience about China's revolution and
construction, proved correct by practice. It achieved **the first historic leap** in
adapting Marxism to China and the times.
:::

::: define Theoretical system of socialism with Chinese characteristics | 中国特色社会主义理论体系
**Deng Xiaoping Theory** (邓小平理论), the important thought of the **"Three
Represents"** ("三个代表"重要思想) and the **Scientific Outlook on Development**
(科学发展观). It achieved another leap in adapting Marxism to China and the times.
:::

1997 | Deng Xiaoping Theory (15th Congress) | 党的十五大
2002 | The "Three Represents" (16th Congress) | 党的十六大
2012 | The Scientific Outlook on Development established as guiding thought (18th Congress; written into the constitution in 2007) | 党的十八大

:::

::: define Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era | 习近平新时代中国特色社会主义思想
Created by the Chinese Communists with Xi Jinping as their chief representative, by
combining Marxism's basic principles with China's concrete reality and its fine
traditional culture. It answers the major questions of the new era — what kind of
socialism with Chinese characteristics to uphold and develop and how; what kind of
modern socialist country to build and how; what kind of long-governing Marxist party to
build and how. In the course's account it is the latest theoretical result of adapting
Marxism to China and the times, "contemporary Chinese Marxism and twenty-first-century
Marxism", and achieved a new leap.
:::

::: callout pitfall Order and counting of the leaps
Mao Zedong Thought is the **first** leap; the theoretical system of socialism with
Chinese characteristics is the next; Xi Jinping Thought is the newest. Leninism is
**not** one of these leaps: it developed Marxism in Russia, before its adaptation to
China.
:::

## The four distinguishing features

::: define Distinguishing features of Marxism | 马克思主义的鲜明特征
**Scientific** (科学性): a scientific theory that creatively reveals the laws of
human social development. **Of the people** (人民性): the people's theory, the first
system of thought for the people's own liberation. **Practical** (实践性): a theory of
practice, guiding the people's action to change the world. **Developing** (发展性): an
open theory that keeps developing and always stands at the forefront of the times.
Together: **the unity of its scientific and its revolutionary character**
(科学性与革命性的统一).
:::

The course illustrates each with Xi Jinping's speech for Marx's 200th birthday
(4 May 2018):

- **Scientific.** Marx founded the materialist conception of history and the theory of
  surplus value, revealed the general law of human social development and the special law
  of capitalism's operation, and showed the way from the "realm of necessity" to the
  "realm of freedom" (必然王国 → 自由王国).
- **Of the people.** Marxism for the first time sought the road to human freedom from the
  people's standpoint; it has influence across countries and ages because it is rooted in
  the people.
- **Practical.** The viewpoint of practice and of life is the basic viewpoint of
  Marxist epistemology; practicality is what most clearly distinguishes Marxism from other
  theories. It is not book-learning in a study, but formed and developed in the people's
  practice of liberation (compare thesis 11, Lesson 1.2).
- **Developing.** Marxism is not dogma but a guide to action, developing with practice
  (the law above); this is why it "stays forever young".

| Feature | Which side of the unity | Short form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scientific (科学性) | Scientific character | It reveals the laws of social development |
| Of the people (人民性) | Revolutionary character | It stands with the people |
| Practical (实践性) | Revolutionary character | It is for changing the world |
| Developing (发展性) | Revolutionary character | It keeps developing with the times |

::: callout key One sentence
The distinguishing features of Marxism, in one sentence: **the unity of its scientific
and revolutionary character**. Being scientific means it states the laws of history;
being revolutionary means it takes the people's side and sets out to change the world.
:::

## Its contemporary value

When the socialist movement was at a low ebb, Marxism met three objections: that it is
**outdated** ("过时", a theory from over 170 years ago), **useless** ("无用", since
capitalism's basic contradiction has eased), and **failing** ("失灵", unable to meet the
challenge of the information revolution). The course replies that Marx's thought came
from its time but went beyond it, the essence of that age's spirit and of human spirit
as a whole, and that it has deeply changed both the world and China. It gives Marxism
three roles today.

| Role | What the course says it gives us |
| --- | --- |
| A tool for observing the changing world (观察当代世界变化的认识工具) | A broad view; a sharp eye to see through the storms of the age; a long view and strategic composure |
| A guide to action for China's development (指引当代中国发展的行动指南) | The spiritual banner and the spiritual driving force of China's development — "standing up, growing rich, growing strong" |
| A scientific truth leading human progress (引领人类社会进步的科学真理) | An answer to "what is wrong with the world, where is humanity going?" |

The slides' cases: the 2008 financial crisis, the 2011 Occupy Wall Street movement and
the 2022 Ukraine crisis as events to be understood by **seeing the essence through the
phenomena** and the whole through local changes; and rapid technical progress alongside
widening gaps between rich and poor (the slide's figure: the top 0.1% of Americans earn
196 times the bottom 90% on average), global warming (2021 about 1.09 °C above
1850–1900) and terrorism, as problems the course says Marxism helps humanity face.
The course's phrase for the dialectical way of looking: **see change in motion, and
development in change** (从运动中看到变化，从变化中看到发展).

::: example The 2022 exam question: why does Marxism "work"?
The Party, the question says, upholds the basic principles of Marxism, seeks truth
from facts, starts from China's reality and keeps adapting Marxism to China and the
times; Xi Jinping says the Party "can" and socialism with Chinese characteristics "is
good" because "Marxism works". What is the **fundamental** reason Marxism works?
== Sort the options ==
A clear political stand, a conscious sense of historical responsibility, a weapon for
the party's self-revolution — each is true of Marxism in the course's account, but none
is the *root*.
== Find the root ==
Marxism works because it is a **scientific worldview and methodology**: it reveals the
laws of nature, society and thought, so action guided by it fits the objective laws.
This is its scientific character, the first of the four features.
== Model answer ==
The fundamental reason is that Marxism is a scientific worldview and methodology. It
reveals the general laws of human social development; applied to China's concrete
reality, it has guided successful practice. Hence "Marxism works", and specifically
"Marxism adapted to China and the times works".
:::

## How to study and apply Marxism

The course's last section turns to the student. It names four tasks.

1. **Learn the basic stand, viewpoints and method.** The most fundamental thing is to
   grasp them (Lesson 1.1), understand Marxism's essence, form a correct worldview and
   methodology, think scientifically, and improve your ability to analyse and solve
   problems.
2. **Learn the results of adapting Marxism to China and the times**, above all Xi
   Jinping Thought, and its worldview and methodology, whose core is the **"six musts"**
   (六个必须坚持): put the people first (人民至上); have self-confidence and
   self-reliance (自信自立); uphold fundamental principles and break new ground (守正创新);
   be problem-oriented (问题导向); apply systems thinking (系统观念); and keep the whole
   world in mind (胸怀天下).
3. **Link theory with practice** (理论联系实际的马克思主义学风): connect it with
   China's objective reality, especially the new realities of the new era, and with your
   own reality, remoulding your subjective world.
4. **Take it to heart and put it into action** (内化于心、外化于行): build integrity
   (read the originals), temper your thinking, raise your outlook, and guide practice.

The two slogans that sum it up: **study, thought and application joined up**
(学、思、用贯通: study lays the theoretical foundation, thinking grasps its truth,
application confirms its power) and **knowledge, belief and action unified**
(知、信、行统一).

The slide's models for the third point are two scientists. The geologist **Li Siguang**
(李四光) consciously used dialectics in his research and founded **geomechanics**
(地质力学). The rocket scientist **Qian Xuesen** (钱学森) said that Marxist philosophy is
truly a treasure and a sharp weapon, and that a scientist who drops it in research is
foolish. That leads straight to the course's discussion question.

::: example Think it through: science and philosophy
The week 2 discussion question: drawing on everyday experience and your own field,
discuss the relation between science and philosophy, with examples. (What philosophy
is, and worldview and methodology, are defined in Lesson 2.1.)
== How they differ ==
A science studies one field of the world (physics motion and matter, biology life,
engineering how to make things work) and its particular laws. Philosophy studies the
world **as a whole** — its most general nature and laws, and our relation to it — and so
gives a worldview and a methodology.
== Science feeds philosophy ==
Philosophy generalises the results of science. Lesson 1.2's three discoveries are the
case: cell theory, the conservation and transformation of energy and evolution showed
that nature is connected and develops, and gave dialectical materialism its scientific
basis. Each great advance in science changes the form of materialism (Engels's point,
taken up in Lesson 2.2).
== Philosophy guides science ==
A scientist always works with some worldview and method, consciously or not. The
"hothouse flower" who only collects facts finds no laws; Li Siguang used dialectics to
found geomechanics; HIT's "building-block" machine tool came from finding the principal
contradiction (Lesson 1.1). A student example: debugging a system by looking for the
one bottleneck that decides the whole is the same method.
== Model answer ==
Science and philosophy are dialectically united: they differ — the sciences study the
particular laws of particular fields, philosophy the most general laws of the world as a
whole — and they are connected. Science is the basis of philosophy: philosophy draws on
and generalises scientific results, as the three great nineteenth-century discoveries
supported dialectical materialism. Philosophy guides science: it gives science a
worldview and methodology, as dialectics guided Li Siguang's geomechanics and HIT's
modular machine tools. So scientists should consciously study a correct philosophy, and
philosophy must keep summing up new scientific results.
:::

::: callout pitfall Science is not "above" philosophy, nor the other way round
Two wrong answers to avoid: "philosophy is the science of sciences and can replace
them" (it can't: it gives general method, not particular laws), and "science has made
philosophy unnecessary" (every science still works with some worldview; the question is
only whether it is a correct one).
:::
