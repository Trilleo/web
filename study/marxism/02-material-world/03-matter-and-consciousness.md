If the world is matter (Lesson 2.2), where do our thoughts fit in? This lesson answers
with the **dialectical relation between matter and consciousness**: matter determines
consciousness, and consciousness acts back on matter. That pair gives two of the most
quoted methodologies in Chinese political writing — **start from reality and seek
truth from facts** (一切从实际出发，实事求是) and **bring subjective initiative into
play** while respecting objective laws. It ends with a question from today: can AI
become conscious? Expect all of this in multiple-choice cases and in material-analysis
questions.

## What consciousness is

Your brain has close to a hundred billion nerve cells, linked into channels that carry
signals. Consciousness depends on that organ — but it is not just electricity in it:
it is *about* something, an image of the world.

::: define Consciousness | 意识
A function and property of the **human brain**, and a **subjective image of the
material world**.

> 意识是人脑的机能和属性，是客观世界的主观映象。
:::

Both halves matter. "Function of the brain" says where consciousness comes from (a
material organ). "Image of the world" says what it contains (the world outside). The
slides' map of this lesson:

| Relation | What it says | Its methodology |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matter determines consciousness 物质决定意识 | Consciousness comes from matter and reflects it | Start from reality, seek truth from facts |
| Consciousness acts back on matter 意识对物质具有能动作用 | Consciousness can guide practice and change the world | Bring subjective initiative into play |

## Matter determines consciousness: where consciousness comes from

### A product of nature's long development

Consciousness did not appear from nowhere. In the course's account it is the end of a
long chain of ever more complex ways that matter responds to the world (反映), in three
key steps:

1. From the **reactive property of all matter** to the **irritability of simple
   living things**. Even a stone "responds": water dripping on it wears a hole
   (水滴石穿). Simple organisms go further: a mimosa folds its leaves when touched
   (刺激感应性).
2. From irritability to the **sensation and mind of higher animals**: a cat sees,
   hears and remembers; a gorilla can solve simple problems (感觉和心理).
3. From animal mind to **human consciousness**: inner models of the world, beliefs,
   rules and values that steer what people do.

### A product of social history: labour and language

The step from animal mind to human consciousness needed more than nature. In the
course's account, **driven by labour and language, the ape brain evolved into the
human brain** ({{zh 在劳动和语言的推动下，猿脑进化成了人脑}}). Apes that used tools to
work, hunted together and had to coordinate came to need speech. Engels wrote that
these "men in the making" reached a point where they had something to say to each
other, and the undeveloped larynx of the ape was slowly but surely transformed
({{zh 这些正在形成中的人，已经到了彼此间有些什么非说不可的地步了}}; *The Part Played by
Labour in the Transition from Ape to Man*, in *Dialectics of Nature*).

::: example The wolf child and the pig child
In 1920, near the town of Midnapore in India, a girl raised by wolves was found and
taken to an orphanage, where she was named Kamala. She walked on all fours, saw well in
the dark and made sharp sounds that were neither human nor animal. In 1983, in a
Chinese village, a little girl was found living among pigs: she grunted and shook her
head like them. But she had also lived with her parents, so she had not fully lost her
human social nature: she learned to dress, eat properly and speak a little. What do the
two cases show about consciousness?
== The brain alone is not enough ==
Kamala had a human brain, yet without human society she did not develop human
consciousness: no language, no human ways of thinking.
== Society makes the difference ==
The pig child, who kept some contact with people, kept some human consciousness and
could recover more. How much consciousness each had followed how much social life
each had.
== Conclusion ==
Consciousness is a product of **nature** (it needs the human brain) **and** of **social
history** (it forms only in society, through labour and language). 意识是自然界长期发展的产物，也是社会历史发展的产物.
:::

## The essence of consciousness

::: define Essence of consciousness | 意识的本质
Consciousness is a **subjective image of the material world**, the unity of **objective
content** and **subjective form**.

> 意识是物质世界的主观映象，是客观内容和主观形式的统一。
:::

Marx put it: {{zh 观念的东西不外是移入人的头脑并在人的头脑中改造过的物质的东西而已}} —
the ideal is nothing but the material world transposed into the human head and
translated there (*Capital*, volume 1, afterword to the second edition, 1873).

- **Objective content**: what consciousness is *about* comes from outside. Even
  fantasy is built from real parts. Lu Xun wrote that however much geniuses boast, they
  can't create from nothing: when they paint gods and ghosts, they give a person a
  third eye or a neck two or three feet longer — that's all.
- **Subjective form**: *how* it appears depends on the person. Look at one autumn
  through four poets. Li Qingzhao: rain on the parasol tree at dusk, "how can one word,
  sorrow, hold it all?" Du Fu: a lone goose at the frontier, "the moon is brightest over
  home". Liu Yuxi: "since ancient times autumn has meant sadness; I say autumn beats
  spring". Mao Zedong: "autumn wind is strong… unlike spring, yet better than spring".
  The same autumn, four images: objective content, subjective form.

The slides add the physiological side: the brain works with a **first signal system**
(reacting to real stimuli, like Pavlov's dog salivating at a bell — the conditioned
reflex) and, in humans only, a **second signal system**: **language and writing**,
signals of signals. That is why language is the outer form of consciousness.

::: callout pitfall "Subjective image" doesn't mean "made up"
"Subjective" names the *form* (it happens in a person's head, shaped by their
experience), not the *content*. A correct image and a wrong one are both subjective
images of something objective; even a mistake has its source in the world. That is the
materialist **theory of reflection** (反映论).
:::

::: law Matter determines consciousness | 物质决定意识
Matter is primary and consciousness secondary: consciousness arises from matter (it is
a product of nature and of social history), depends on it (a function of the brain)
and reflects it (its content is objective).
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Do everything **starting from reality**, and **seek
truth from facts** ({{zh 一切从实际出发，实事求是}}): base decisions on how things
actually are, not on wishes, books or orders alone.

## Consciousness acts back on matter

Xunzi: {{zh 心不使焉，则白黑在前而目不见，雷鼓在侧而耳不闻}} — when the mind is not on
it, black and white are before your eyes and you don't see them, thunderous drums
beside you and you don't hear them (《荀子·解蔽》). Consciousness is not a passive
mirror: it chooses what to see, and then steers what we do.

::: law Consciousness acts back on matter | 意识对物质具有能动的反作用
Consciousness has an active (能动) role. It shows in four ways:

1. it is **purposive and planned** (目的性和计划性): people act with a goal and a plan
   in mind;
2. it is **creative** (创造性): it can go beyond what is given, form concepts and
   theories, and design things that don't yet exist;
3. it **guides practice to change the objective world** (指导实践改造客观世界);
4. it **regulates human behaviour and physiological activity** (调控人的行为活动和生理活动):
   mood affects health, confidence affects performance.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Value the role of correct ideas, theory and spirit, and
bring subjective initiative into play — within the limits set out in the next section.
Correct consciousness helps practice; wrong consciousness hinders it.

::: callout pitfall "Active role" is not "consciousness decides"
Consciousness acts *back on* matter (反作用); it does not *determine* it. Its effect
works only **through practice** and only within **objective laws**. "If you believe it
strongly enough, it will happen" crosses the line into idealism.
:::

### How sharp is the line between matter and consciousness?

Lenin, in *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism*, says the opposition between matter and
consciousness has **absolute** meaning only within a very limited field: the basic
epistemological question of **which is primary and which secondary**. Beyond that, the
opposition is **relative** — consciousness is itself a property of highly organised
matter, it acts back on matter, and the two are unified in practice.

## Objective laws and subjective initiative

::: define Objective laws | 客观规律性
The laws of things that exist independently of human will: people can't create,
abolish or change them, only discover and use them.
:::

::: define Subjective initiative | 主观能动性
People's capacity to know and change the world consciously and actively: their
consciousness's active role, and the practical activity it guides.
:::

::: law The dialectical unity of objective laws and subjective initiative | 客观规律性和主观能动性的辩证统一
First, **respecting objective laws is the premise** of bringing subjective initiative
into play. Second, **only by fully bringing subjective initiative into play** can people
correctly know and use objective laws. The two are united in **practice**.
:::

**Methodological requirement — how to bring initiative into play correctly:**

1. **Starting from reality** is the premise (从实际出发是前提).
2. **Practice** is the fundamental way (实践是根本途径).
3. It **depends on certain material conditions and means** (依赖于一定的物质条件和物质手段).

::: example Pulling up the seedlings, and the planned harvest
The farmer of Song in 《孟子》 thought his rice grew too slowly, so he pulled each
seedling up a little; next morning they were all withered (揠苗助长). Compare a farmer
who studies the soil and the season, chooses a fitting variety and irrigates in time.
== Where the first farmer went wrong ==
He was very active, but against the law of how plants grow: initiative without
respect for objective laws. The result was failure.
== Where the second one is right ==
She first learns the laws (soil, season, plant), then acts on them with planning and
effort, using material means (water, tools). Laws respected, initiative fully used.
== And the opposite mistake ==
"Plants grow by themselves, there's nothing to do" respects laws but gives up
initiative: fatalism. The laws are only known and used *through* active practice.
== Conclusion ==
Respect objective laws as the premise, and bring initiative into play through practice
and with material means — the unity of the two.
:::

::: example The in-class question: 2019's buzzwords
In December 2019 the magazine 《咬文嚼字》 chose the year's ten buzzwords, among them
文明互鉴 (mutual learning between civilisations), 区块链 (blockchain), 硬核 (hardcore),
柠檬精 (envious "lemon spirit"), 996 (the 9-to-9, six-day work week), 我太难了 ("it's
so hard for me") and 霸凌主义 (bullying in international affairs). Buzzwords reflect
their era. What does that show?
== Matter determines consciousness ==
Each phrase reflects something real in society that year: new technology, working
hours, international relations. Social existence comes first and the words follow.
== An active image, made by society ==
The phrases are not copies but lively, often witty images (柠檬精, 我太难了): an
**active** reflection. And they arise from social life, not from one brain: consciousness
is a **product of social history**.
== Language ==
They are words: language is the outer form in which consciousness shows itself.
== Conclusion ==
All four readings fit: matter determines consciousness; consciousness is an active
image of the objective world; consciousness is a product of social history; language is
the external form of consciousness (in-class answer ABCD).
:::

## Consciousness and artificial intelligence

The slides end the section with cases from AI's recent history:

::: timeline AI cases on the slides
March 2016 | AlphaGo beats world Go champion Lee Sedol 4–1 | 李世石与 AlphaGo 人机大战
October 2017 | The robot Sophia is granted Saudi Arabian citizenship, the first robot to get one | 机器人索菲娅
February 2018 | Robots by Siasun (新松) perform with skaters in "Beijing 8 Minutes" at the PyeongChang Winter Olympics closing ceremony | 北京8分钟
November 2020 | AlphaFold largely solves the long-standing protein-folding problem | 阿尔法折叠
2025 | XPeng shows its new-generation humanoid robot IRON | 小鹏 IRON 人形机器人
:::

Two questions follow: will AI one day have **self-consciousness** as a subject? And
might it escape human control with devastating effects?

In the course's account, AI shows that human consciousness has reached a new stage:
part of its activity is **separated from the brain and made material in machines**, as
physical processes that extend the organs of consciousness. **But AI cannot replace or
surpass human intelligence**, for four reasons:

1. **Knowing, feeling and willing.** Human consciousness unites cognition, emotion and
   will (知情意); AI only simulates and extends human **rational** intelligence, without
   emotions, beliefs or will.
2. **Social nature.** Being social is an essential property of human consciousness
   (remember the wolf child); AI cannot truly have human social properties.
3. **Natural language.** Human language is the material shell of thinking and the real
   form of consciousness; AI finds it hard to fully grasp the real meaning of natural
   language.
4. **What can't be digitised.** AI can take in the parts of consciousness that can be
   reduced to digital signals, but there is always much in the human mind that can't
   be.

Looking at the overall trend, AI will come ever closer to human thinking and look more
and more "human" in the **outward forms of thinking**, but only as **imitative
approach** (模仿性趋近). Whether a future AI could have true self-consciousness and
threaten humanity is something to keep watching. The course's conclusion: AI is still
developing; we should observe it with an **open, objective attitude**, and **guide and
regulate** it towards what benefits human survival and development.

::: example Think it through: can AI have self-consciousness?
The teacher's discussion question (week 2): will artificial intelligence one day have
self-consciousness as a subject? Will it escape human control and cause devastating
harm?
== What AI is, in this framework ==
AI is part of the activity of consciousness, made material in machines by people: a
product of human labour and an extension of human organs of thought. It is, in this
sense, a result of consciousness's creativity, not a new source of consciousness.
== Why it doesn't amount to human consciousness ==
Consciousness is a function of the human brain and a product of social history (labour,
language, society). AI lacks the unity of knowing, feeling and willing, real social
nature and full understanding of natural language, and handles only what can be made
digital. So its approach to human thinking is imitation.
== The control question ==
Whether AI harms or helps depends on how people design, use and govern it: human
practice and social relations decide. That calls for watching its development
seriously, and for rules that steer it.
== Model answer ==
意识是人脑的机能和属性，是客观世界的主观映象，是自然界长期发展和社会历史发展的产物。人工智能是人类意识活动部分地从人脑中分化出来、物化为机器的物理运动，是人类智能的延伸，但不能取代或超越人类智能：第一，人类意识是知情意的统一体，人工智能只是对理性智能的模拟和扩展，不具备情感、信念、意志；第二，社会性是人类意识的本质属性，人工智能不可能真正具备；第三，自然语言是思维的物质外壳和意识的现实形式，人工智能难以完全理解其真实意义；第四，人脑中有许多内容无法化约为数字信号。人工智能在思维的表现形式上会越来越"像"人，但只是模仿性趋近。对其未来发展和可能的风险应时刻关注，以开放、客观的态度观察和把握，并引导、规范其向更有利于人类生存和发展的方向发展。
:::
