Lesson 2.1 said materialism takes matter as the origin of the world. But what *is*
matter? Water? Atoms? Quarks? This lesson follows how materialists answered, from
ancient guesses to Lenin's definition — matter is **objective reality** — which is the
foundation of everything else in the course. Then it asks how matter exists: always in
**motion**, with rest only relative, and always in **time and space**. Finally it shows
how human practice "doubles" the world. The definition of matter, "motion is absolute,
rest is relative" and the methodology "act according to time, place and conditions" are
classic exam material.

## What is the origin of the world?

Raphael's *School of Athens* shows the Greek philosophers arguing; the slide's caption
asks the question they all started from: {{zh 何为世界的本原？}} — what is the origin of
the world? An "origin" (本原) has two meanings on the slide:

- the **basic stuff** (基质) that things are made of;
- the **principle** (原则): the reason things exist and move.

Materialists answer: matter. But their idea of matter went through three historical
forms ({{zh 唯物主义物质观的三种历史形态}}). The first two together are called the
**old materialist** view of matter (旧唯物主义物质观).

## Ancient naive materialism: matter = concrete forms

In {{term ancient naive materialism | 古代朴素唯物主义}}, the origin of the world is
**one or a few concrete forms of matter** (物质 = 具体物质形态). The slides' three cases:

- **China's Five Phases** (五行说): metal, wood, water, fire and earth (金木水火土) make
  everything, in cycles of generation (wood feeds fire, fire makes earth (ash), earth
  bears metal, metal gathers water, water feeds wood) and of overcoming (wood breaks
  earth, earth dams water, water quenches fire, fire melts metal, metal cuts wood).
- **Democritus' atoms**: keep cutting a thing and you reach tiny, uncuttable pieces —
  "the bricks of the universe" (宇宙之砖).
- **Ancient India's four great elements** (四大): water, earth, fire, wind.

Xunzi's {{zh 天地合而万物生，阴阳接而变化起}} ("heaven and earth unite and the myriad
things are born; yin and yang meet and change arises") belongs here too.

::: thinker Democritus | 德谟克利特
dates: c. 460–c. 370 BC
works: Many works, lost; known through later writers such as Aristotle
A Greek philosopher who, with his teacher Leucippus, held that the world is made of
countless indivisible **atoms** moving in the void. The course takes this "naive atomism"
as a high point of ancient materialism: a guess, not yet science.
:::

The course's verdict:

- **Right in principle**: it explains the world from the world itself, not from gods.
- **But only a guess**: it had no scientific evidence.
- **Too simple**: it reduces the origin of everything to one or a few concrete forms of
  matter, simplifying a complex question.

## Modern metaphysical materialism: matter = atoms

In the 17th and 18th centuries, with mechanics and chemistry booming, materialists
built on science. {{term Modern metaphysical materialism | 近代形而上学唯物主义}}
identified matter with the **atom** (物质 = 原子) and mixed up the *philosophical*
concept of matter with the *natural-science* concept. ("Metaphysical" is the course's
word for seeing things as isolated and unchanging; Lesson 3.1 defines it.)

| Thinker | Lived | What the slide quotes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thomas Hobbes 霍布斯 | 1588–1679 | Bodies exist independently of our thoughts, take up space and can be known; the human heart is like a clock's spring |
| Denis Diderot 狄德罗 | 1713–1784 | Matter is the only substance; there is no supernatural reason or God; nature is made of countless material elements |
| Baron d'Holbach 霍尔巴赫 | 1723–1789 | Matter and motion are eternal and objective; motion is matter's way of existing |
| John Dalton 道尔顿 | 1766–1844 | Elements are made of atoms that can't be divided, created or destroyed |

The picture was a ladder: matter » elements » atoms, with the atom at the bottom.
Today's physics goes further: atom (about $10^{-10}$ m) → nucleus ($10^{-14}$ m) →
proton ($10^{-15}$ m) → quarks and electrons (at most $10^{-18}$ m) → and then? Nobody
knows yet; the slide ends with question marks.

The course's verdict:

- **Its advance**: it tried to use and generalise the latest science, which took it
  beyond ancient guesswork.
- **Its flaw**: it became one-sided. Its "world" built of atoms left out human society
  and human practice. So it was an **abstract, incomplete** materialism, and once it
  entered social history it fell into idealism (explaining history by ideas, great men,
  reason).

::: callout pitfall Matter as a philosophical category is not a physical substance
The mistake of the old materialists was to treat "matter" as one kind of stuff —
water, fire, atoms. Physical substances are what *science* studies, and science keeps
finding new levels. The *philosophical* category of matter says only what all of them
have in common. So no physical discovery can "refute materialism" — it only deepens
our knowledge of matter.
:::

## The crisis in physics, and Lenin's answer

Around 1900, physics found that the atom was not the end. X-rays (1895), radioactivity
(1896) and the electron (1897) showed atoms have parts and can change; mass, once
thought fixed, turned out to vary with speed. Some philosophers concluded that "matter
has disappeared" — and with it materialism.

Lenin answered in *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism*: "matter is disappearing" only
means that **the limit of our knowledge of matter** is disappearing and our knowledge
is going deeper. Properties once thought absolute and unchanging (impenetrability,
inertia, mass) turn out to be relative, belonging only to some states of matter.

> "'物质在消失'这句话的意思是说：至今我们认识物质所达到的那个界限正在消失，我们的知识正在深化……"
> ——列宁：《唯物主义和经验批判主义》

::: timeline The idea of matter
c. 5th century BC | Democritus: the world is atoms and void; China's Five Phases take shape | 原子论；五行说
1588–1789 | Hobbes, Diderot, d'Holbach: modern (mechanical) materialism | 近代形而上学唯物主义
1803–08 | Dalton's atomic theory: indivisible atoms | 道尔顿原子论
1873–83 | Engels writes *Dialectics of Nature*: matter as an abstraction from all things | 《自然辩证法》
1895–97 | X-rays, radioactivity and the electron: the atom has parts | 物理学革命
1905 | Einstein's special relativity: mass, length and time depend on motion | 狭义相对论
1908 | Lenin writes *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* (published 1909): matter = objective reality | 《唯物主义和经验批判主义》
1964 | Quarks proposed inside protons and neutrons | 夸克模型
:::

## Dialectical materialism: matter = objective reality

Engels had already pointed the way. "Matter" is not one more thing beside other things:
it is a **concept abstracted from the sum of all things**, a short word that gathers
many different things by what they have in common.

> "物、物质无非是各种物的总和，而这个概念就是从这一总和中抽象出来的……"
> ——恩格斯：《自然辩证法》

Like "fruit": you can eat an apple or a pear, but never "fruit as such". Still, "fruit"
names something real that apples and pears share. What do *all* things share, from
stars to atoms to societies? Lenin's answer: they exist **outside and independently of
our consciousness**.

::: define Matter | 物质
The philosophical category for **objective reality**: reality that people perceive
through their senses, that exists independently of our sensations, and that our
sensations copy, photograph and reflect (Lenin).

> "物质是标志客观实在的哲学范畴，这种客观实在是人通过感觉感知的，它不依赖于我们的感觉而存在，为我们的感觉所复写、摄影、反映。"
> ——列宁：《唯物主义和经验批判主义》

The textbook's short form: 物质是不依赖于人的意识，并能为人的意识所反映的客观实在。
:::

The slides unpack it in four points:

1. Matter is a **philosophical category** (not a physical one).
2. Matter's **only** property (as a philosophical category) is **objective reality**
   ({{term objective reality | 客观实在性}}): being there whether or not anyone thinks
   about it.
3. Matter exists outside human consciousness and can be reflected by it: it is
   **knowable**.
4. The concept of matter is **inseparable from practice**: Lenin says the conclusion
   that things exist outside us "is drawn by all people in living human practice".

::: callout key The one idea to remember
Matter = **objective reality** (客观实在). Not water, not atoms, not "things you can
touch": anything that exists independently of consciousness — including society's
production and relations — is matter in the philosophical sense.
:::

### Why Lenin's definition matters

The slides list the **theoretical significance of the Marxist category of matter**
({{zh 马克思主义物质范畴的理论意义}}):

1. It holds to **materialist monism** (matter is the one origin; Lesson 2.4) and draws
   a line against idealism and dualism.
2. It holds to the **active theory of reflection** (能动的反映论) and to
   **knowability**, against agnosticism: matter can be reflected by consciousness.
3. It unites **materialism and dialectics**, overcoming the flaws of metaphysical
   materialism: matter is no longer a fixed list of properties that new physics could
   "abolish".
4. It unites the **materialist view of nature and the materialist view of history**,
   laying the basis for a *thorough* materialism: society is objective reality too.

### How Marxist materialism sees the world

The slides add two points on how Marxist materialism grasps the world, which separate
it from the old materialism:

- **From practice**: the material world that matters to us is the real world that our
  practice is aimed at and has drawn in. The world not yet within people's view and
  practice is only an abstract "world in itself" (自在世界).
- **From the subject**: the material world is understood as the world of objects human
  practice has created, rich with people's essential powers, everywhere marked by
  human will.

This does not make the world depend on our will (that would be idealism); it means
Marxism studies matter as it is *in relation to human activity*, not only as nature
apart from people. (Practice itself is the subject of Unit 4.)

| Form | Matter is… | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient naive 古代朴素 | Concrete forms (five phases, atoms, four elements) | Explains the world from itself | Guesswork; oversimplified |
| Modern metaphysical 近代形而上学 | The atom (a scientific concept taken as philosophical) | Uses science | One-sided, mechanical; leaves out society and practice; idealist about history |
| Dialectical 辩证唯物主义 | Objective reality (a philosophical abstraction) | Monist, dialectical, covers nature and society | — (the course's position) |

::: example The in-class true-or-false questions on matter
Are these right? (1) Matter is concrete forms of matter. (2) Matter is the same as
concrete things. (3) The category of matter does not include human society.
== Statements 1 and 2 ==
Both identify matter with particular things or forms — the old materialist mistake.
Matter in philosophy is the abstraction of what *all* things share: objective reality.
A table is material, but "matter" is not "tables and chairs". **Both wrong.**
== Statement 3 ==
Society's material production and economic relations exist independently of what any
person thinks of them: they are objective reality too. Leaving society out was exactly
the old materialists' flaw. **Wrong.**
:::

## Motion: matter's way of existing

### Matter is always moving

Tang poet-thinker Liu Zongyuan wrote, of mountains and rivers: they move and rest by
themselves, stand and flow by themselves, fight and dry up, collapse and crumble by
themselves — "what has that to do with me?" ({{zh 自动自休，自峙自流，是恶乎与我谋？}}).
Nature moves on its own, not for us. Against it the slides set the Chan Buddhist story
from the *Platform Sutra* (《六祖坛经》): two monks argued whether the wind moved or the
flag moved; Huineng said: "Neither the wind nor the flag — it is your mind that
moves" ({{zh 不是风动，不是幡动，仁者心动}}). That puts motion in the mind: idealism.

::: define Motion | 运动
The philosophical category for **all changes and processes** of all things and
phenomena. Motion is the fundamental attribute and way of existing of matter.

> 运动是标志一切事物和现象的变化及其过程的哲学范畴。运动是物质的根本属性和存在方式。
:::

"Motion" here is far wider than moving from place to place: a chemical reaction, a
plant growing, an idea changing, a society developing — all are motion.

::: law Matter and motion are inseparable | 物质与运动不可分割
Matter is matter in motion: all matter moves (物质是运动的物质). Motion is the motion
of matter: matter is its carrier (运动是物质的运动，物质是运动的载体).
:::

::: callout pitfall Two ways to split matter from motion
- **Motion without matter** (motion with no material carrier — "it's your mind that
  moves") leads to **idealism**.
- **Matter without motion** (matter fixed and unchanging, moved only from outside)
  leads to **metaphysics**.
:::

### Rest

::: define Rest | 静止
A stable state of matter's motion under given conditions: either its **relative
position in space** or its **basic nature** has not changed for the time being. Rest
is a special state of motion.

> 静止是物质运动在一定条件下的稳定状态，包括空间相对位置和事物根本性质暂时未变这两种运动的特殊状态。
:::

A sleeping passenger on a train is at rest relative to the train, but moving relative
to the ground; a river is still "the Yellow River" this year and last, though every
drop of water has changed. Both are rest — relative to something, for a while.

The Ming–Qing thinker Wang Fuzhi put it: {{zh 静者静动，非不动也}} — rest is motion at
rest, not the absence of motion. The paradox {{zh 飞鸟之影，未尝动也}} ("the shadow of a
flying bird never moves") points the other way: at each instant the shadow is in one
place, yet it moves.

::: callout note Who said "the flying bird's shadow never moves"
The slide credits {{zh 飞鸟之影，未尝动也}} to Zhuangzi. It comes from the 《庄子·天下》
chapter, but there it is one of the paradoxes of the logicians (辩者) around Hui Shi
(惠施), which Zhuangzi's book reports and criticises — not Zhuangzi's own view.
:::

::: thinker Wang Fuzhi | 王夫之
dates: 1619–1692
works: 《思问录》; 《读四书大全说》; 《周易外传》
A thinker at the end of the Ming and start of the Qing, called by the course a peak of
ancient Chinese materialism and dialectics. He held that 气 (material force) is the
basis of 理 (principle) — {{zh 气者，理之依也}} — against Zhu Xi's 理 first; and that
rest is a form of motion, {{zh 静者静动，非不动也}}.
:::

::: law Motion is absolute, rest is relative | 运动是绝对的，静止是相对的
Motion is **absolute**: unconditional, eternal, everywhere. Rest is **relative**: it
holds only under certain conditions, in a certain respect, for a time. The two are
distinct, but they depend on each other, interpenetrate and contain each other: there
is rest in motion, motion in rest.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Hold to the unity of absolute motion and relative
rest:

- Only by recognising **relative rest** can we hold to the view that the material world
  is in eternal motion, understand the **variety** of things (without rest nothing would
  stay itself long enough to be a distinct thing), and see how it is **possible to know
  and change** the world.
- **Exaggerating rest** leads to **metaphysics** — the "nothing changes" view (不变论).
  The story *carving the boat to find the sword* (刻舟求剑, from 《吕氏春秋》): a man drops
  his sword overboard, marks the boat's side, and looks for it there when the boat
  stops — as if the boat had not moved.
- **Exaggerating motion** leads to **idealism** and **sophistry** (诡辩论), denying that
  anything is stable enough to be known; the slides set the wind-and-flag story here.

::: thinker Heraclitus | 赫拉克利特
dates: c. 540–c. 480 BC
works: *On Nature* (only fragments survive)
A Greek philosopher of Ephesus who held that everything flows and the world is an
"ever-living fire". "You cannot step into the same river twice" is his. Lenin called him
one of the founders of dialectics.
:::

::: example Think it through: Heraclitus and Cratylus
The teacher's discussion question (week 2): using the principle of motion and rest,
evaluate Heraclitus, "a person cannot step into the same river twice", and Cratylus,
"a person cannot step into the same river even once".
== What each one says ==
Heraclitus: the river's water keeps flowing, so the second time you step in, it is
other water — the river is always changing. Cratylus, his follower, went further: the
river changes even while you step in, so there is no "same river" at all. Aristotle
reports that Cratylus ended up refusing to say anything, and only moved his finger.
== Heraclitus: the dialectical view ==
He sees that motion is absolute: the river is never still. Yet "the same river" still
makes sense in his sentence: the river keeps its relative stability (it is still the
same river, in the same bed). So he recognises motion and, implicitly, relative rest —
a naive dialectical view, which the course values.
== Cratylus: absolutising motion ==
Cratylus denies relative rest completely. If nothing is ever "the same" even for a
moment, there are no distinct things, nothing can be named or known, and every
statement is as good as any other. This is **relativism** and leads to **sophistry**
and agnosticism.
== Model answer ==
运动是绝对的，静止是相对的，二者相互依赖、相互渗透、相互包含。赫拉克利特"人不能两次踏进同一条河流"，看到了河水永恒流动、运动的绝对性，同时也承认了河流的相对稳定性，是朴素辩证法的观点，基本正确。克拉底鲁"人连一次也不能踏进同一条河流"，只承认绝对运动，否认相对静止，把运动绝对化，割裂了运动和静止的辩证统一，陷入了相对主义和诡辩论。因此，我们既要承认运动的绝对性，又要承认静止的相对性；只有承认相对静止，才能理解事物的多样性，才能认识和改造世界。
:::

## Time and space: the forms in which matter moves

::: define Space | 空间
The **extension and spread** (广延性和伸张性) of matter's motion: things exist with a
tendency to extend without limit (广延性) and stretch out in every direction — up and
down, front and back, left and right (伸张性). Space is **three-dimensional**.
:::

::: define Time | 时间
The **duration and sequence** (持续性和顺序性) of matter's motion: every motion takes
a longer or shorter process (持续性), and different processes come one after another
in an order (顺序性). Time is **one-dimensional** (it runs one way).
:::

Time and space are the **forms in which moving matter exists**
({{zh 时间和空间是物质运动的存在形式}}), and they cannot be separated from it:

- On one side, time and space **can't exist apart from matter in motion**: there is no
  empty "box" of space or "river" of time with nothing in it.
- On the other, matter's motion **can't exist apart from time and space**: every
  process happens somewhere and lasts some while.

And the properties of time and space **change with the nature of matter's motion**.
Albert Einstein's relativity (special, 1905; general, 1915) showed it:

- Time and space change with **speed**: near the speed of light, lengths shrink and
  time slows down.
- They change with the **distribution of mass**: the more and the denser the mass, the
  stronger gravity and the greater the "curvature" of space, and time runs slower.

Geometry tells the same story. The slides' three surfaces:

| Geometry | Surface | Angles of a triangle add up to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Euclidean 欧氏几何 | Flat plane | Exactly 180° |
| Lobachevskian 罗氏几何 | Saddle (negative curvature) | Less than 180° |
| Riemannian 黎氏几何 | Sphere (positive curvature) | More than 180° |

Which one describes real space depends on matter: general relativity uses Riemann's
geometry for space curved by mass.

::: callout pitfall Absolute and relative, again
Time and space as such are **absolute** (nothing exists outside them), but their
particular properties are **relative** (they vary with motion). Don't read relativity
as "time and space are just in our heads": in the course's account it confirms that
they belong to moving matter.
:::

::: law The unity of matter, motion, time and space | 物质、运动、时间、空间的内在统一
Matter, motion, time and space are inherently unified: matter is always in motion, and
moving matter always exists in time and space.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** In thinking about problems and doing things, act
according to the **concrete time, place and conditions** ({{zh 想问题办事情，要以具体的时间、地点、条件为转移}}).
Marx and Engels said of their own *Manifesto*: {{zh 随时随地都要以当时的历史条件为转移}}
— its principles must be applied everywhere and always according to the historical
conditions of the time. A policy right in one place and decade may be wrong in another.

::: callout note Where the Manifesto quotation comes from
The slide gives the source as 《共产党宣言》. The sentence is from Marx and Engels'
**preface to the 1872 German edition** of the *Manifesto*, written 24 years after it
first appeared (1848), where they explain why some of its proposals had become dated.
:::

## The doubling of the material world

::: define Doubling of the material world | 物质世界的二重化
Through practice, human beings have hugely changed the face of the world and split it
in two: **human society** out of **nature**, and a **subjective world** (people's
ideas and knowledge) out of the **objective world**.
:::

Before humans, there was only nature. Once early humans made tools, used fire and built
shelters, a new part of the world appeared: society, with its own laws. And once people
began to think about the world, there was an "inner" world of ideas beside the outer
one. The slides' banner: **human practice is the key** to how nature and society, the
objective and the subjective world, are both split apart and joined together
({{zh 人的实践活动是自然界与人类社会、客观世界与主观世界相分化和相统一的关键}}).

::: callout pitfall Doubling doesn't mean two origins
Society and the subjective world split *out of* the material world, through material
activity. They are not a second origin beside matter: the world is still one material
world (Lesson 2.4).
:::

? According to Lenin, matter's only property as a philosophical category is:
text: 客观实在性 | objective reality | 客观实在
> Not mass, extension or divisibility — those are physical properties that science keeps revising. The one property all matter shares is existing independently of consciousness.

- [ ] Matter exists only in our sensations
> In *Materialism and Empirio-Criticism* Lenin answers the "crisis in physics": what disappears is an old limit of knowledge and properties once wrongly thought absolute, not matter.
