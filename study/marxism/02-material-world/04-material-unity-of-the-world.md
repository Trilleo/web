Lessons 2.1–2.3 asked what the world is made of and how consciousness fits in. This
lesson draws the conclusion: **the world is one, and what makes it one is matter**.
The textbook calls this principle the most basic, core view of dialectical materialism,
the cornerstone of Marxism, and it is a standard long-answer question ("how do you
understand that the world's unity lies in its materiality?"). The lesson gives the
competing answers, the principle in three parts, its significance, the model answer,
and a review of the whole section.

## Is the world one? Four answers

Look around: stars, rivers, plants, cities, laws, poems, feelings. Is there something
that *all* of these are, so that the world is one world — or is it two worlds, or many?
Philosophies give four answers.

::: define Monism | 一元论
The view that the world has **one** origin. **Materialist monism** (唯物主义一元论) says
the one origin is matter; **idealist monism** (唯心主义一元论) says it is spirit —
subjective spirit or objective spirit (Lesson 2.1).
:::

::: define Dualism | 二元论
The view that the world has **two** independent origins, matter and spirit, neither
derived from the other.
:::

::: define Pluralism | 多元论
The view that the world has **many** origins or basic parts. The slides' example is
the three "worlds" of the philosopher Karl Popper: the physical world, the world of
mental states, and the world of objective knowledge (theories, books, ideas once they
are written down).
:::

| View | What the world is one in | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materialist monism 唯物主义一元论 | Matter | Marxism (and, in their way, the older materialisms) |
| Idealist monism 唯心主义一元论 | Spirit: subjective (my mind) or objective (an Idea, 理) | Berkeley, Wang Yangming; Plato, Zhu Xi, Hegel |
| Dualism 二元论 | Two origins: matter and spirit, side by side | Descartes' two substances, mind and body |
| Pluralism 多元论 | Many: e.g. the physical world, the mental world and the world of objective knowledge | Popper's "three worlds" |

::: callout pitfall Why dualism and pluralism end in idealism
In the course's account, dualism and pluralism try to stand between materialism and
idealism, but they can't stay there. Once spirit is given a place *beside* matter as
an equal origin, it no longer depends on matter — which is the core of idealism. So
there are really only two camps (Lesson 2.1), and the course holds that dualism and
pluralism lean, in the end, towards idealism.
:::

## The principle of the material unity of the world

Engels, in *Anti-Dühring* (1878), answered Dühring's claim that the world is one because
we can *think* it as one: the unity of the world does not lie in its being, nor in
our thought of it, but in its **materiality** — {{zh 世界的真正的统一性在于它的物质性}} —
and that is proved not by a few clever phrases, but by the long and slow development of
philosophy and natural science.

::: law The principle of the material unity of the world | 世界的物质统一性原理
1. **The world's real unity lies in its materiality** (世界的真正统一性在于它的物质性).
2. **The world is unified in matter** (世界统一于物质):
   - **nature** is material;
   - **human society** is, in essence, a material system formed on the basis of
     productive practice;
   - **human consciousness** is unified in matter.
3. The material unity of the world is a **unity of diversity**, and a unity **in motion,
   change and development** (世界的物质统一性是多样性的统一，是在运动变化、发展中的统一).
:::

Take the three parts of point 2 one by one — this is also the skeleton of the model
answer below.

**Nature is material.** Nature existed long before humans and human society. The
Earth is about 4.6 billion years old; humans a few million years at most. It does not
depend on human consciousness: it is objective reality.

**Society is material.** This is what the old materialists missed. Society is part of
the material world: it has a natural basis (geography, population) and is built on
the material activity of producing the means of life. People's productive practice,
their tools and the relations they enter in production exist and work whatever anyone
thinks of them. In practice, people come to know the world and discover its laws.

**Consciousness is unified in matter.** By its **origin**, consciousness is a product of
the material world's long development (nature, then labour and language). By its
**essence**, it is a function of the brain and a subjective image of the objective
world. By its **role**, its active power works only on the premise of objective laws
(Lesson 2.3). Consciousness is the one thing that seems "not material" — and it too
turns out to be unified in matter.

::: callout key Unity in diversity, not sameness
The material unity of the world does **not** say everything is the same. Stars, cells,
societies and ideas are very different, with their own laws. What they share is
*materiality*: they exist objectively, or (consciousness) as a property and image of
what does. The world is one *because* the many are all material, and it stays one while
everything in it moves and develops.
:::

::: callout pitfall Unity is not "everything is atoms"
"The world is unified in matter" uses Lenin's philosophical category (objective
reality, Lesson 2.2), not a physical substance. "The world is unified in atoms" or "in
energy" is the old metaphysical materialism.
:::

## Why the principle matters

::: callout summary Its significance
- **Theoretical significance:** it is the **most basic, most central view of
  dialectical materialism**, the **cornerstone of Marxism**
  ({{zh 它是辩证唯物主义最基本、最核心的观点，是马克思主义的基石}}).
- **Practical significance:** **starting from reality in everything** is the principle
  of the material unity of the world brought to life in real life and work
  ({{zh 一切从实际出发，是世界的物质统一性原理在现实生活中和实际工作中的生动体现}}).
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Since the world — society included — is material and
has its own laws, start from reality in everything, seek truth from facts, and
**combine theory with practice**: neither copy books (dogmatism) nor trust only
narrow experience.

::: example Starting from reality: China's basic national condition
In the textbook's account, the most important "reality" for China is its basic national
condition: it is, and will long remain, in the **primary stage of socialism**
(社会主义初级阶段). How does the principle of the material unity of the world apply?
== The principle ==
The world, society included, is material; its state is objective and does not change
because we wish it would. So policies must start from society as it actually is.
== Applying it ==
The course holds that China's line and policies were drawn up from this condition
(its level of development, its problems), rather than from a model copied from books or
from other countries.
== Conclusion ==
一切从实际出发 is the practical side of the material unity of the world: grasp the
basic reality first, then decide.
:::

## The section's subjective question

::: example How do you understand that the unity of the world lies in its materiality? (7 marks)
The subjective question at the end of the section (the slides give its marking scheme).
== Thesis ==
Dialectical materialism holds that matter is the origin of the world and the world is
unified in matter. Then show it for nature, society and consciousness.
== Nature is material (2 marks) ==
Nature existed before humans and society, independently of human consciousness: it is
objective reality.
== Society is material (2 marks) ==
Human society is part of the material world; its natural basis and its activity of
producing the means of life are material. In practice, people know the world and
discover its laws.
== Consciousness is unified in matter (2 marks) ==
In origin, consciousness is a product of the material world's long development; in
essence, a function of the brain and a subjective image of the objective world; in its
role, the active power of consciousness rests on objective laws.
== Significance (1 mark) ==
The principle is the most basic, core view of dialectical materialism and the
cornerstone of Marxism, and helps us form a correct worldview; so we should start from
reality in everything and seek truth from facts.
== Model answer ==
马克思主义辩证唯物主义认为，物质是世界的本原，世界统一于物质。（1）自然界是物质的。自然界先于人和人类社会而存在，不依赖于人的意识，具有客观实在性。（2）人类社会也是物质的。人类社会是物质世界的一部分，人类社会的自然基础和获取物质生活资料的活动是物质的；人类在实践活动中认识世界，发现规律。（3）意识统一于物质。从意识的起源看，意识是物质世界长期发展的产物；从意识的本质看，意识是人脑的机能，是客观世界的主观映像；从意识的作用看，意识能动性的发挥以客观规律为前提。（4）意义：世界的物质统一性原理是辩证唯物主义最基本、最核心的观点，是马克思主义的基石，有利于我们树立正确的世界观。因此，我们应当做到一切从实际出发，实事求是。
:::

## Looking back: Marxist materialism

The slides close the section with a map. In words:

**How earlier philosophy saw the world**

- How worldviews developed: myth → religion → philosophy (Lesson 2.1).
- The philosophical worldview splits into **materialism** (ancient naive, modern
  metaphysical, dialectical) and **idealism** (subjective, objective) (Lessons 2.1, 2.2).
- The basic question of philosophy: thinking and being (Lesson 2.1).

**How Marxist materialism sees the world**

- The Marxist views of **matter**, **motion** and **time and space** (Lesson 2.2).
- The Marxist view of **consciousness** (Lesson 2.3).
- The dialectical relation of matter and consciousness: **matter determines
  consciousness**; **consciousness acts back on matter** (Lesson 2.3).
- The **material unity of the world**: the world is unified in matter; its unity is a
  unity of diversity (this lesson).

::: callout exam How this is examined
- The long question above, almost word for word: learn the four parts and their marks.
- Multiple choice: "the cornerstone of Marxism is…" → the principle of the material
  unity of the world; "its practical expression is…" → 一切从实际出发.
- Sorting pairs of quotations into materialism vs idealism (the first quiz question
  below); spotting views that split motion from rest (the third).
- Traps: "unity means sameness"; "the world is unified in atoms"; "dualism is
  neutral".
:::
