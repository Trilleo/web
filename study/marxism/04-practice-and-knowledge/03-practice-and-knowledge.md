Lessons 4.1 and 4.2 said what practice is. This lesson shows what it does for
knowledge: the course's central claim that **practice is the basis of knowledge** and
**plays the decisive role in it**, in four ways — as its source, its driving force, its
purpose and the sole test of its truth. These four are among the most examined points
of the whole course: you'll meet them in multiple choice, in 辨析题 and as the
"principle" step of nearly every 材料分析题 on knowledge. With them we can finally answer
the chapter's first question, whether "knowing is doing". The lesson ends at the start
of the next question: what knowing *is*.

## Practice is the basis of knowledge

> 辩证唯物主义认为，实践是认识的基础，实践在认识活动中起着决定性的作用。

Mao opens *On Practice* (1937) with the line the slide quotes:

> 实践的观点是辩证唯物论的认识论之第一的和基本的观点。——毛泽东《实践论》

"The standpoint of practice is the first and basic standpoint of the dialectical-materialist
theory of knowledge" — repeating Lenin (Lesson 4.1). Here {{term knowledge | 认识}} means
everything people come to know about the world, from a child's first impressions to
scientific theories. Practice decides it in four ways:

| Role of practice | Chinese | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The source of knowledge | 认识的来源 | Knowledge comes out of practice. |
| The driving force of its development | 认识发展的动力 | Practice pushes knowledge forward. |
| The purpose of knowledge | 认识的目的 | Knowledge is for practice. |
| The sole criterion of its truth | 检验认识真理性的唯一标准 | Practice tests whether knowledge is true. |

## Practice is the source of knowledge

::: law Practice is the source of knowledge | 实践是认识的来源
The content of knowledge arises and develops on the basis of practical activity. Only by
actually transforming and changing an object in practice can people grasp its
properties, essence and laws accurately and form correct knowledge. Knowledge cut off
from practice cannot arise; **all genuine knowledge originates in direct experience**.

> 认识的内容是在实践活动的基础上产生和发展的……离开实践的认识是不可能产生的，一切真知都是从直接经验发源的。
:::

Mao's famous example:

> 你要有知识，你就得参加变革现实的实践。你要知道梨子的滋味，你就得变革梨子，亲口吃一吃。——毛泽东《实践论》

You can read every description of a pear; you only know its taste when you **change**
the pear — bite it. Knowing comes from acting on the object, not from looking at it.

The slide applies the same idea to politics, quoting Xi Jinping (speech at the 11th
collective study session of the 18th Politburo, 3 December 2013): the Party's theories,
line and policies are correct "because they are all based on the social existence of our
country in the present era". In the course's account, policy is right when it comes from
the real conditions of practice.

### Direct and indirect experience

An obvious objection: I know the Earth goes round the Sun, and I never checked it. Most
of what anyone knows comes from books and teachers. Does that contradict "practice is the
source"?

::: define Direct experience | 直接经验
Perceptual experience gained **through one's own practice** (经过亲身实践获得的感性经验).
:::

::: define Indirect experience | 间接经验
**Other people's** perceptual experience, learnt from books and teaching
(从书本和传授中学习得来的别人的感性经验).
:::

::: callout key Source and stream
Direct and indirect experience are related as **source and stream** (源与流). What I learn
indirectly was someone else's direct experience: "在我为间接经验者，在人则仍为直接经验"
(Mao, *On Practice*). Follow any piece of book knowledge back far enough and you reach
somebody's practice. So:

- for **an individual**, most knowledge comes from indirect experience — and it should:
  nobody can rediscover everything;
- for **knowledge as such**, practice is the only source: all genuine knowledge
  originates in direct experience.
:::

::: callout pitfall "Practice is the source" doesn't mean "learn everything first-hand"
Saying "all of a person's knowledge comes from their own direct experience" is **wrong**;
saying "book knowledge has nothing to do with practice" is wrong too. The correct
statement has two layers: practice is the (ultimate) source of knowledge; an individual's
knowledge comes from both direct and indirect experience, and indirect experience is
itself, at its origin, others' direct experience.
:::

::: example A marked answer: are direct experience and practice the same thing for the individual?
The slide shown in class after this point is a model answer to a 辨析题 whose statement
is not preserved (its question slide is missing). From the answer, the statement was
of the kind "a person's knowledge all comes from their own direct experience". The
answer shows how such questions are marked (8 points).

== Verdict (1 point) ==
错误。The statement is wrong.

== Principle (2 points) ==
State the decisive role of practice in knowledge, especially that practice is the source
of knowledge: practice is the source of knowledge, the driving force of its development,
its purpose, and the criterion of its truth.

== Analysis (3 points) ==
Practice is the source of knowledge. But an individual's knowledge has two sources,
direct and indirect experience: an individual's knowledge does **not** all come from
direct experience; most of it comes from indirect experience, from books and teaching.

== Conclusion (2 points) ==
So practice is the source of knowledge, and knowledge cut off from practice cannot
arise; all genuine knowledge originates in direct experience. As to the origin of
knowledge, no knowledge can be separated from direct experience.

> 错误（1分）。原理：实践对认识的决定性作用，尤其是"实践是认识的来源"（2分）。个人知识的获得有直接经验和间接经验两部分来源，个人的知识并不都来源于直接经验，多数知识来自间接经验，是从书本和传授中得来的（3分）。总结：实践是认识的来源，离开实践的认识是不可能产生的，一切真知都是从直接经验发源的；就知识的本源来说，任何知识都不能离开直接经验（2分）。
:::

::: callout exam The shape of a 辨析题 answer
Verdict (right / wrong / partly right) → the principle → apply it to the statement →
conclusion. The verdict alone earns only one point; the principle and analysis carry the
marks.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Go into practice to get first-hand knowledge (investigate
and study, 调查研究); and study books and others' experience as well, since they are
condensed practice — while remembering that they too must be tested in practice.

## Practice is the driving force of knowledge

::: law Practice is the driving force of the development of knowledge | 实践是认识发展的动力
Practice pushes knowledge to arise and develop in three ways:
1. the **needs** of practice drive knowledge to arise and develop (实践的需要推动认识的产生和发展);
2. practice provides the **means and conditions** for knowledge to develop (instruments,
   experimental equipment, data);
3. practice trains and raises people's **ability to know** (认识能力).
:::

The slide's pictures: a drone taking a class photo, and FAST, China's 500-metre radio
telescope ("China's Sky Eye") — instruments that practice built and that let people see
what they couldn't before. Engels put the first point sharply, in a letter to W. Borgius
of 25 January 1894:

> 社会一旦有技术上的需要，这种需要就会比十所大学更能把科学推向前进。

"If society has a technical need, that helps science forward more than ten universities."

The slide's case is the Harbin Institute of Technology's sense of national mission. Its
scientists put it in their own words: "I have no specialty; the country's needs are my
specialty" (Academician Qin Yukun 秦裕琨); "the country's needs are our first choice"
(Academician Tan Jiubin 谭久彬). Professor Yuan Shijian (苑世剑) tells materials students
that publishing papers isn't enough: "our industrial users want **parts, not
materials**", so research must join material, process and equipment. His team, working
with the China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology, developed a fluid-pressure forming
technique that made, for the first time in the world, a five-way joint in **one piece**
instead of several welded pieces, solving the low reliability of welded joints. It has
been in steady production since 2016 and flown on the Long March 7 and 7A rockets,
including the launch of the Tianzhou-2 cargo craft on 29 May 2021. A real need (a
reliable rocket part) drove new knowledge (a new forming technique).

The class's single-choice question on the BeiDou navigation system makes the same point;
it is in the quiz below.

**Methodological requirement.** Let the real needs of practice — the country's, society's,
production's — set the questions research tries to answer; build the means that practice
needs; and develop your own abilities by taking part in practice.

## Practice is the purpose of knowledge

::: law Practice is the purpose of knowledge | 实践是认识的目的
People gain knowledge through practice not out of curiosity for novelties (猎奇) or as a
refined pastime (雅兴), not for knowledge's own sake: its **final purpose is to serve
practice, guide practice, and meet people's needs in life and production**.

> 人们通过实践获得认识……其最终目的是为实践服务，指导实践，以满足人们的生活和生产需要。
:::

The slide's case, under the heading "strengthen strategic thinking", is radar scientist
**Liu Yongtan** (刘永坦), honoured as a "model of the times" for building the "Great Wall of
coastal defence". China's sea areas — the "three million square kilometres of blue
territory" — include exclusive economic zones reaching 200 nautical miles (about 370 km)
from the coast under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. Conventional radar can't see
low-flying targets far out at sea, beyond the horizon. Liu said:

> 如何能看得远、如何把我们的海域全都保护起来，这是国家的需求，所以一定要做出这个新体制雷达……核心技术必须靠我们自己的智慧和奋斗拼出来、干出来。

His team spent decades on the theory of electromagnetic waves and radar signals and on the
laws of sea clutter and long-range detection. The slide reads it in two steps:

- **Practice produces knowledge**: a real defence need pushed the team to study theory and
  run experiments, producing scientific knowledge of a new kind of radar.
- **The purpose of knowledge**: the point of that theory was never just papers or
  understanding the formulas; it was to **build the radar** and secure the country's
  coastal defence.

**Methodological requirement.** Link theory with practice: study in order to use, apply
what you learn to real problems, and don't pursue knowledge cut off from practice.

## Practice is the sole criterion of truth

::: law Practice is the sole criterion for testing the truth of knowledge | 实践是检验认识真理性的唯一标准
Whether knowledge is true cannot be proved from knowledge itself, nor answered by the
object of knowledge itself: it can **only be verified in practice**.

> 认识的真理性，不能从认识本身得到证实，也不能从认识对象中得到回答，只有在实践中才能得到验证。

> 判定认识或理论之是否真理，不是依主观上觉得如何而定，而是依客观上社会实践的结果如何而定。真理的标准只能是社会的实践。——毛泽东《实践论》
:::

Why "sole"? An idea can't check itself: however convincing it feels, a feeling is still
subjective. The object can't check it either: the Moon doesn't tell you whether your
theory of the Moon is right. Only practice **connects** the two — it carries the idea out
into the objective world and shows whether the result matches. This is the second thesis
on Feuerbach (Lesson 4.1) and the principle of the 1978 debate. The slide closes with a
clip from a documentary episode on the Four Crossings of the Chishui River (四渡赤水,
1935), a Red Army campaign whose plan the course presents as proved right by its result.
(Why practice can test truth, and in what sense the test is both absolute and relative,
belongs to the lessons on truth.)

**Methodological requirement.** Don't take an idea as true because it is logical, popular
or said by an authority; test it in practice, and correct it when practice shows it wrong
(实事求是).

::: callout pitfall "Sole criterion" vs logic and authority
Logical proof is a powerful **aid** (a theorem follows from axioms), but whether a theory
fits the world is finally decided by practice; and no authority — a famous person, a
classic text — can stand in for it. A multiple-choice option saying "logic is also a
criterion of truth" or "the classics are the criterion" is a trap.
:::

## The answer: is "knowing doing"?

We can now answer the question from Lesson 4.1.

::: callout key The course's answer to 知就是行
Dialectical materialism holds that **knowledge (知) and practice (行) are the two basic
sides of humans' relation with the world**. Knowledge can't be reduced to or equated with
practice, and practice can't be reduced to or equated with knowledge. Wang Yangming's
"knowing is doing, doing is knowing" **exaggerates the role of 知** and **confuses 知 with
行**. Its root is that it doesn't see the deep nature and decisive role of 行 in real
production and social practice; so it **necessarily leads to subjective idealism**.

> 王阳明关于"知就是行，行就是知"的思想，夸大了"知"的作用，也混淆了"知"和"行"……必然导致主观唯心主义。
:::

In plain terms: if a thought already counts as an act, then 行 has been swallowed into
the mind — the world is changed by thinking about it, which is subjective idealism. For
Marxism the order runs the other way: practice comes first and is the basis; knowledge
comes from it, serves it and is tested by it — and then guides practice in turn.

::: example Think it through: can an intelligent robot have knowledge by learning on its own?
Answer from the standpoint of dialectical-materialist epistemology: can intelligent
robots come to have knowledge through autonomous learning (自主学习)? (Week 7 discussion
question 2. Lesson 2.3 discusses consciousness and artificial intelligence; this answer
uses the view of practice.)

== Step 1: the principle ==
Practice is the basis of knowledge: its source, driving force, purpose and sole
criterion of truth. Practice is the active, social, material activity of **human
beings**; knowledge is the human subject's reflection of the objective world on the
basis of practice, and the subject is people living in social relations.

== Step 2: what the robot does ==
A learning machine adjusts its parameters from large amounts of data and can then
recognise images, translate or play chess better than people. That data, though, is
mostly a record of human practice (texts, photos, games people played); its goals and
the yardstick of success are set by people; its "learning" is a computation that has no
purpose of its own, no needs and no social relations. It doesn't take part in social
practice as a subject, and it doesn't understand what its outputs mean.

== Step 3: so what is it? ==
From the course's standpoint, an intelligent robot is a highly developed **medium of
practice** (Lesson 4.2): a tool that extends human ability to know, like the telescope or
the computer, and a product of the objectification of human knowledge. What it "learns" is
information processed from human practice; whether its results are right is still
decided by human practice. It is not a subject of knowledge in the strict sense.

== Step 4: the other side ==
That doesn't mean AI is unimportant for knowledge: as a means provided by practice it
greatly drives knowledge forward (the second role of practice), and people must use it
well, while guarding against its risks.

== Model answer ==
从辩证唯物主义认识论看，智能机器人不能真正"拥有知识"。实践是认识的基础，是认识的来源、动力、目的和检验认识真理性的唯一标准；认识是主体在实践基础上对客体的能动反映，而实践和认识的主体是处在一定社会关系中、从事现实社会实践活动的人。智能机器人的"自主学习"是对人类实践所产生的数据进行的计算和处理，其目标、评价标准都由人设定；它没有自身的需要和目的，不处在社会关系之中，不能作为主体参与社会实践，也不能真正理解其结果的意义，其结论是否正确仍要由人的实践来检验。因此，智能机器人本质上是人类实践的中介和认识的工具，是人的本质力量对象化的产物，而不是认识的主体。同时，人工智能作为实践提供的新手段，能够极大地推动人类认识的发展，我们应当发挥其积极作用，并防范其风险。
:::

## The nature of knowledge: is knowing like a mirror?

The chapter's second question begins here: **is knowing the world a mirror-like reflection
of things as they are?** When people talk about how the mind knows things, they easily
think of a mirror, and philosophers in China and the West have described the mind's
ability to know as a mirror reflecting things:

| Thinker | Saying | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zhuangzi 庄子 (c. 369–286 BC, Warring States) | "The perfect man uses his mind like a mirror: it neither sends off nor welcomes, it responds but does not store." 至人之用心若镜，不将不迎，应而不藏，故能胜物而不伤。 | *Zhuangzi*, Inner Chapters (《应帝王》) |
| Wang Yangming 王阳明 (1472–1529, Ming) | "The sage's mind is like a bright mirror … it reflects everything." 圣人之心如明镜……无物不照。 | 《传习录》 |
| Étienne Bonnot de Condillac 孔狄亚克 (1714–1780, France) | The image a mirror reflects represents its object exactly, so we shouldn't think the images in our minds fail to match things. | *Treatise on Systems* 《体系论》 (1749) |
| Richard Rorty 罗蒂 (1931–2007, USA) | "The picture which holds traditional philosophy captive is that of the mind as a great mirror, containing various representations." | *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* 《哲学和自然之镜》 (1979) |

Note that Rorty quotes the picture in order to **reject** it: he argued that philosophy
should give up the idea of the mind as a mirror.

The slide asks: **is the mind really a mirror reflecting all things? Is the metaphor
right — where is it right, and where wrong?** Keep the question open: the course's answer
— that knowing is a reflection, but an active, creative one on the basis of practice —
comes in the next lessons.

## Idealism's answer: a-priorism

Before giving the materialist answer, the course sets out idealism's. Its line of
knowledge runs **from thought and sensation to things** (从思想和感觉到物) — the reverse of
materialism's line from things to sensation and thought.

::: define A-priorism | 先验论
The idealist theory of knowledge: it holds that knowledge **does not depend on external
things**; human knowledge comes **before sensory experience** or lies beyond experience,
and the material world is a product of thought and spirit. All idealist epistemology is
a-priorism.

> 唯心主义认识论都是先验论，因为它认为认识不依赖于外部事物，人的认识都是先于感觉经验或者是超越于经验范围之外的。
:::

It has two forms, matching the two forms of idealism (Lesson 2.1):

| Form | What it holds | Examples on the slides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subjective idealist epistemology 主观唯心主义认识论 | Knowledge **arises by itself from the subject**: people are born knowing (生而知之). | **Mencius** 孟子 (c. 372–289 BC): "What people can do without learning is their innate ability (良能); what they know without thinking is their innate knowledge (良知)" (《孟子·尽心上》). **Huineng** 惠能 (638–713), the Sixth Patriarch of Chan Buddhism: two monks argue whether the wind or the banner is moving; Huineng says "it's neither the wind nor the banner — it's your mind that moves" (不是风动，不是幡动，仁者心动, *Platform Sutra* 《六祖坛经》). |
| Objective idealist epistemology 客观唯心主义认识论 | Knowledge is a **revelation from God** or the product of some objective spirit. | **Plato** 柏拉图 (427–347 BC): the theory of **recollection** (灵魂回忆说): the soul knew the eternal Ideas before birth, and learning is remembering them. **René Descartes** 笛卡尔 (1596–1650): the theory of **innate ideas** (天赋观念说): ideas such as God, the self and mathematical truths are implanted in the mind by God. |

::: callout note Where Descartes belongs
The slide puts Descartes under objective idealist epistemology, by his innate ideas (which,
for him, God placed in the mind). Elsewhere the course lists Descartes ("I think, therefore
I am") among the subjective idealists, and histories of philosophy usually call him a
**rationalist** and a **dualist** (mind and matter as two substances). What is safe to say
in an exam: Descartes' **theory of innate ideas** is a form of **a-priorism**. Plato's
recollection is also where his cave story (Lesson 4.1) belongs.
:::

::: callout pitfall A-priorism vs the role of indirect experience
"Most of my knowledge didn't come from my own experience" is **not** a-priorism: it came
from others' experience (indirect experience). A-priorism says knowledge comes from
*no* experience at all — born in the mind, or given by God.
:::

The nature and the process of knowledge — the materialist answer to the mirror question,
and whether knowledge can be reached in one go — continue in the next lessons.

## Summary

::: callout summary The lesson in five lines
- Practice is the basis of knowledge and decides it: its source, driving force, purpose,
  and sole criterion of truth.
- Source: all genuine knowledge originates in direct experience; indirect experience is
  others' direct experience (source and stream).
- Driving force: needs, means and conditions, and trained abilities (HIT; BeiDou).
  Purpose: to serve and guide practice (Liu Yongtan's radar).
- Sole criterion: truth can't be proved by knowledge itself or by the object, only in
  practice. "知就是行" exaggerates 知, confuses 知 and 行 and leads to subjective idealism.
- Idealist epistemology is a-priorism: subjective (born knowing: Mencius, Huineng) and
  objective (God or spirit: Plato's recollection, Descartes' innate ideas).
:::
