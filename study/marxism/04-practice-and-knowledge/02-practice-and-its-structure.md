Lesson 4.1 showed how Marx put practice at the centre of philosophy. This lesson says
precisely **what practice is**: its definition, the three features that set it apart
from an animal's activity, the parts it is made of and the relations between them, and
the forms it takes, old and new. These are the most frequently examined definitions in
the chapter — "is X practice?" is a classic 辨析题 — and the next lesson's claim that
practice decides knowledge only makes sense once you know what counts as practice.

## The spider, the bee and the architect

The section opens with a famous passage from Marx's *Capital*, volume 1:

> 蜘蛛的活动与织工的活动相似，蜜蜂建筑蜂房的本领使人间的许多建筑师感到惭愧。

A spider's web looks like a weaver's work; a bee's honeycomb, with its perfect
hexagons, would shame many human architects. The class then watched a documentary clip
of a spider and of bees at work, with two questions: **Are a spider spinning its web and
a bee building its comb practice? What is the essential difference between an animal's
activity and a human's?** Keep them in mind; the answer comes with the second feature
below.

## The essence of practice

::: define Practice | 实践
Human beings' **active** (能动的), **social** (社会性的), **material activity** (物质活动)
of transforming the world.

> 实践是人类能动地改造世界的社会性的物质活动。
:::

The three marked words carry the definition: *material* (it really changes things —
not thinking about them), *active* (conscious and purposeful), *social* (done by people
in society). The slides add what practice is for human beings:

- the most basic activity by which humans **live and develop** (人类生存和发展的最基本的活动);
- the **essence of human social life** (人类社会生活的本质);
- the **basis on which knowledge arises and develops** (人的认识产生和发展的基础);
- the **basis of the unity of truth and value** (真理与价值统一的基础).

## The three basic features of practice

::: define Basic features of practice | 实践的基本特征
Practice has three basic features: **objective reality** (客观实在性), **conscious
initiative** (自觉能动性) and a **social and historical character** (社会历史性).
:::

### Objective reality

Practice is not something that happens in the head. The slide gives three reasons:

1. Its **elements** — the subject, object and medium of practice (below) — are all
   perceptible, objectively real things: people, materials, tools.
2. Its **level, breadth and depth**, and how it develops, are limited by objective
   conditions and governed by objective laws. You can't build a skyscraper with Stone Age
   tools, however much you want to.
3. It **changes the objective world**: it turns something that existed only as an idea
   in the head into something that really exists, and gives people real results.

The slide's photo shows workers at vices in a skills contest, with a diagram: the
**subject**, the **medium**, the **object** and the **result** of practice are all
{{term objective reality | 客观实在}}.

### Conscious initiative

::: callout key Purpose is the mark of human practice
Human practice is **conscious and purposeful**; purposefulness (目的性) is the main form
its initiative takes. Animal activity is **instinctive and passive adaptation**.
:::

This answers the spider-and-bee question. Marx's passage goes on:

> 最蹩脚的建筑师从一开始就比最灵巧的蜜蜂高明的地方，是他在用蜂蜡建筑蜂房以前，已经在自己的头脑中把它建成了。——马克思《资本论》第一卷

"What distinguishes the worst architect from the best of bees is that the architect
builds the cell in his mind before he builds it in wax." The result of human practice
exists **as an idea, as a goal, in the head of the person at the start**, and the goal
decides what they do. The slide shows it as a chain: a forest → logs → a table and
chairs. The carpenter sees the table before the tree is felled.

So a spider spinning a web and a bee building a comb are **not practice**: however
skilful, they act from instinct, the same way for millions of years, adapting to nature
rather than consciously transforming it. They have no goal in their head beforehand,
and they don't change how they do it by learning.

::: callout pitfall Practice vs any activity
Not every activity is practice. **Animals' activity** isn't (no conscious purpose, no
society). **Purely mental activity** — thinking, imagining, dreaming, planning — isn't
either: it doesn't materially change anything (it is 认识, knowledge, or part of it).
Practice must be *material*, *conscious* and *social* at once.
:::

### Social and historical character

- Practice is **social** from the start. Its subject, a person, always lives in definite
  social relations; nobody's activity is cut off from society.
- Being social makes it **historical**. Its content, nature, scope, level and ways are
  limited by definite social and historical conditions, and change as they change.

The slide shows farming through history: primitive people clearing land with fire and
stone axes; ancient Chinese planting, reaping and winnowing by hand; today's combine
harvesters. It is "the same" activity, growing food, but its tools, organisation and
scale are those of each age.

::: example Think it through: Robinson Crusoe
Daniel Defoe's novel *Robinson Crusoe* (《鲁滨逊漂流记》, 1719) tells of an English sailor
of the 17th century, shipwrecked alone on an island, who survives there for more than
twenty years: he builds a shelter, makes tools, keeps goats, grows grain and keeps a
diary. Was his activity practice? Did it have a social and historical character?
(Week 6 discussion question 1.)

== Step 1: the principle ==
Practice is human beings' active, social, material activity of transforming the world;
its three basic features are objective reality, conscious initiative and a social and
historical character.

== Step 2: is it practice? ==
Yes. His building, farming and herding are **material** activities that really change
his surroundings (objective reality). He plans them with a goal in mind — he decides to
build a fence before he cuts the stakes — and improves his methods from experience
(conscious initiative). That is the architect, not the bee.

== Step 3: is it social and historical, though he is alone? ==
Yes. Being physically alone doesn't take him out of society. The **tools** he saves
from the wreck (guns, axes, a saw) and the ones he makes are products of society. His
**skills** — farming, carpentry, keeping a calendar — and the **language** he thinks
and writes in were learnt in society. Even his **goals** and values are those of an
Englishman of his time. And what he can do is limited by the level of 17th-century
technique: he can't do what a modern castaway with a solar charger could. So his
activity bears the marks of a definite society and age.

== Model answer ==
鲁滨逊的活动是实践，并且具有社会历史性。实践是人类能动地改造世界的社会性的物质活动，具有客观实在性、自觉能动性和社会历史性。第一，鲁滨逊建房、种植、驯养等活动是改造自然的物质活动，有客观的对象、工具和结果，具有客观实在性；他事先确定目的、制订计划并在经验中改进方法，具有自觉能动性。第二，他虽然独自生活在荒岛上，但他使用的工具、掌握的劳动技能、思考所用的语言乃至他的目的和价值观念，都是在社会中获得的，是一定社会关系和历史条件的产物；他活动的内容、方式和水平受17世纪社会历史条件的制约。所以，实践从一开始就是社会性的活动，个人的活动离不开与社会的联系，鲁滨逊的活动同样具有社会历史性。
:::

## The basic structure of practice

Every practice has three basic elements.

::: define Subject of practice | 实践主体
**People with certain abilities as subjects, engaged in real social practice.** Subjects
come at three levels: the **individual** subject (个体主体), the **group** subject
(群体主体) and **humankind** as subject (人类主体).
:::

The slide's example is China's space station. Astronaut Chen Dong working outside the
station is an individual subject; the mission-control team is a group subject; the
station itself, a step in humanity's exploration of space, stands for humankind as
subject.

::: define Object of practice | 实践客体
**What practice and knowledge are directed at.** Objects can be classed as **natural or
artificial** (天然客体 / 人工客体: a pine tree; a table made from a trunk), **natural or
social** (自然客体 / 社会客体: mountains and a lake; a class of students), and **material
or mental** (物质性客体 / 精神性客体: a stone; the textbook's ideas, a mind map).
:::

::: define Medium of practice | 实践中介
**The tools and means of every kind, and the procedures and methods for using and
operating them**, through which subject and object act on each other. Two systems: the
**material tool system** (物质性工具系统), which extends people's limbs, senses and
strength (a locomotive, a computer), and the **language and symbol tool system**
(语言符号工具系统: a dictionary, a programming language such as Python).
:::

> 人的实践活动是以改造客观世界为目的的客观过程，是实践的主体与客体之间的相互作用，这种相互作用必须借助于一定的手段和工具，即实践的中介。三者的有机统一构成实践的基本结构。

The **organic unity of subject, medium and object** is the basic structure of practice.
Take one away and there is no practice: no one to act, nothing to act on, or nothing to
act with.

### Objective things and objects of practice

The slides ask: **is every objective thing an object of practice?** No. Two tests:

1. Has it been **brought within the range** of the subject's practical activity?
2. Is the subject's activity **directed at it**, and does it **interact** with the
   subject?

An objective thing exists whether or not anyone deals with it (that is what objective
means). It becomes an *object of practice* only when people's activity is aimed at it
and works on it. So objects of practice are part of the objective world, but not all of
it, and the range of objects grows as practice grows.

::: example Think it through: the Moon and the Moon-landing project
Are objective things and objects of practice the same? Use the Moon and the
Moon-landing project as an example. (Week 6 discussion question 2.)

== Step 1: the principle ==
Objects of practice are what practice and knowledge are directed at. Objective things
exist independently of people; a thing becomes an object of practice only when it is
brought into the range of the subject's practice, the activity is directed at it, and
it interacts with the subject.

== Step 2: the Moon as an objective thing ==
The Moon existed for billions of years before there were people, and it would exist if
there were none. For most of history people could look at it and tell stories about it
(Chang'e flying to the Moon), but couldn't act on it: it was an objective thing, not an
object of practice — at most an object of observation.

== Step 3: the Moon as an object of practice ==
With modern rockets the Moon was drawn into human practice: the Apollo landings (from
1969), and China's lunar exploration programme (Chang'e 嫦娥工程), whose probes have
landed on the far side and brought back samples (Chang'e 5 in 2020, Chang'e 6 from the far
side in 2024). Now subjects (astronauts, engineering teams, a whole nation) act on the
Moon through a medium (rockets, landers, drills, the control software) and it acts back
(its gravity, dust and temperatures set the conditions). At that point the Moon is an
object of practice.

== Model answer ==
客观事物与实践客体不是一回事。客观事物是不依赖于人的意识而存在的事物；实践客体是实践和认识活动所指向的对象。客观事物只有被纳入主体实践活动的范围之内，为主体的实践活动所指向、并与主体相互作用时，才成为实践客体。月亮作为客观事物早在人类出现以前就存在，在人类长期的历史中只是被观察、被想象的对象，并不是实践客体；随着航天技术的发展，登月工程（如我国探月工程）通过火箭、着陆器、采样装置等实践中介作用于月球，月球才进入人的实践活动范围，成为实践客体。可见，实践客体是客观事物的一部分，而且随着实践的发展，实践客体的范围不断扩大。
:::

### The three relations between subject and object

Subject and object of practice stand in three relations to each other:

| Relation | Chinese | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical relation | 实践关系 | The subject **transforms** the object, and is transformed by it. |
| Cognitive relation | 认识关系 | The subject **knows** the object. |
| Value relation | 价值关系 | The object **meets the subject's needs** (is useful or good for it). |

::: law The practical relation is the most basic | 实践关系是最根本的关系
Of the three relations between subject and object, the **practical relation is the most
fundamental**. The subject and object of practice are essentially the same as the
subject and object of knowledge; the relation between knower and known is first of all a
relation between **transformer and transformed**. The process of knowing an object is
also the process of transforming it; and people know and transform objects in the end
**to meet their own needs**, which makes it a value relation too.
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Don't treat knowing as a spectator's look: we come to know
things by working on them, so to know something well, take part in changing it. And
judge what we know and do by whether it meets real human needs.

## Objectifying the subject and subjectifying the object

The subject, object and medium of practice keep changing and developing, so the
structure of practice changes historically too. The change shows up mainly as a
**two-way movement**:

::: define Objectification of the subject | 主体客体化
Through practice, a person applies their **essential powers** (本质力量: knowledge,
skill, will, strength) to the object and transforms it, so that it changes form,
structure or function as the subject needs, producing an "object-thing" (对象物) that did
not exist in the world before. The subject's powers become embodied in an object.
:::

::: define Subjectification of the object | 客体主体化
The object turns from something existing outside the subject into a **part of the
subject's life or essential powers**: it loses its form as an object and becomes part of
the subject. Food becomes the body; tools and knowledge become abilities; experience
becomes skill and character.
:::

The slide's example: workers building a railway across a high plateau (the photo is of
track-laying at altitude, like the Qinghai–Tibet railway). On one side, by creative work
they turn their will and aims into a railway that didn't exist before — objectification of
the subject. On the other, the work hardens their will and bodies and widens their
experience: the object's forces become part of them — subjectification of the object.

::: callout key Why the two-way movement matters
Humans keep resolving the contradictions of the real world through the two-way movement
of objectifying the subject and subjectifying the object (人类通过它们的双向运动不断解决现实世界的矛盾).
The two happen together, in the same practice, and each prepares the next round: a more
capable subject makes better objects, and better objects make the subject more capable.
:::

::: example Think it through: objectification and subjectification in your own field
Give an example of objectifying the subject and subjectifying the object from your own
field. (Week 7 discussion question 1; asked in class after the railway example.) Here is
one from electronic engineering; any field works the same way.

== Step 1: the principle ==
Practice is the interaction of subject and object through a medium. Its structure
develops as a two-way movement: objectification of the subject (the subject's essential
powers are embodied in a new object) and subjectification of the object (the object
becomes part of the subject's abilities).

== Step 2: the case ==
A student team designs and builds a circuit board for a small robot. They turn their
knowledge of circuits, their design goal and their effort into a working board, a thing
that didn't exist before — through a medium of soldering irons, an oscilloscope,
schematic software and a programming language. That is **objectification of the subject**.

== Step 3: the other direction ==
While debugging, they find why a signal is noisy, learn the habits of careful soldering
and grounding, and come out with skills and judgement they didn't have. What they
learnt from the board, and the tools they've mastered, have become part of their own
abilities. That is **subjectification of the object**. (A programmer is the same: their
idea becomes software; writing and debugging it becomes their skill.)

== Model answer ==
实践的基本结构表现为主体客体化与客体主体化的双向运动。主体客体化是人通过实践使自己的本质力量作用于客体，改造客体，形成世界上原先不存在的"对象物"；客体主体化是客体从客观对象的存在形式转化为主体生命结构或本质力量的因素。以电子信息专业为例：学生团队运用电路知识，借助电烙铁、示波器、设计软件和编程语言等实践中介，把自己的设计目的和能力物化为一块能工作的电路板，这是主体客体化；在设计、焊接和调试中，他们加深了对电路规律的认识，掌握了工具的使用方法，提高了专业能力和工程素养，客体的因素融入主体之中，这是客体主体化。二者在同一实践过程中同时发生、相互促进，人正是在这种双向运动中不断解决现实矛盾、提高自身能力。
:::

## The forms of practice

As people's relations with the world develop, especially as the social division of labour
advances, practice takes more and more specific forms. They grow out of three basic
ones.

::: define Basic forms of practice | 实践的基本形式
1. **Material production practice** (物质生产实践): the most basic practical activity;
   it decides the basic nature and appearance of a society.
2. **Social and political practice** (社会政治实践): handling political relations,
   chiefly political activity. It is bound up with state power: struggle, compromise and
   alliance between classes; revolution, construction and reform of political systems;
   the people's political participation. The slide's example is the annual "Two
   Sessions" (全国两会), which the course calls a vivid practice of whole-process people's
   democracy.
3. **Scientific and cultural practice** (科学文化实践): creating spiritual and cultural
   products, in many forms — chief among them **scientific experiment, artistic creation
   and education**. It rests on material production but is relatively independent; its
   marks are exploration, ideals and ideas (探索性、理想性、思想性).
:::

::: law How the three forms of practice are related | 三种实践形式之间的联系
**Material production practice** is the most basic practical activity and the
**foundation of all social life**: it conditions (制约) social-political and
scientific-cultural practice, and they in turn act back (反作用) on it.
Social-political and scientific-cultural practice **influence each other** (相互影响).
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Put material production — economic development — first
as the foundation, while giving political and scientific-cultural work their due, since
they act back on production; and look at the three as one whole.

## Virtual practice

::: define Virtual practice | 虚拟实践
A **new form of practice** brought about by modern information technology: a two-way
objectifying activity between subject and object, through a **digital medium**, in
**virtual space**. It is active mainly in the online world, and is **interactive, open
and indirect** (交互性、开放性、间接性).

> 虚拟实践是主体和客体之间通过数字化中介系统在虚拟空间进行的双向对象化的活动。
:::

In class, groups tried the *Seeking Dunhuang* (寻境敦煌) virtual tour of the Dunhuang caves
and discussed two questions: what are the subject, object and medium in virtual
practice, and how does it compare with visiting in person? (A class poll then asked
whether such activities help; it had no right answer.)

| Question | Virtual tour of Dunhuang | Visiting the caves in person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | The visitor (and the team that built the model) | The visitor |
| Object | A digital model of the caves and murals, and through it the real caves | The caves and murals themselves |
| Medium | A digital system: scans, software, a headset or phone, the network | The body, eyes, a guide, transport |
| What it offers | Access from anywhere, close-ups, no damage to fragile murals, interaction | Direct, full sensory contact with the real thing |

The course's assessment:

- Virtual practice is a **derivative form** of practice (实践活动的派生形式): it depends
  on real material practice (someone had to scan the real caves; the servers are real).
- It has a **major effect on real practice**.
- It gives people a **wide range of free space** for living and developing and greatly
  increases the autonomy and creativity of their activity.
- It brings **new problems**: risks must be guarded against, and legal supervision and
  moral restraint strengthened.

::: callout pitfall Is virtual practice "not real"?
It happens in virtual space, but it is still **objective** activity: real people use
real devices, and it has real effects (a remote surgery, an online purchase, a digital
twin that changes how a factory runs). That's why the course counts it as practice — but
as a form derived from, and resting on, material practice, not a replacement for it.
:::

## Summary

::: callout summary The lesson in five lines
- Practice: human beings' active, social, material activity of transforming the world.
- Three features: objective reality; conscious initiative (purpose: the architect vs
  the bee); a social and historical character (even Robinson Crusoe's).
- Structure: subject (individual, group, humankind), object (natural/artificial,
  natural/social, material/mental), medium (material tools; language and symbols); the
  practical relation is the most basic of the practical, cognitive and value relations.
- Its structure develops as a two-way movement: objectification of the subject and
  subjectification of the object.
- Forms: material production (the most basic), social-political, scientific-cultural;
  plus virtual practice, a new, derivative form.
:::

::: callout exam How this is examined
- 辨析题: "蜜蜂筑巢也是实践" (false: instinct, no purpose, not social); "思想活动也是实践"
  (false: not material); "个人独自的活动没有社会性" (false: Robinson Crusoe).
- Multiple choice: which is **the most basic** form of practice (material production);
  which relation is **the most basic** (the practical); which is the main form of practice's
  initiative (purposefulness).
- 材料分析题 often give a construction project or an engineer's story: name the subject,
  object and medium, and show both directions of the two-way movement.
:::
