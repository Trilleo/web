Chapter 1 asked what the world is and how it changes. Chapter 2 asks how we come to
**know** it — and the course's answer starts not with the mind but with what people
*do*. This lesson introduces Marxist epistemology and its first and most basic idea, the
{{term scientific view of practice | 科学实践观}}: how Marx arrived at it by criticising
every philosopher before him, how later Marxists developed it, and why the textbook says
it changed philosophy. Almost everything else in the chapter (what knowledge is, how it
develops, what truth is) is built on it, so it is worth getting the story straight.

## Two warm-ups: the cave and the drawing

The lecture opens with a picture of **Plato's cave**, from Plato's *Republic*
(《理想国》). Prisoners are chained facing a wall all their lives. Behind them a fire
burns, and people carry objects past it — a horse, a dog — so the prisoners see only
shadows moving on the wall. They take the shadows for the real things. Only a prisoner
who is freed, turns round and walks out of the cave into the sunlight sees the world as
it is.

Why do the prisoners first form wrong ideas? The course's reading: because they can only
*look*. They never touch, move or change the things they see, so they have nothing to
check their impressions against. The freed prisoner learns by **acting** — getting up,
turning, climbing out.

The second warm-up is a classroom activity. Close your eyes, draw a square, then draw a
circle inside it that just touches all four sides — without opening your eyes or feeling
the edge of the paper. Then compare drawings with your neighbour. The slide draws three
lessons from it:

| Question | Answer on the slide | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do you know what a square and a circle look like? | 认识 (knowledge) | You already have the idea in your head. |
| Then why can't you draw them well with your eyes shut? | 缺乏实践 (lack of practice) | Knowing *about* something isn't the same as being able to do it; the idea has to be tested against reality. |
| Open your eyes and draw it ten times: does it get better? | 实践指导认识 (practice guides knowledge) | Repeated doing, with feedback from what you see, corrects and improves what you know. |

::: callout key The point of the warm-ups
Knowledge cut off from practice is poor and easily wrong; practice is what produces,
corrects and improves it. That is the theme of the whole lesson.
:::

## What Marxist epistemology asks

{{term Epistemology | 认识论}} is the part of philosophy that studies knowledge: where it
comes from, what it is, how it develops and how we can tell whether it is true. The
slides put the three questions this way: *why* do we come to know the world, *how is it
possible* for us to know it, and *how* do we come to know it?

::: define Dialectical-materialist epistemology | 辩证唯物主义认识论
The Marxist theory of knowledge. Its basic standpoint and viewpoints fall into three
parts:
1. the **scientific view of practice** (科学实践观): practice is the basis of knowledge;
2. the **theory of the essence of knowledge** (认识本质论): what knowing is;
3. the **theory of the process of knowledge** (认识过程论): how knowing develops.
:::

The lecture organises the chapter around three questions, one for each part:

| Part | Question the lecture asks |
| --- | --- |
| The scientific view of practice | 一、"知就是行"是正确的吗？ Is "knowing is doing" right? |
| The essence of knowledge | 二、认识世界是照镜子式的原物映现吗？ Is knowing the world like a mirror reflecting things? |
| The process of knowledge | 三、认识能不能一蹴而就？ Can knowledge be reached in one go? |

This lesson and the next two answer the first question. The other two follow in later
lessons.

## The opening question: is "knowing doing"?

The relation between {{term knowing and doing | 知与行}} is one of the great themes of
Chinese philosophy. Its most famous answer is Wang Yangming's.

::: thinker Wang Yangming | 王阳明
dates: 1472–1529 (Ming dynasty)
works: *Instructions for Practical Living* 《传习录》 (his sayings and letters, compiled by his students)
The leading thinker of the Ming "School of Mind" (心学), which holds that principle is
found in one's own mind (心即理). He taught the **unity of knowing and acting**
(知行合一), in a moral sense: true moral knowledge (良知) already is action. He also
compared the sage's mind to a bright mirror that reflects everything (Lesson 4.3). The
course treats his thought as subjective idealism.
:::

In the *Instructions*, Wang complains that people treat knowing and acting as two
separate things, so when a bad thought arises they don't stop it, because they haven't
"done" anything yet. He insists that the moment a thought arises, that already **is**
acting — so a bad thought must be rooted out at once.

> 今人学问，只因知行分作两件，故有一念发动，虽是不善，然却未曾行，便不去禁止。我今说个知行合一，正要人晓得一念发动处，便即是行了。——王阳明《传习录》

::: define Unity of knowing and acting | 知行合一
Wang Yangming's thesis that knowing and acting are one thing: true knowing is already
acting ("知就是行，行就是知"). He meant it about moral cultivation: a thought counts as
a deed.
:::

As moral advice it has a point — watch your thoughts. But as a theory of knowledge the
slides ask: **what exactly is 知, and what is 行? Which comes first, and which is the
basis?** To answer, we need a precise idea of "doing", which philosophy took a long time
to reach. The course gives its answer at the end of section 4 (Lesson 4.3): Wang
exaggerates 知 and confuses it with 行.

## What philosophers before Marx said about practice

The word "practice" (实践, German *Praxis*) is old. The slides quote three German
philosophers who all gave it an important place — and, in the course's account, all
misunderstood it.

| Philosopher | What he said (as quoted on the slides) | How he understood practice | The course's verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Immanuel Kant 康德 (1724–1804) | "Everything that is possible through freedom is practical." 一切通过自由而可能的东西都是实践的。(*Critique of Pure Reason*) | An **a-priori moral activity of reason**: practice means acting on the moral law that reason gives itself. | Practice shrinks to ethics inside the mind. |
| G. W. F. Hegel 黑格尔 (1770–1831) | "Consciousness deals with reality by transforming it, by acting." 意识对付现实性的办法就是改造或行动。(*Phenomenology of Spirit*) | A **spiritual activity in which the subject realises itself**: it is Spirit (the Idea) that acts and changes the world. | Comes close — practice changes reality — but it is the activity of spirit, not of real people. |
| Ludwig Feuerbach 费尔巴哈 (1804–1872) | "From the practical standpoint, man deals with all things only for himself, for his own interest and use." (*Lectures on the Essence of Religion*) | **Everyday life activity**, even the same as an animal adapting to its surroundings: eating, using things. | A materialist, but sees practice as something low and selfish, not as what transforms the world. |

::: callout key The common failure
In the course's account, none of them **grasped the essence of human practice**, and none
saw its **decisive role in social life and in knowledge**. Kant and Hegel made practice
an activity of reason or spirit (idealism); Feuerbach made it ordinary, almost animal
behaviour (an old, contemplative materialism).
:::

## Marx founds the scientific view of practice

### The Theses on Feuerbach

Marx founded the scientific view of practice in his *Theses on Feuerbach*
(《关于费尔巴哈的提纲》): eleven short notes he wrote in Brussels in the spring of 1845.
They were never meant for print. Engels found them in Marx's notebook after Marx's death
and published them in 1888, as an appendix to his *Ludwig Feuerbach and the End of
Classical German Philosophy*. In the preface he described them as notes hastily written
for later study, but "invaluable as the first document in which is deposited the
brilliant germ of the new world outlook" (包含着新世界观的天才萌芽的第一个文献).

What do the Theses say? The slides pick out four points.

**1. The chief defect of all earlier materialism.** The first thesis begins:

> 从前的一切唯物主义（包括费尔巴哈的唯物主义）的主要缺点是：对对象、现实、感性，只是从客体的或者直观的形式去理解，而不是把它们当做感性的人的活动，当做实践去理解，不是从主体方面去理解。——马克思《关于费尔巴哈的提纲》

In plain words: old materialists, Feuerbach included, treated the world only as an
**object to be looked at** (直观, "contemplation"), as if humans were spectators. They
didn't see that the world people live in is shaped by **human activity**, and that we
come to know things by working on them. So they couldn't explain the active side of
knowing — and, the thesis goes on, idealism took that side over, though only abstractly.

From this the slide draws Marx's characterisation of practice:

> 实践是感性的、对象性的物质活动。

Practice is a **sensuous** (real, perceptible), **object-directed** (it works on and
changes something outside us) **material** activity — not a thought, and not passive
looking.

**2. All social life is essentially practical.**

> 凡是把理论引向神秘主义的神秘东西，都能在人的实践中以及对这种实践的理解中得到合理的解决。

Questions that seem mysterious when you only think about them (what is man? where does
religion come from?) find their rational answer when you look at what people actually do.
A year later, in *The German Ideology* (《德意志意识形态》, 1845–46), Marx and Engels
went further: the **production of the means of material life** is the first social
practice in human history, the precondition of every other historical activity. People
must eat, drink, have shelter and clothes before they can do politics, science or art.

**3. Practice is the test of truth.**

> 人的思维是否具有客观的真理性，这不是一个理论的问题，而是一个实践的问题。人应该在实践中证明自己思维的真理性，即自己思维的现实性和力量，自己思维的此岸性。

Whether an idea is objectively true can't be settled by more arguing; it is settled by
**putting it into practice**. Thinking proves its "this-sidedness" (此岸性: that it
belongs to this real world) by working.

**4. The basic task of philosophy.** The eleventh thesis, later engraved on Marx's
tombstone:

> 哲学家们只是用不同的方式解释世界，问题在于改变世界。

"The philosophers have only interpreted the world in various ways; the point is to
change it." (In Marx's German: *Die Philosophen haben die Welt nur verschieden
interpretirt, es kommt darauf an sie zu verändern.*)

### What the Theses mean for Marxism

| What the slides say | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| Their writing marks Marx's **complete break with Feuerbach** | Marx stops being a follower of Feuerbach's materialism. |
| The first time Marxist philosophy **drew a clear line against all old materialism** | Marxism is not just "materialism again", but a new kind based on practice. |
| Marx's **final break with every influence of bourgeois philosophy** | Neither Hegel's idealism nor Feuerbach's contemplative materialism any more. |
| Laid the foundation for a **complete, thoroughgoing materialist world outlook** | Materialism that reaches into society and history too (historical materialism). |

::: define Scientific view of practice | 科学实践观
Marx's view, founded in the *Theses on Feuerbach* (1845), that practice is the
**sensuous, object-directed material activity** of human beings; that social life is
essentially practical; that practice is the basis of knowledge and the test of its
truth; and that philosophy's task is to change the world, not only to interpret it.
:::

::: callout pitfall "Practice" in the Marxist sense is not just "doing something"
Wang Yangming's 行 is a moral act of the mind; Kant's practice is moral reasoning;
Feuerbach's is daily consumption. Marx's practice is **material activity that changes
the objective world**: producing, organising society, experimenting. A thought,
however serious, is not practice. Lesson 4.2 gives the precise definition.
:::

## How the view of practice was developed

::: timeline The scientific view of practice: founding and development
1845 (spring) | Marx writes the *Theses on Feuerbach* in Brussels | 《关于费尔巴哈的提纲》
1845–46 | Marx and Engels, *The German Ideology*: material production is the first historical act | 《德意志意识形态》
1867 | *Capital*, vol. 1: the bee and the architect (Lesson 4.2) | 《资本论》第一卷
1888 | Engels publishes the Theses as an appendix to *Ludwig Feuerbach* | 《路德维希·费尔巴哈和德国古典哲学的终结》
1908–09 | Lenin, *Materialism and Empirio-criticism*: practice is the first and basic standpoint of epistemology | 《唯物主义和经验批判主义》
1937 | Mao Zedong's lectures *On Practice* | 《实践论》
1938 | Mao, *On Protracted War*: practice is "the subjective made objective" | 《论持久战》
1978 (May) | The debate on the criterion of truth begins with the article "Practice is the sole criterion for testing truth" | 真理标准问题大讨论
1978 (13 Dec) | Deng Xiaoping's speech at the closing session of the Central Work Conference | 《解放思想，实事求是，团结一致向前看》
:::

**Lenin** applied Marxism to the Russian revolution and, in the course's account,
enriched the view of practice. In *Materialism and Empirio-criticism* he states:

> 生活、实践的观点，应该是认识论的首要的和基本的观点。

"The standpoint of life, of practice, should be first and fundamental in the theory of
knowledge." He also traced the dialectical movement of knowledge on the basis of
practice, and argued that taking practice as the basis of epistemology **leads
necessarily to materialism** (如果把实践标准作为认识论的基础，那么我们就必然得出唯物主义):
practice deals with real things, so it can't be squared with the view that the world is
only our sensations.

**Mao Zedong** and the Chinese Communists, combining Marxism with China's conditions and
traditional culture, "creatively developed" the view of practice in the textbook's
account. Mao described practice as **"the subjective made objective"**
(主观见之于客观的东西):

> 思想等等是主观的东西，做或行动是主观见之于客观的东西，都是人类特殊的能动性。——毛泽东《论持久战》

Ideas are subjective; doing is the subjective *showing itself in* the objective —
a plan in the head becoming a change in the world. Both are humanity's special
conscious activity. In *On Practice* (1937) — whose full title adds "on the relation
between knowledge and practice, between knowing and doing" (论认识和实践的关系——知和行的关系)
— Mao set out systematically the place and decisive role of social practice in knowledge.
Many of the quotations in Lesson 4.3 come from it.

**Deng Xiaoping** firmly supported the 1978 **debate on the criterion of truth**, which
the course says laid the ideological foundation for reform and opening up. He stressed
that practice is the **sole** criterion of truth, and that thought must match reality,
the subjective the objective. (The slides show his calligraphy 实践是检验真理的唯一标准
and a still from his 13 December 1978 speech.)

::: callout note Why the 1978 debate mattered
In the course's account, after the Cultural Revolution some held that whatever Mao had
said or decided must be upheld (the "two whatevers"). The debate restated a principle
from the Theses: no statement, whoever made it, is true just because of who said it;
only practice decides. That reopened the way to "emancipating the mind and seeking truth
from facts" (解放思想，实事求是).
:::

## The four meanings of the scientific view of practice

The textbook sums up why the founding and development of the scientific view of practice
matter in four points (科学实践观的意义). They are a classic short-answer question, so
learn them in order.

::: law The four meanings of the scientific view of practice | 科学实践观的四个方面意义
1. **It overcame the basic defect of old materialism and laid the theoretical
   foundation for dialectical materialism.** It understands the real world, and its
   relation to people, starting from practice; on the basis of practice it unites
   materialism and dialectics organically; so it founded a new, modern form of
   materialism. (克服了旧唯物主义的根本缺陷，为辩证唯物主义的创立奠定了科学的理论基础)
2. **It revealed the decisive role of practice in knowledge and laid the foundation for
   the active, revolutionary theory of reflection.** Practice is the basis, driving
   force and purpose of knowledge, the basis of the unity of truth and value, and the
   sole criterion of truth. This refutes idealist a-priorism and agnosticism, and
   overcomes old materialism's passive, "intuitive" theory of reflection.
   (揭示了实践对认识的决定性作用，为能动的、革命的反映论的创立奠定了科学的理论基础)
3. **For the first time in the history of thought, it revealed the practical essence of
   social life and laid the foundation for historical materialism.** Social practice,
   above all producing the means of life, is the basis of society's existence and
   development; every social "problem" arises in practice and can only be solved by
   "revolutionising practice", not by interpretation. Carrying practice into history
   unified the materialist-dialectical view of nature and of history.
   (第一次揭示了社会生活的实践本质，为唯物史观的创立奠定了科学的理论基础)
4. **It gave people the basic method of thinking and of working for actively knowing and
   changing the world.** Theory must be put into practice, guide practice, become the
   action of the masses and a material force for changing the world.
   (为人们能动地认识世界和改造世界提供了基本的思想方法和工作方法)
:::

**Methodological requirement.** Start from practice when you try to understand anything;
link theory with practice (理论联系实际); put what you learn into action and test it there;
and, in changing both the objective world and your own subjective world, bring your
initiative, drive and creativity into play.

{{term The active, revolutionary theory of reflection | 能动的革命的反映论}} is the
Marxist theory of knowledge itself: knowing *reflects* the objective world (materialism),
but actively and on the basis of practice, which changes the world (dialectics).
{{term Old materialism's intuitive theory of reflection | 直观反映论}} saw the mind as a
passive receiver of impressions — the mirror picture Lesson 4.3 takes apart.

::: callout exam How this is examined
- Short answers: "科学实践观的创立有什么意义?" — give the four points, each with its
  one-line reason.
- Multiple choice: which work founded the scientific view of practice? (the *Theses on
  Feuerbach*, 1845, not the *Manifesto*); who called practice "主观见之于客观的东西"?
  (Mao, *On Protracted War*); who said practice is "the first and basic standpoint of
  epistemology"? (Lenin; Mao repeats it in *On Practice*).
- The eleventh thesis is often quoted in material questions: it is about the **task of
  philosophy** (changing the world), not about the criterion of truth (that is the
  second thesis).
:::

::: example Plato's cave read with the view of practice
Why do the prisoners in Plato's cave take shadows for real things, and what does the
scientific view of practice say about how they could find out the truth?

== Step 1: what the prisoners lack ==
They have impressions (shadows), but no **practice**: they can't turn round, handle the
objects or test what they see. Their "knowledge" is pure contemplation — exactly the
"intuitive" view the first thesis criticises.

== Step 2: what the freed prisoner does ==
He moves, turns, climbs out and compares the shadows with the things. His ideas change
because his activity brings him into contact with the objects themselves.

== Step 3: Plato's answer and Marx's ==
Plato, an idealist, meant the story about the soul turning towards eternal Ideas (the
true reality, for him, beyond the senses). Read with Marx, the moral is different: we
escape illusions not by turning away from the material world but by **acting on it**.
Knowledge is corrected in practice, and practice is the test of whether an idea is true.
:::

## Summary

::: callout summary The lesson in five lines
- Marxist epistemology has three parts: the scientific view of practice, the essence of
  knowledge, the process of knowledge.
- Kant (moral reason), Hegel (spirit) and Feuerbach (daily life) didn't grasp practice.
- Marx's *Theses on Feuerbach* (1845) founded the scientific view of practice: practice
  is sensuous, object-directed material activity; social life is practical; practice
  tests truth; philosophy must change the world.
- Lenin, Mao (*On Practice*, "主观见之于客观") and Deng (the 1978 debate) developed it.
- Four meanings: dialectical materialism; the active, revolutionary theory of
  reflection; historical materialism; a method of thinking and working.
:::
