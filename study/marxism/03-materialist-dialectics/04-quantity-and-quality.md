Lesson 3.3 showed *why* things change: the contradictions inside them. This lesson
asks *how* change happens. Does the world change smoothly, a little at a time, or in
sudden jumps? The course's answer is "both, in turn": small, gradual changes pile up
until, at a certain point, the thing becomes something else. This is the
{{term law of quantitative and qualitative change | 量变质变规律}}, the second of the
three basic laws of materialist dialectics, which reveals the **state in which things
exist and the form their development takes** (世界存在的状态与发展的形式). It is one of
the most examined parts of the course, because it comes with three practical lessons
that exams love: keep to the right measure, do the patient everyday work, and seize the
moment when a leap is due.

## How do grains become a heap?

The lecture opened with an old puzzle from the Greek philosopher Eubulides
(欧布里德, 4th century BC), the {{term heap argument | 谷堆论证}}. One grain of corn is
not a heap. Add one more grain: still not a heap. If adding a single grain never turns
a non-heap into a heap, then however many grains you add, you never get a heap. Yet
heaps obviously exist. Where did the reasoning go wrong?

The puzzle is a good way into this lesson because it sets two things side by side:
**how much** there is (the number of grains, which changes a little at a time) and
**what** the thing is (a few grains, or a heap). Dialectics answers the puzzle by saying
that these two are connected: each added grain changes the amount without changing
what the thing is, but the additions do not go on forever without effect. Somewhere
there is a limit, and when the amount crosses it, the thing itself changes. The
puzzle's mistake is to look only at one step at a time and never at the accumulation.

## Quality, quantity and measure

Every thing has three "determinations" (规定性): what it is, how much of it there is,
and the range within which it can stay what it is.

::: define Quality | 质
The inner determination that makes a thing the thing it is and distinguishes it from
other things.

> 质是一事物区别于其他事物的内在规定性。
:::

Quality is grasped through a thing's properties. Water is a liquid that flows and takes
the shape of its container; ice is a solid; steam is a gas. These are different
qualities.

::: define Quantity | 量
The determination of a thing that can be expressed in numbers: its size, degree,
speed and so on.

> 量是事物的规模、程度和速度等可以用数量关系表示的规定性。
:::

The temperature of the water, its volume, how fast it flows: all quantities. Quantity
also covers the **arrangement** of a thing's parts (see the definition of quantitative
change below).

::: define Measure | 度
The quantitative limit within which a thing keeps its quality: the thing's limit, range
and scope.

> 度是保持事物质的稳定性的数量界限，即事物的限度、幅度和范围。
:::

The points at either end of a measure, where quantity turns into quality, are called
{{term nodal points | 关节点}} (also 临界点, critical points).

The three are a unity, shown on the slide as a triangle with quantity, measure and
quality as its sides:

- A thing's **quality and quantity are unified**: there is no quality without a
  quantity and no quantity that isn't the quantity of some quality.
- That unity is **expressed in the measure**: the measure is exactly the range of
  quantity that goes with a given quality.

The standard picture is water at one standard atmosphere:

| Temperature (at 1 atm) | Quality | What happens at the boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below 0 ℃ | Ice (solid) | At 0 ℃, melting or freezing |
| Between 0 ℃ and 100 ℃ | Water (liquid) | Within this range, warming changes only the quantity |
| Above 100 ℃ | Steam (gas) | At 100 ℃, boiling or condensing |

Between 0 ℃ and 100 ℃ you can heat or cool the water as much as you like and it stays
water: that range is its measure. At 0 ℃ and 100 ℃ (the nodal points) a change in
quantity becomes a change in quality.

Engels generalised this. A certain minimum current is needed before the platinum wire
in a lamp glows; every metal has its point of incandescence and its melting point;
every liquid, at a given pressure, has a fixed freezing and boiling point; every gas
has a **critical point** at which pressure and cooling turn it into a liquid. In short,
he wrote, most of the so-called **physical constants** are nothing but marks of the
nodal points at which an increase or decrease in the quantity of motion causes a
qualitative change of state: "在这些关节点上，量转化为质" (at these nodal points,
quantity turns into quality).

::: callout note Where the nodal-points quotation comes from
The slide attributes this passage to Engels's *Dialectics of Nature* (《自然辩证法》).
It is from *Anti-Dühring* (《反杜林论》), Part One, chapter XII ("Dialectics: Quantity and
Quality"), where Engels discusses water's freezing and boiling points just before it.
:::

::: callout pitfall Measure is not quality
度 and 质 are easy to mix up in multiple-choice questions. **Quality** is *what* a
thing is (water, as opposed to ice). **Measure** is a *range of quantity*: the limits
(0–100 ℃) inside which that quality is kept. A measure is always a quantitative limit
tied to a particular quality, so it unites the two, but it is not the quality itself.
:::

## The principle of moderation

Because every quality holds only within its measure, the course draws a rule for life
and work: in everything, keep a sense of proportion and stick to the
{{term principle of moderation | 适度原则}} (在实际工作和生活中，凡事都要注意掌握分寸，
坚持适度原则). Chinese has many idioms for it, six of which were on the slide:

| Idiom | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 过犹不及 | Going too far is as bad as not going far enough |
| 适可而止 | Stop at the right point |
| 不疾不徐 | Neither too fast nor too slow |
| 恰到好处 | Just right |
| 张弛有度 | Tension and relaxation in due measure |
| 恰如其分 | Exactly fitting |

Exercise makes you stronger, too much injures you; fertiliser helps a crop, too much
burns it; a little pressure motivates a student, too much breaks them. Each time, a
quantity that is good within its range turns into its opposite when it crosses a limit.

But moderation does not mean never crossing a limit. That is the point of a
discussion question the slides set as a 辨析题 (judge-and-explain question).

::: example 辨析: "Holding to moderation means never going beyond a thing's measure"
Judge the statement 掌握适度原则就是任何时候都不要超过事物的度 and explain.
== Judge ==
The statement is **wrong**.
== The principle ==
The measure is the quantitative limit within which a thing keeps its quality. This
category tells us to hold to the principle of moderation when we understand and
handle things.
== Analysis ==
It depends on what we want. To understand a thing and to keep it existing and
developing normally, we must keep it within its measure: a healthy body, a working
machine, a stable society. But to make a thing **develop**, we must at some point go
beyond its old measure, and especially seize the right moment to bring about a
qualitative change: a student who never goes beyond what they can already do never
learns anything new.
== Model answer ==
这一观点是错误的。度是保持事物质的稳定性的数量界限，即事物的限度、幅度和范围。度这一
哲学范畴启示我们在认识和处理问题时要掌握适度原则。为了认识事物并维持事物的存在与正常
发展，必须保持事物原有的度；为了促进事物发展，又必须超出事物原有的度，特别要适时抓住
时机，促进事物的质变。适度原则并不是要限制我们认识和改造世界，要学会辩证对待并灵活运用
这一原则。
:::

## Quantitative change and qualitative change

Now to change itself. Things change in two ways.

::: define Quantitative change | 量变
A change in the amount of a thing (increase or decrease) or in the order in which its
components are arranged; an inconspicuous change that keeps the thing's quality
relatively stable.

> 量变是事物数量的增减和组成要素排列次序的变动，是保持事物的质的相对稳定性的不显著变化。
:::

Note the two kinds: **more or less** of something (water warming from 20 ℃ to 60 ℃) and
a **rearrangement** of the same parts (the desks in a classroom moved from rows into
groups). Rearrangement matters because it, too, can build up to a change of quality:
the textbook's example is chemistry, where ethanol and dimethyl ether have exactly the
same atoms (C₂H₆O) arranged differently and are different substances.

::: define Qualitative change | 质变
A fundamental change in the nature of a thing: a leap from one state of quality to
another.

> 质变是事物性质的根本变化，是事物由一种质态向另一种质态的飞跃。
:::

::: callout key The core difference
The slide asked: what is the core difference between quantitative and qualitative
change? It is **whether the thing's quality changes**, which is the same as asking
**whether the change stays inside the measure** or breaks through it. Quantitative
change is gradual, continuous and hard to notice, and the thing stays what it is;
qualitative change is a leap, a break in continuity, after which the thing has become
something else.
:::

| Aspect | Quantitative change (量变) | Qualitative change (质变) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What changes | Amount, degree, or the arrangement of parts | The nature of the thing |
| Relation to measure | Stays within the measure | Breaks through the measure |
| How it looks | Gradual, continuous, inconspicuous | A leap, a break, conspicuous |
| The thing afterwards | Still the same thing | A new thing (new quality) |

The class was then asked for examples from their own field. That question is the
teacher's discussion question for week 4.

::: example Think it through: an example of quantitative and qualitative change from your own field
The week-4 question: 结合专业领域，举出反映量变质变的例子. Here is an answer from
electronics, with ideas for other majors at the end.
== Pick a case with a clear limit ==
A capacitor stores charge between two plates separated by an insulator (the
dielectric). Raise the voltage across it: 1 V, 5 V, 20 V… The charge stored grows, the
electric field in the dielectric grows, but the dielectric is still an insulator and the
capacitor still works. That is **quantitative change** within a measure: the
capacitor's rated voltage marks the safe range.
== Find the nodal point ==
At the dielectric's breakdown voltage the insulator suddenly conducts: a current
punches through it and the capacitor is destroyed or becomes, in effect, a conductor.
The thing's quality has changed: **qualitative change**, a leap. The same pattern
appears in a diode, which conducts almost nothing until its forward voltage reaches
about 0.6–0.7 V (for silicon) and then conducts freely.
== Read it with the law ==
Each small increase in voltage prepared the breakdown (quantitative change is the
necessary preparation); once the field passed the limit, breakdown had to happen
(qualitative change is the necessary result). This is why engineers rate components
with a margin: knowing the measure and keeping within it is the principle of moderation
in practice.
== Other fields ==
Computing: a server handles more and more requests per second until, at its capacity,
response times explode and it falls over. Economics: small price rises are absorbed
until inflation crosses a point where behaviour changes. Medicine: a dose is a cure
within its range and a poison beyond it. Learning: hours of practice add up until a
skill "clicks".
== Model answer ==
量变质变规律揭示了事物发展的形式和状态：量变是质变的必要准备，质变是量变的必然结果，
量变和质变相互渗透。以电容器为例：在额定电压以内，电压升高只使电容器储存的电荷和介质中
的电场强度增加，电容器仍能正常工作，这是量变；当电压超过介质的击穿电压（关节点）时，绝缘
介质被击穿而导电，电容器的性质发生根本变化，这是质变。因此，在专业学习和工程实践中，既要
重视量的积累、把握事物的度（留出安全裕量），又要在需要时善于促成质变。
:::

## How quantity and quality turn into each other

The lecture followed the relationship through one long case: the
{{term Red Flag Canal | 红旗渠}} in Linxian (now Linzhou), Henan. The county was
chronically short of water, so from 1960 its people cut a canal through the cliffs of
the Taihang Mountains to bring water from the Zhang River in Shanxi (引漳入林, "lead
the Zhang into Lin"). With hand tools and enormous labour, they had the main canal
flowing in 1965 and finished the whole network, about 1,500 km with its branches, in
1969. The course uses it, and the spirit it stands for (红旗渠精神), to show each point
of the relationship. The slides open each point with lines from Xunzi's
*Exhortation to Learning* (《荀子·劝学》), the classic Chinese statement of
accumulation.

### Quantitative change is the necessary preparation for qualitative change

No leap comes from nothing: a qualitative change has to be prepared by enough
quantitative change. The canal was built by the day's progress piling up, metre by
metre. Xunzi: piling up earth makes a mountain, piling up water makes a deep pool;
"不积跬步，无以致千里；不积小流，无以成江海" (without accumulating half-steps you cannot
travel a thousand li; without accumulating small streams there is no river or sea).

### Qualitative change is the necessary result of quantitative change

Accumulation does not go on forever without consequence: once quantitative change
reaches the limit of the measure, a qualitative change *must* follow. When the 616-metre
Youth Tunnel (青年洞) was cut through and water flowed down the canal, the dry valley
became an irrigated one. Xunzi: "锲而舍之，朽木不折；锲而不舍，金石可镂" (carve and give up,
and even rotten wood won't break; carve without stopping, and metal and stone can be
engraved).

### Quantitative and qualitative change interpenetrate

The two are not cleanly separated: each contains something of the other.

::: define Partial qualitative change | 部分质变
A change of quality that happens **within** a process of quantitative change while the
thing's fundamental quality stays the same. It is **stage-wise** (阶段性部分质变): the
whole passes through distinct stages, each a partial change in quality; or **local**
(局部性部分质变): some parts change quality while the whole has not yet changed.
:::

On the canal, the project as a whole was still "a canal under construction" for nearly
ten years, but it advanced in stages (each finished section of the main canal, then the
branches) — stage-wise partial changes — and individual tunnels and sections were
completed one by one — local partial changes.

In the other direction, **qualitative change contains quantitative expansion**: during
a qualitative change the old quality shrinks in quantity while the new quality expands
(旧质在量上的收缩，新质在量上的扩张). The slide shows it as the line of workers along a
dry gorge turning into a river in a green valley: the new state, irrigated land, spread
out section by section as the old dry state shrank.

::: example Reading the Red Flag Canal with the law
Use the Red Flag Canal to illustrate the three points of the relationship between
quantitative and qualitative change.
== Preparation ==
Nearly ten years of daily cutting, blasting and hauling, each day adding a little length:
quantitative change, without which no canal could exist.
== The leap ==
When the Youth Tunnel was holed through and water flowed, the water supply of the
county changed fundamentally: qualitative change, the necessary result of the
accumulation.
== Interpenetration ==
Within the long quantitative process, stages and sections were completed (partial
qualitative change); within the qualitative change, the irrigated area grew while the
dry area shrank (quantitative expansion of the new quality).
== Lesson ==
The canal's builders did the patient daily work, and also pushed through at the key
moments: exactly the methodology of the law below.
:::

::: example Why does 90% × 5 fail?
An in-class question: 90% × 90% × 90% × 90% × 90% ≈ 59%. Ninety looks like an excellent
mark, but in a chain of linked tasks, if every link loses a little, the result is a
fail. What dialectical principle is this?
== The quantities ==
Each step keeps 90% of the quality of the work: one 10% shortfall is small. But the
shortfalls multiply: 0.9 to the fifth power is 0.59049.
== The leap ==
Somewhere along the chain the accumulated loss crosses the pass line (60%): the result
changes from "pass" to "fail", a change of quality.
== The principle ==
**Quantitative change leads to qualitative change** (answer B in the quiz below). It
is also a warning in the spirit of bottom-line thinking: small slips, left alone, add
up to crossing a line.
:::

## The law and what it demands of us

Put together, the relationship is a rhythm that runs through all development.

::: law The law of quantitative and qualitative change | 量变质变规律
Quantitative change leads to qualitative change; on the basis of the new quality, the
thing begins a new quantitative change; the two alternate in this way without end, and
this alternation makes up the process of a thing's development:
量变 → 质变 → 新的量变 → 新的质变 → …

> 量变引起质变，在新质的基础上，事物又开始新的量变，如此交替循环，构成了事物的发展过程。

The relationship has three parts: quantitative change is the necessary preparation for
qualitative change; qualitative change is the necessary result of quantitative change;
quantitative and qualitative change interpenetrate (partial qualitative change within
quantitative change, quantitative expansion within qualitative change).
:::

**Methodological requirements (方法论意义).**

1. **While a thing is in the stage of quantitative change**, do the everyday work solidly
   and patiently, preparing for the major change to come (当事物的发展处在量变阶段时，要
   踏踏实实做好日常工作，为未来重大改变作准备). Don't expect a leap without accumulation, and
   don't despise small things.
2. **When a qualitative change arrives**, seize the opportunity resolutely and without
   losing time, bring the change about, and take the work to a new level (当质变来临时，
   要果断地、不失时机地抓住机遇，促成质变，使工作迈上新台阶).
3. Throughout, **hold to the principle of moderation**: keep a thing within its measure
   when you want it to stay as it is, and break through the measure at the right time
   when you want it to develop.

::: callout pitfall Gradualism and "leapism"
The law rules out two one-sided views. Seeing only quantitative change (development is
smooth growth, nothing ever really becomes new) is vulgar evolutionism (庸俗进化论);
seeing only leaps (we can jump to the goal without preparation) is the theory of sudden
change (激变论), which ignores the necessary accumulation. The law insists on both, in turn.
:::

## Bottom-line thinking

The last part of the lecture applied the measure to policy. The slides use a news
case: tanker lorries that carried edible oil were found also to carry chemical liquids,
without the tanks being cleaned in between. Food safety has a line that may not be
crossed: once it is crossed, edible oil is no longer food but a danger. Another slide
shows global food insecurity (a 2021 UN report: at least 155 million people in severe
food insecurity in 2020) beside China's "red line" of 1.8 billion *mu* (18亿亩) of
farmland, the minimum area that must never be built over.

::: define Bottom-line thinking | 底线思维能力
The ability to set a minimum goal objectively and, standing on that lowest point, to
strive for the best possible outcome.

> 底线思维能力是客观地设定最低目标，立足最低点争取最大期望值的能力。

The **bottom line** (底线) is a boundary that must not be crossed: the critical point at
which a thing undergoes qualitative change (所谓底线，就是不可逾越的界限，是事物发生质变的
临界点).
:::

This is why the course puts bottom-line thinking right after the measure: the bottom
line is a nodal point that we deliberately refuse to cross, because what lies beyond it
(food unsafe, farmland lost, a system collapsing) is a change of quality we do not
want. Strengthening bottom-line thinking is the precondition for not stepping on "red
lines" or into "minefields" (不踩"红线"、不越"雷区"). Lesson 3.6 returns to it as one of
the seven thinking abilities, with what it requires.

::: callout exam How this law is examined
- **Multiple choice**: idioms and sayings (积土成山, 千里之堤溃于蚁穴, 过犹不及, 90%×90%…)
  to be matched with "quantitative change leads to qualitative change" or "hold to the
  measure"; distractors are usually other laws (肯定中包含否定, 必然通过偶然).
- **Definitions**: know 质, 量, 度, 量变, 质变 word for word.
- **辨析题**: "moderation means never exceeding the measure" (wrong: see above).
- **Material analysis**: a case of accumulation and breakthrough (the Red Flag Canal, a
  career, a country's development): state the three points of the relationship, then
  the two methodological requirements.
:::
