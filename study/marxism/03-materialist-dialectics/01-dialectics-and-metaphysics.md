Unit 2 asked what the world **is**: matter, with consciousness as its product. This unit
asks what **state** the world is in: are things isolated and fixed, or connected and
changing? The course's answer is **materialist dialectics** (唯物辩证法), and this lesson
sets it up: the two opposite ways of looking at things, where the words "dialectics" and
"metaphysics" come from, what Marx and Engels took from Hegel, and the map of laws and
categories that the rest of the unit fills in. Exam questions about any later law
start from the contrasts drawn here.

## Two questions of a worldview

A worldview (世界观, Lesson 2.1) has to answer two different questions about the world:

| Question | What it asks | The two answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The world's **essence** (世界的本质问题) | What is the world, at bottom: matter or spirit? | Materialism (唯物主义) vs idealism (唯心主义) |
| The world's **state** (世界的状态问题) | How does the world exist: connected and developing, or isolated and static? | Dialectics (辩证法) vs metaphysics (形而上学) |

The first question is the basic question of philosophy, covered in Lesson 2.1. The
second is this unit's subject. The two questions are independent, so the answers
combine: a philosopher can be a materialist yet think metaphysically (the
mechanical materialists of the 17th–18th centuries), or an idealist yet think
dialectically (Hegel). Marxism joins materialism with dialectics: **dialectical
materialism**.

::: callout pitfall Materialism is not dialectics
"Materialism vs idealism" and "dialectics vs metaphysics" are two different
oppositions, answering two different questions. Don't write "metaphysics" as a
synonym for idealism, or "dialectics" as a synonym for materialism. Hegel was an
idealist **and** a dialectician; Feuerbach was a materialist whose method stayed
metaphysical.
:::

## The two opposed outlooks

Think of how two doctors might treat a headache. One looks only at the head, gives a
painkiller, and expects the problem to stay solved. The other asks what else is going
on (sleep, eyes, blood pressure, stress), how the condition has been changing, and
what is pulling it in different directions. The first doctor sees the problem as
**isolated, static and one-sided**; the second as **connected, developing and
whole**. That is the difference the course draws between metaphysics and dialectics.

::: define Dialectics | 辩证法
The way of thinking, and the theory, that looks at things, understands them and
handles problems from the standpoint of **connection, development and
comprehensiveness** (联系的、发展的、全面的观点), and that sees contradiction as the
source of things' own movement and development.
:::

::: define Metaphysics (as opposed to dialectics) | 形而上学
The way of thinking that looks at things, understands them and handles problems from
an **isolated, static and one-sided** (孤立的、静止的、片面的) standpoint, and denies
that contradiction is what moves things.
:::

| Point of comparison | Materialist dialectics | Metaphysics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How things relate | Connected (联系的) | Isolated (孤立的) |
| How things exist | Developing (发展的) | Static (静止的); change is only growth or shrinking in quantity, or a change of place |
| How things are seen | Comprehensively (全面的) | One-sidedly (片面的) |
| Contradiction | Admitted: the source and motive force of "self-movement" and "self-development" | Denied; change, if any, is pushed from outside |

::: callout key The focus of the opposition
The slides name the **focus** (对立的焦点) of the quarrel: whether contradiction is
admitted as the source and motive force of things' "self-movement" (自己运动) and
"self-development" (自己发展). Everything else follows from that.
:::

## Where the word "metaphysics" comes from

The word has a curious history. **Aristotle** (384–322 BC) founded a school in Athens,
the Lyceum (吕克昂学院). About three centuries later, **Andronicus of Rhodes** (安德罗尼可),
the school's eleventh head, edited Aristotle's writings. He put the works on nature
and bodies first and called them *Physics* (物理学); the works on the essence of things
and abstract principles he placed after them, under the title "the books after the
*Physics*" (Greek *ta meta ta physika*, 物理学之后). Aristotle himself had called that
subject "first philosophy" (第一哲学). So "metaphysics" began as a librarian's label
meaning "after physics", and came to mean the study of what lies beyond the physical.

When the word reached China, some translated it 玄学 ("the abstruse learning"). The name
that stuck, 形而上学, comes from the *Book of Changes* (《周易·系辞》): "形而上者谓之道"
(what is above form is called the Way). The slide credits **Yan Fu** (严复) with the
translation.

::: callout note Who coined 形而上学?
Many Chinese textbooks, like the slide, credit Yan Fu. The term is usually traced to
Meiji Japan, where Inoue Tetsujirō's dictionary of philosophy (《哲学字汇》, 1881) used
it for "metaphysics", taking it from the same line of the *Book of Changes*; it came into
Chinese around 1900. For the exam, the slide's account is what's expected; the point
either way is the source in 形而上者谓之道.
:::

The modern, anti-dialectical sense came much later. **Hegel** was the first to use
"metaphysics" this way: for a way of thinking that is isolated, static, one-sided and
denies contradiction. So the word now has two senses:

| Sense | What "metaphysics" means |
| --- | --- |
| In the sense of "philosophy" | The study that seeks the most general essence and the most universal laws of things |
| In opposition to "dialectics" | A way of thinking that "thinks in absolutely irreconcilable antitheses" (在"绝对不相容的对立中思维"): things are either this or that, never both |

::: callout pitfall Two meanings of one word
In this course, "metaphysics" nearly always means the second sense, the
anti-dialectical way of thinking. In a Western philosophy class, "metaphysics" usually
means the first: the branch of philosophy about being. Read the context.
:::

## Where the word "dialectics" comes from

The course names **Socrates** (苏格拉底, 469–399 BC) as the first philosopher to use the
idea of dialectics. Its first meaning was "conversation" or "the art of debate"
(谈话、论辩术): a way of reaching truth in an argument by **exposing and overcoming the
contradictions in the other side's claims**. Socrates would ask a confident speaker
what justice is, draw out what their answer implied, and show that it contradicted
something else they believed, so that both had to think again.

::: callout note Socrates or Zeno?
The slide credits Socrates, and so does the course. Aristotle called **Zeno of Elea**
the inventor of dialectic, and Plato made it the name of his method; Socrates'
question-and-answer is its best-known early form. In an exam, follow the course.
:::

Notice that even in this first sense, dialectics is tied to **contradiction**: truth is
found by facing contradictions, not by ignoring them.

## Hegel: dialectics as the soul of science

Two thousand years later, dialectics became a whole philosophy. For Hegel, dialectics
is "the true philosophical method" (真正的哲学方法), "the moving principle of all
motion, all life and all activity, and the soul of all genuinely scientific knowledge"
(是一切运动、生命、事业的推动原则，又是知识范围内一切真正科学知识的灵魂). The course
credits him with being the first to give dialectics its content of **connection,
development, comprehensiveness and contradiction**.

::: thinker Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel | 黑格尔
dates: 1770–1831
works: *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807), *Science of Logic* (1812–16), *Elements of the Philosophy of Right* (1821)
The great German classical philosopher, an **objective idealist**: for him the world is
the unfolding of the "absolute idea" or "absolute spirit". The course credits him with
three contributions to dialectics:

1. he showed nature, society and thought all to be in connection and development;
2. he set out the three great laws of connection and development and their basic
   categories;
3. he held that dialectics, logic and the theory of knowledge are one and the same.

And it names two serious defects:

1. **His dialectics was joined to idealism.** The absolute spirit exists before the
   material world and is its essence; development is really the development of
   concepts, and the development of nature and society is only a copy of it, not real.
2. **He didn't carry dialectics through to the end.** He gave knowledge an end point,
   truth a summit and history a final stage; he denied that development is universal
   (only the absolute spirit truly develops; nature and society are its outward show)
   and that contradiction is universal (there is none in the infinite absolute spirit,
   none in the final "synthesis").
:::

So Hegel's dialectics was, in a famous image, "standing on its head": the right method
in an idealist system that the method itself contradicted. A system that says
everything develops yet declares its own end point is at odds with itself.

## Marx and Engels: the "rational kernel"

Engels describes what a thinking person first sees in nature, history or their own
mind:

> "当我们通过思维来考察自然界或人类历史或我们自己的精神活动的时候，首先呈现在我们眼前的，是一幅由种种联系和相互作用无穷无尽地交织起来的画面，其中没有任何东西是不动的和不变的，而是一切都在运动、变化、生成和消逝。"
> (Engels, *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific*, 《社会主义从空想到科学的发展》)

That picture, endless connection and interaction, with nothing fixed, is what
dialectics tries to describe scientifically. Marx and Engels found its best version in
Hegel, but wrapped in idealism.

::: define Rational kernel | 合理内核
What Marx and Engels took from Hegel's idealist dialectics: its dialectical ideas of
connection, development and contradiction, separated from the idealist system (the
absolute spirit) that held them.
:::

In the 1840s, Marx and Engels, **on the basis of their scientific view of practice**,
critically absorbed the rational kernel of Hegel's idealist dialectics and joined it
organically with the new materialism. That revealed the objective state, dialectical
nature and overall picture of the material world's universal connection and eternal
development, and created dialectics in its **scientific form**.

::: define Materialist dialectics | 唯物辩证法
The scientific form of dialectics founded by Marx and Engels in the 1840s: Hegel's
"rational kernel" joined with the new materialism on the basis of the scientific view
of practice. It is the science of the **universal connection** and **eternal
development** of the material world.

> 马克思、恩格斯以科学实践观为依托，批判地吸收了黑格尔唯心辩证法的"合理内核"，并把它和新唯物主义有机地结合起来，创立了科学形态的唯物辩证法。
:::

::: timeline From "conversation" to materialist dialectics
5th century BC | Socrates uses dialectics as the art of conversation and debate: truth by exposing contradictions | 苏格拉底：谈话、论辩术
4th century BC | Aristotle founds the Lyceum and writes his "first philosophy" | 亚里士多德：第一哲学
1st century BC | Andronicus of Rhodes edits Aristotle; the books placed "after the *Physics*" give metaphysics its name | 安德罗尼可："物理学之后"
1807–1831 | Hegel makes dialectics a full philosophy, inside objective idealism, and uses "metaphysics" for anti-dialectical thinking | 黑格尔
1840s | Marx and Engels take the rational kernel and found materialist dialectics | 马克思、恩格斯创立唯物辩证法
1873–1886 | Engels' *Dialectics of Nature* (written 1873–83), *Anti-Dühring* (1878) and *Ludwig Feuerbach* (1886) set out its laws | 恩格斯
1913 | Lenin calls dialectics the most complete doctrine of development | 列宁《马克思主义的三个来源和三个组成部分》
:::

### Three classic definitions

The slides give three quotations that define dialectics; each stresses a different
side:

| Who and where | Definition | What it stresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engels, *Dialectics of Nature* (《自然辩证法》) | "辩证法是关于普遍联系的科学。" Dialectics is the science of universal connection. | Connection |
| Engels, *Ludwig Feuerbach and the End of Classical German Philosophy* (《路德维希·费尔巴哈和德国古典哲学的终结》) | Dialectics is "关于外部世界和人类思维的运动的一般规律的科学": the science of the general laws of motion of the external world and of human thought. | Laws of motion |
| Lenin, *The Three Sources and Three Component Parts of Marxism* (《马克思主义的三个来源和三个组成部分》) | "辩证法，即最完备最深刻最无片面性的关于发展的学说": the most complete, deepest and least one-sided doctrine of development. | Development |

Put together: **dialectics is the science of the universal connection of the material
world, and also the science of its eternal development**. These two, connection and
development, are the "overall features" (总特征) of materialist dialectics, the subject
of Lesson 3.2.

## What dialectics asks of us

The course turns the outlook into three requirements for how we look at problems, plus
a fourth that underlies them.

**Look at things in their connections**, following the objective links between them.
The slide's case is the **butterfly effect**: a butterfly flapping its wings in the
Amazon rainforest might, two weeks later, help set off a tornado in Texas. The tiny
airflow it stirs changes the air around it, which changes the systems around that, and
the change can grow rather than fade.

**Look at things in their development**, handling a process as a process. The slide
shows three photos: Shenzhen in the 1970s, a fishing town; the Shenzhen Special
Economic Zone today; and the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area (粤港澳大湾区),
with the banner "the road of reform" (改革之路). What a place is cannot be read from one
snapshot.

**Look at things comprehensively**, handling the relation between part and whole,
local and overall. Xi Jinping, explaining the decision on comprehensively deepening
reform (2013), called it a strategic plan for the whole of the Party's and the
country's work, "not a single reform in one field or one aspect", and quoted the Qing
writer Chen Danran (陈澹然): "不谋全局者，不足谋一域" (whoever does not plan for the
whole cannot plan well for one part).

**Look at things through contradiction.** This is the root of the other three. The
deepest difference between dialectics and metaphysics is whether contradiction is
admitted; whether it is admitted as the source and motive force of development; and
whether contradiction analysis (矛盾分析法) is admitted as the basic method of knowing
things. The slides show it with three questions that all come down to one issue:

| Question | Dialectics answers | Metaphysics answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is connection? | The connection of things that are in contradiction with each other | The connection of things that have nothing to do with each other |
| What is development? | The unity and struggle of opposites, the rise of the new and the death of the old | More or less of the same, or a change of place |
| What drives development? | The movement of the contradiction inside the thing itself | An outside push |

::: example Analysing the butterfly effect dialectically
The slide's case: Edward Lorenz's claim that a butterfly in the Amazon might set off a
tornado in Texas. Which requirement of dialectics does it illustrate, and how?

== The requirement ==
"Look at things in their connections": things are not isolated; each stands in a web
of objective connections with others.

== The case ==
The butterfly's wings change the air beside it; that small airflow changes the air
and other systems around it; step by step the change spreads and can grow, until it
contributes to a large change in a distant system. The butterfly and the tornado are
connected **through intermediate links** (Lesson 3.2 calls these "mediation", 中介).

== The lesson ==
A metaphysical thinker who looks only at the butterfly, or only at the tornado, can't
understand either. The dialectical view follows the real chain of connections, and
also notices that tiny initial differences can matter a lot (Lesson 3.2,
"conditionality").
:::

::: callout note The butterfly's date
The slide says Lorenz "proposed" the butterfly effect in 1963. His 1963 paper showed
that weather models depend sensitively on their starting conditions; the butterfly
image itself comes from the title of his 1972 talk ("Does the flap of a butterfly's
wings in Brazil set off a tornado in Texas?"). Either date may appear; the idea is the
same.
:::

## The map of materialist dialectics

The slides close the section with a map of everything the unit covers. Connection and
development are the **overall features** (总特征); the **basic laws** (基本规律) say how
connection and development work; and the **basic categories** (基本环节, the paired
concepts) are the knots in the net of connections.

| Part | Content | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall features (总特征) | Universal connection and eternal development | Lesson 3.2 |
| Law of the unity of opposites (对立统一规律) | The **source and motive force** of development (源泉、动力) | Lesson 3.3 |
| Law of quantitative and qualitative change (质量互变规律) | The **state and form** of development (状态) | Lesson 3.4 |
| Law of the negation of the negation (否定之否定规律) | The **direction and path** of development (趋势、道路) | Lesson 3.5 |
| Basic categories (基本环节) | Cause and effect; phenomenon and essence; content and form; necessity and chance; possibility and reality | Lesson 3.6 |
