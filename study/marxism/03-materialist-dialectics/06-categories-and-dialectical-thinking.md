The three laws (Lessons 3.3–3.5) describe how the world develops as a whole. But when
you face a particular new problem, where do you start? This lesson gives the tools.
First, five pairs of **categories**, the "knots" in which connection and development
show themselves: content and form, essence and appearance, cause and effect, necessity
and contingency, reality and possibility. Each pair is a pair of opposites, and each
comes with a methodological requirement that exams ask for. Then the lesson steps back:
what is materialist dialectics in essence, how does dialectical thinking relate to the
dialectics of the world, which thinking methods does it give us, and what are the
"seven thinking abilities" the course asks students to build. This closes Unit 3.

## What categories are

Lenin, reading Hegel's *Science of Logic* in 1914, wrote a note that the lecture
started from: before man there is a **net of natural phenomena**. Instinctive man, the
savage, does not distinguish himself from nature; conscious man does, and
"范畴是区分过程中的梯级，即认识世界的过程中的梯级，是帮助我们认识和掌握自然现象之网的网上纽结"
(categories are stages in this process of distinguishing, that is, of knowing the
world, the knots in the net that help us know and master it). The note is in his
*Conspectus of Hegel's "Science of Logic"* (《黑格尔〈逻辑学〉一书摘要》), part of the
*Philosophical Notes* (《哲学笔记》).

Where does the word come from? The Greek *katēgoria* was a legal term: an accusation, a
charge. Charging someone means assigning a responsibility to them, so the word also came
to mean "assigning to a class". Aristotle took it into philosophy, in the *Categories*,
to sort the ways we can say something about a thing. His ten categories:

| Category | Greek sense | Chinese |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Substance | what a thing is | 实体 |
| Quantity | how much | 数量 |
| Quality | what sort | 性质 |
| Relation | related to what | 关系 |
| Place | where | 地点 |
| Time | when | 时间 |
| Position | in what posture | 姿态 |
| State | having what (e.g. armed) | 状态 |
| Action | doing what | 活动 |
| Affection | undergoing what | 遭受 |

The Chinese word 范畴 has its own origin: the "nine divisions of the great plan"
(洪范九畴) in the *Book of Documents* (《尚书·洪范》). Tradition says that two years
after Zhou conquered Shang, the Shang noble Jizi (箕子) explained to King Wu of Zhou
(周武王) the nine fundamental rules for governing the world, beginning with the Five
Phases (五行). So 范畴 carries the sense of rule, norm and type.

::: define Category | 范畴
A concept that sums up and reflects the universal essence of things. The **basic
categories of materialist dialectics** reveal, each from its own angle, the basic
links (基本环节) in the connection and development of things; they are a concrete
application of the method of contradiction analysis, and have important methodological
significance for knowing and changing the world.

> 范畴是对事物普遍本质的概括和反映。
:::

Mastering materialist dialectics means mastering both its basic laws and its basic
categories. Faced with a new thing or a new problem, the five pairs give five questions
to ask: what is it made of, and in what form? What is it really, beneath what it looks
like? What caused it, and what will it cause? What in it is necessary and what is
chance? What is it now, and what could it become?

## Content and form

A book's content is its ideas and facts; its form is its structure, its language, the
printed volume or the e-book. The same story can be a novel, a film or a comic.

::: define Content and form | 内容与形式
**Content** is the sum of all the elements that make up a thing (构成事物的一切要素的
总和). **Form** is the structure that unifies those elements, or the way the content is
expressed (把诸要素统一起来的结构或表现内容的方式).
:::

**How they are related.**

- **Opposed**: content and form don't correspond one to one. Form has relative
  independence: the same content can be expressed in many forms (and one form can hold
  different contents).
- **Unified**: every thing is a unity of content and form. Content is the basis of a
  thing's existence and **determines** the form; form **reacts back** on the content
  (helping it when it fits, holding it back when it doesn't).

**Methodology.** Put the emphasis on content, and oppose **formalism** (形式主义), which
ignores content and exaggerates the role of form. When the content changes, the form
must change with it: keep replacing old forms that no longer fit the content, create
new ones, and use a suitable form to promote the content's development.

The slide's image of formalism is the old story 买椟还珠 from *Han Feizi*: a man from
Zheng buys a pearl in a beautifully decorated box, keeps the box and gives back the
pearl. Its modern version is the "sky-high mooncake", where the packaging is worth more
than the cakes, which four government departments acted together to curb.

## Essence and appearance

::: define Essence and appearance | 本质与现象
**Essence** is a thing's internal connections and fundamental nature (事物的内在联系和
根本性质). **Appearance** (phenomenon) is its external connections and surface features
(事物的外部联系和表面特征). Appearances are either **true appearances** (真象), which show
the essence directly, or **false appearances** (假象), which show it in a distorted,
reversed way.
:::

**How they are related.**

- **Opposed**: essence is general, universal, relatively stable and hidden inside the
  thing; appearance is individual, concrete, changeable and fleeting, and shows on the
  outside.
- **Unified**: every thing is a unity of essence and appearance. Essence determines
  appearance; appearance expresses essence. A true appearance expresses it positively;
  a false appearance expresses it negatively (反面表现本质): a false appearance is
  still an appearance of the thing, not an illusion in the observer's head.

**Methodology.** Use rational thinking to grasp the essence through the appearances,
going from this to that and from the outside in (由此及彼、由表及里), so that knowledge keeps
deepening. And collect a large number of appearances, using technical means and
theoretical analysis to discard the dross and keep the essential, discard the false
and keep the true (去粗取精、去伪存真), distinguishing true appearances from false ones.

::: example Jiufang Gao judges a horse
The story (from *Liezi*, 《列子·说符》): Bole, the great judge of horses, recommended
Jiufang Gao (九方皋) to Duke Mu of Qin. Jiufang Gao found a superb horse but reported
it as a yellow mare; it turned out to be a black stallion. The duke was angry, but Bole
praised him: what Gao looks at is the horse's inner workings; he grasps the essential
and forgets the coarse, sees the inside and forgets the outside (得其精而忘其粗，在其内
而忘其外).
== Appearance ==
Sex and colour are surface features: appearances that don't decide whether a horse is
a 千里马.
== Essence ==
The horse's build, spirit and strength: what makes it a superb horse.
== The lesson ==
Grasp the essence through the appearances, and don't be distracted by striking but
unimportant surface features. (The story exaggerates: appearances still matter, since
essence is only ever known through them.)
:::

## Cause and effect

::: define Cause and effect | 原因与结果
A **cause** is a phenomenon that brings about another phenomenon (引起某种现象的现象); an
**effect** is the phenomenon that a cause brings about (被某种现象即原因所引起的现象).
:::

**How they are related.**

- **Opposed**: in a given causal connection, the cause is the cause and the effect is
  the effect; the cause comes first and the effect after, and they must not be confused
  or reversed.
- **Unified**: cause and effect depend on each other, and under certain conditions
  turn into each other: what is a cause in one connection may be an effect in another,
  and the other way round (rain makes the crops grow; good crops cause a good harvest;
  the harvest causes higher income…).

**Methodology.** Grasp the causal connections of things correctly to guide what we know
and do; and use our conscious activity to remove unfavourable causes, so that the chain
of cause and effect runs in the direction that benefits people and reaches the results
we need. Two proverbs on the slide: 种瓜得瓜，种豆得豆 (plant melons and you get melons,
plant beans and you get beans: effects follow their causes), and 千里之堤，溃于蚁穴 (a
thousand-li dike collapses because of an ant hole: small causes, big effects, so remove
them early).

### Internal and external causes

Among the causes of a thing's development, the course distinguishes those inside it
from those outside it.

::: define Internal and external causes | 内因与外因
The **internal cause** is the contradiction inside a thing (事物的内部矛盾); the
**external cause** is the contradiction between a thing and other things (事物的外部
矛盾). The internal cause is the **basis** of change and **decides its direction**; the
external cause is the **condition** of change, which can **speed it up or slow it
down**.
:::

::: callout note The slide's definitions of 内因 and 外因
Slide 11-235 defines 内因 as "the thing's inner connections and fundamental nature" and
外因 as "its external connections and surface features": these are the definitions of
essence and appearance, copied from the slide before. The textbook's definitions are the
ones above: the internal and the external contradiction. The slide's diagram (内因:
internal, basis, decides the direction; 外因: external, condition, speeds up or slows
down) is right.
:::

::: law The dialectical relation of internal and external causes | 内因与外因的辩证关系
Internal and external causes are both indispensable: a thing's development is the
result of both acting together (内因外因缺一不可，事物发展是二者共同作用的结果). The
internal cause is the basis and the external cause the condition, and **external causes
work through internal causes** (外因必须通过内因起作用).
:::

**Methodology.** Put the emphasis on internal causes without neglecting external ones
(注重内因、不忽视外因); and seize opportunities (把握机遇), since favourable external
conditions don't last. Mao's image (from *On Contradiction*): in a suitable temperature
an egg turns into a chick, but no temperature can turn a stone into a chick.

::: example Cape Verde, the World Cup's dark horse
The class case (as the slides present it): at the 2026 World Cup, the small island
nation of Cape Verde, at its first World Cup, became a "dark horse"; the slides show
posts celebrating its 40-year-old goalkeeper and a clean sheet against Spain that won
the team its first World Cup point. Was it luck or strength?
== Question 1: without the external conditions? ==
The tournament was expanded to 48 teams, which gave smaller football nations more
places, and Cape Verde could draw on many players who grew up or play in European
leagues. Without these **external causes**, it could hardly have reached the World Cup
stage: external causes are conditions that can speed development up.
== Question 2: why only Cape Verde? ==
Many African countries have plenty of players in Europe, yet few did as well. The
difference is the **internal cause**: the team's cohesion, its worked-out tactics, its
goalkeeper's form. The same external conditions worked through different internal
causes, with different results.
== Conclusion ==
Both are indispensable, the internal cause is the basis and decides the direction, and
external causes work through internal ones. Luck alone (external) explains nothing
without the strength to use it (internal).
:::

::: callout pitfall 内因 vs 外因
Two traps. "External causes are unimportant" is wrong: they are indispensable
conditions and can sometimes play a big role. "External causes decide" is wrong: they
always work **through** the internal cause, which decides the direction. Also, which is
internal depends on what you take as "the thing": for a single player the team's
tactics are external; for the team they are internal.
:::

## Necessity and contingency

::: define Necessity and contingency | 必然与偶然
**Necessity** is the tendency in the connection and development of things that is
certain and cannot be otherwise (确定不移的趋势). **Contingency** (chance) is the
tendency that is uncertain, that may or may not appear, in this way or that (不确定的
趋势).
:::

**How they are related.**

- **Opposed**: necessity comes from causes in the thing's inner essence and, under given
  conditions, cannot be avoided; contingency comes from non-essential causes.
- **Unified**: they depend on each other. **Necessity always shows itself through
  contingency, and behind contingency there is always necessity at work** (必然总是通过
  偶然表现出来，偶然背后总有必然在起作用); under certain conditions they turn into each
  other.

**Methodology.** Pay attention to the necessary laws and trends of development and base
goals and plans on them; and fully estimate the role of contingent factors, recognising
and seizing opportunities sharply, so as to reach the intended goal in practice.

::: example Tu Youyou and artemisinin
Searching for an antimalarial drug, Tu Youyou (屠呦呦) found, in what looked like a
chance trial, that an extract of sweet wormwood (青蒿) inhibited malaria in 68% of
cases. Following that lead, her team discovered artemisinin, which has saved millions
of lives (she shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine).
== The contingency ==
One particular trial, with one particular extract, gave the breakthrough.
== The necessity behind it ==
The team had collected more than 2,000 traditional Chinese recipes, persisted through
more than 190 failed experiments, and tested the drug on themselves first. With that
much systematic work, a breakthrough was bound to come somewhere.
== The lesson ==
Behind a lucky success there is a necessity; and necessity makes its way through a
particular chance event. Prepare systematically (necessity), and be alert to seize the
chance when it appears (contingency).
:::

## Reality and possibility

::: define Reality and possibility | 现实与可能
**Reality** is the sum of things that actually exist and are connected with one another
(相互联系着的实际存在的事物的综合). **Possibility** is the various tendencies contained
in things that point to how they may develop (包含在事物中、预示事物发展前途的种种
趋势).
:::

**How they are related.**

- **Opposed**: reality is what exists objectively now and marks a thing's present
  state; possibility is a latent tendency and marks the direction of its development.
- **Unified**: development is the process in which reality and possibility turn into
  each other. Reality holds possible directions of development; a possibility holds
  the grounds for becoming reality.

**Methodology.** Stand on reality: analyse and judge both reality itself and the
possibilities it contains, comprehensively. And look to the long term: make use of
conscious activity, work to prevent bad possibilities from becoming real, and actively
create conditions for good possibilities to be realised.

The slide's case: after the defeat of the Great Revolution (1927), the failure of the
Nanchang Uprising and the setbacks of the Autumn Harvest Uprising, many asked "how long
can the red flag keep flying?"; some despaired, some left. In January 1930 Mao answered
with the letter later titled *A Single Spark Can Start a Prairie Fire*
(《星星之火，可以燎原》): the reality was weak, but it held a real possibility of victory,
which became reality in 1949.

::: example Unable, or unwilling?
A 2007 postgraduate-entrance question, from *Mencius* (《孟子·梁惠王上》): "To carry Mount
Tai under your arm and leap over the North Sea, and say 'I can't', is truly being
unable. To break off a twig for an elder (为长者折枝), and say 'I can't', is not being
unable but being unwilling." The passage tells us to distinguish possibility from
impossibility. What is the difference between them?
== The two cases ==
Leaping over the sea with a mountain: nothing in reality makes it possible. Breaking a
twig: everything needed is there; it just isn't done.
== The difference ==
A possibility has **grounds and conditions in reality**; an impossibility has none
(answer C). How *sufficient* the grounds and conditions are distinguishes a real
possibility from an abstract one, which is a different question (option D). Effort and
whether it benefits us don't decide possibility at all.
:::

## The five pairs compared

The lecture ended the categories with a comparison:

**What they have in common.** All five pairs:

- are concrete unfoldings and deepenings of the contradictory relations of objective
  things;
- are universal forms in which the law of the unity of opposites shows itself;
- are products of human knowledge deepening as practice develops;
- are important tools of knowledge: logical forms we cannot do without in knowing and
  changing the world.

**How they differ.**

| Pair | What it captures | Definitions | Methodology in brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Content and form (内容与形式) | The **structural** relation of a thing's connections | Sum of elements; the structure or way of expressing them | Stress content, oppose formalism; change outdated forms |
| Essence and appearance (本质与现象) | The relation of **inside and outside** | Inner connections and fundamental nature; outer connections and surface features | See the essence through the appearance; 去粗取精、去伪存真 |
| Cause and effect (原因与结果) | The **logical basis** of motion and development | What brings a phenomenon about; what is brought about | Grasp causal links; remove unfavourable causes |
| Necessity and contingency (必然与偶然) | The **form in which** development is realised | Certain tendency; uncertain tendency | Plan by necessity; seize chances |
| Reality and possibility (现实与可能) | The **basic trend** of development | What actually exists; tendencies pointing to the future | Stand on reality, look to the long term; prevent bad, realise good possibilities |

**How they are related.** The pairs, and the categories within each pair, are all
connected with and permeate one another; together they sum up and reflect the
universal essence of objective things.

::: callout exam How the categories are examined
- **Matching sayings to pairs**: 买椟还珠 (content and form), 透过现象看本质 / 九方皋相马
  (essence and appearance), 种瓜得瓜 / 千里之堤溃于蚁穴 (cause and effect), 偶然中有必然
  (necessity and contingency), 星星之火可以燎原 / 挟泰山以超北海 (reality and possibility).
- **The relations**: for each pair, "opposed in … ; unified in … ; under certain
  conditions they turn into each other" (cause–effect, necessity–contingency,
  reality–possibility). Know which side **determines** (content determines form; essence
  determines appearance).
- **False appearance** is a trap: it is objective, part of the thing, not an error of
  perception.
:::

## The essence of materialist dialectics

### Critical and revolutionary

Materialist dialectics gives people the **fundamental method** for knowing and changing
the world. Its laws and categories reveal the universal laws of universal connection
and eternal development, so it is both a scientific **worldview** and our basic
**methodology**. And in its essence it is **critical and revolutionary**: as Marx put
it (Lesson 3.5), it understands every existing thing together with its inevitable
passing away, and "lets nothing impose on it". The course quotes Xi Jinping's
*Dialectical Materialism Is the Worldview and Methodology of Chinese Communists*
(《辩证唯物主义是中国共产党人的世界观和方法论》), which lists the pairs that work must
handle: appearance and essence, form and content, cause and effect, contingency and
necessity, possibility and reality, **internal and external causes**, generality and
particularity.

### Objective and subjective dialectics

::: define Objective and subjective dialectics | 客观辩证法与主观辩证法
**Objective dialectics** is the connection and development of the material world itself:
its motion, change and development in the form of interaction and interconnection.
**Subjective dialectics** is dialectical thinking: the reflection of the material
world's connection and development in people's minds, the scientific system of
dialectics.

> 客观辩证法：物质世界本身的联系和发展。主观辩证法：即辩证的思维，是物质世界的联系发展在
> 人们头脑中的反映。
:::

Their relation is **reflecting and reflected** (反映与被反映的关系): objective dialectics
comes first and subjective dialectics reflects it. Marx and Engels showed that
materialist dialectics is the **unity of objective and subjective dialectics**, and the
slide's diagram puts **practice** (实践) in the middle as what joins them: through
practice, thought is tested against the dialectics of the world and brought into line
with it. (Practice is the subject of Unit 4.)

::: callout pitfall Hegel had them the other way round
For Hegel, the dialectics of concepts came first and the world's dialectics was its
outward expression (Lesson 3.1). Materialist dialectics reverses that: the world's
dialectics is primary, thinking's dialectics is its reflection.
:::

### A scientific method of knowing

As modern science studied nature and society more widely and deeply, its research grew
more complex and integrated, and a large family of **modern scientific thinking
methods** formed:

| Method | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Control method (控制方法) | Analyses and studies the distribution of data to reveal regularities and find differences |
| Information method (信息方法) | Analyses the flow of information to understand how complex systems move |
| Systems method (系统方法) | Uses an understanding of systems to guide research on scientific and technical problems |
| Model method (模型方法) | Builds models that reveal the form, features and essence of the original in simplified, idealised form |
| Idealisation method (理想化方法) | Studies a simplified, easier-to-handle substitute in place of the object itself |

Dialectical thinking methods are the **foundation and principle** of modern scientific
thinking methods; modern scientific methods **deepen and unfold** dialectical ones. On
one side, dialectical thinking is the methodological premise of modern scientific
thinking, whose spirit runs through it, so scientific methods should take it
consciously as their guide. On the other, scientific methods enrich dialectical ones:
dialectics looks at connection and development as a whole, at the relation of people
and the world; scientific methods, taking connection and development as given, study
particular relations in depth, and dialectics should learn from them. The two should be
combined.

## Using dialectical thinking methods

::: define Dialectical thinking method | 辩证思维方法
Objective dialectics made concrete in the process of thinking: starting from the
dialectical nature of objective things, unfolding the inner contradictions of concepts,
using the logical forms of judgement and inference, it builds a complete theoretical
system that finally reveals the essence and inner laws of things. It is the scientific
method for knowing and changing the world correctly.
:::

There are four pairs. Each pair is itself a unity of opposites.

::: define Induction and deduction | 归纳与演绎
**Induction** draws a general conclusion from individual facts; **deduction** derives an
individual conclusion from general principles.
:::

Iron conducts electricity, aluminium conducts, lead conducts… so metals conduct
electricity: induction. All humans are mortal; Socrates is a human; so Socrates is
mortal: deduction. They **presuppose each other**: induction is the basis of deduction,
and deduction gives induction its theoretical grounds, aim and direction. They are also
**different and complementary**: induction can never cover everything and must be
corrected by deduction; deduction can't reveal the many sides of an individual thing
and must be supplemented by induction. They concern the relation of **the individual and
the general**, an external relation of things and concepts.

::: define Analysis and synthesis | 分析与综合
**Analysis** breaks the object of knowledge down in thought into its parts, aspects and
elements and studies each separately; **synthesis** combines the parts and elements in
thought, according to their inner connections, into an organic whole and studies that.
:::

A biologist analyses a cell into membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus. Mao's *Analysis of
the Classes in Chinese Society* (《中国社会各阶级的分析》, 1925) studied each class (the
landlords and compradors, the middle class, the petty bourgeoisie, the
semi-proletariat, the proletariat) and put them together into a picture of Chinese
society as a whole, to answer "who are our enemies, who are our friends". In essence
both are the method of **contradiction analysis based on investigation**: analysis is
the basis of synthesis, synthesis the completion of analysis. They concern the relation
of **whole and part**, an internal relation of things and concepts.

::: define Abstract and concrete | 抽象与具体
The **abstract** takes out, in thought, one essential property of an object and sets the
others aside for the moment; the **concrete** (in thought) combines the object's many
determinations, on the basis of the abstractions, into a unified whole.
:::

Knowing a thing's essence starts from the **sensory concrete** (感性具体): apples, pears,
strawberries as you see and taste them. Analysis draws out abstract determinations:
sweet and sour, rich in vitamins, colourful, juicy. Synthesis puts them together into
the **concrete in thought** (思维具体): the concept "fruit", rich with all those
determinations. This "concrete → abstract → concrete" is itself a negation of the
negation (Lesson 3.5). Abstract and concrete, with analysis and synthesis, are the
**higher forms** of dialectical thinking.

::: define Logic and history | 逻辑与历史
**Logic** is the movement of concepts from abstract to concrete, and the order and
relations of logical categories. **History** is the history of objective reality itself
and the history of our knowledge of it.
:::

The historical is the basis of the logical; the logical is the historical reproduced in
thought; so logic must agree with history. But grasping history also needs logic: only
reproducing history's development with logical necessity reveals its inner laws. The
**unity of logic and history** is the inner requirement of rising from the abstract to
the concrete, and the fundamental principle of forming scientific theory on the basis of
practice. The slide's example: writing on the Second Opium War (1856–1860), Marx and
Engels started from the history of capitalism and showed that the war was the necessary
result of the bourgeoisie's attempt to escape the dead end of capitalist production by
forcing open new markets.

| Pair | The relation it handles | Example on the slides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Induction and deduction | Individual and general (external) | Metals conduct; Socrates is mortal |
| Analysis and synthesis | Part and whole (internal) | The cell; Mao's class analysis |
| Abstract and concrete | Many determinations and their unity | Apple, pear → "fruit" |
| Logic and history | Thought's order and history's order | Marx and Engels on the Opium War |

## Strengthening our thinking abilities

Xi Jinping, visiting China University of Political Science and Law on 3 May 2017, told
students that youth is the key time to cultivate scientific ways of thinking, joining
study, observation and practice with thought. The course lists **seven thinking
abilities** (七大思维能力), which it presents as new forms of dialectical thinking that
the Communist Party of China developed by applying it to governance, and so, in the
course's account, its enrichment of materialist dialectics.

| Ability | Its place in materialist dialectics |
| --- | --- |
| Dialectical thinking (辩证思维能力) | The fundamental requirement and concentrated expression of scientific thinking |
| Historical thinking (历史思维能力) | Dialectical thinking combined with a historical view |
| Systems thinking (系统思维能力) | What the view of universal connection implies |
| Strategic thinking (战略思维能力) | Grasping the overall trend and direction in eternal development |
| Bottom-line thinking (底线思维能力) | Grasping the measure at which quantity turns into quality, and using contradiction analysis |
| Innovative thinking (创新思维能力) | Required by the critical and revolutionary essence of materialist dialectics |
| Thinking in terms of the rule of law (法治思维能力) | Grasping dialectically the relation of social rules and human freedom |

### Dialectical thinking

::: define Dialectical thinking ability | 辩证思维能力
The ability, guided by materialist dialectics, to discover, analyse and resolve
contradictions, grasp essences, follow laws and push work forward.

> 辩证思维能力是以唯物辩证法为指导，发现矛盾、分析矛盾、解决矛盾，把握本质、遵循规律、
> 推动工作的能力。
:::

It requires analysing and solving problems with the critical and revolutionary spirit,
with the view of connection and development, with the basic laws and categories, and
with dialectical thinking methods. In observing things, look **objectively, not
subjectively; in connection, not in isolation; in development, not statically;
comprehensively, not one-sidedly; systematically, not piecemeal** — and so grasp
essences and laws and find ways to solve problems. Its concentrated expression is the
ability to analyse and resolve contradictions: grasp unity within opposition and
opposition within unity, and overcome extremes and one-sidedness.

At its centre is the **method of contradiction analysis** (矛盾分析方法), the law of the
unity of opposites as method (Lesson 3.3), at the core of the whole methodology of
materialist dialectics: first admit that contradictions are universal; centrally,
grasp their particularity, analysing each concrete contradiction concretely; and grasp
the principal contradiction and principal aspect while seeing the secondary ones, the
unity of the "two-point" and "key-point" views. It asks us to sharpen our sense of
problems and, above all, to make solving the principal contradiction the breakthrough
that brings the others along.

::: example Growth or environment?
The slide's question: the traditional view treats economic growth and environmental
protection as a choice of one, with growth necessarily paid for by the environment. As a
policy-maker, how would you choose?
== See the opposition ==
In the short run, they do conflict: polluting industry grows output; protection costs
money and closes factories.
== See the unity ==
Their **purpose** is the same, meeting people's desire for a better life; and their
**content** is unified: a good ecology has great economic value in itself, and
protecting the environment makes development sustainable. "Lucid waters and lush
mountains are invaluable assets" (绿水青山就是金山银山) sums up the relation.
== Policy ==
The course points to how this became policy: the 18th Party Congress (2012) put
ecological civilisation into the "five-sphere integrated plan"; the 19th (2017) made
"harmony between humanity and nature" a basic policy; the 20th (2022) named it one of
the Chinese features of Chinese modernisation.
== The method ==
Refuse the either-or of metaphysics; find the unity within the opposition and the
conditions that turn conflict into mutual support.
:::

### Historical thinking

::: define Historical thinking ability | 历史思维能力
The combination of dialectical thinking with a historical view: the ability to learn
from history, knowing the past to understand the present, and to use a historical view
to understand laws of development, grasp the direction of progress and guide present
work. It is a concrete application of the Marxist view of history.
:::

The slide's example: in *On Protracted War* (《论持久战》, 1938) Mao examined the
Sino-Japanese war through how it arose and developed, correctly judged its nature, laws
and trend, foresaw its course (a long war in stages, ending in China's victory) and
guided the resistance. Strengthening it means grasping historical laws, recognising
historical trends, summing up historical experience, holding a correct view of Party
history and a "grand view of history", and carrying forward the spirit of historical
initiative; concretely, studying the history of the Party, of New China, of reform and
opening up and of socialism, and the long history of Chinese civilisation.

### Systems thinking

::: define Systems thinking ability | 系统思维能力
The ability to think systematically about the interconnected aspects of a thing and
its structure and function: to analyse and handle problems comprehensively and as a
whole.
:::

Since everything universally connected is itself a system (with the four features of
wholeness, structure, levels and openness, Lesson 3.2), things must be grasped within the
system of universal connections, as wholes, optimising structure and function. Pay
attention to the relations between systems, between elements, between structure and
levels, and between system and environment; handle the whole and the part, the present
and the long term; plan the whole with systems methods.

The slide's case is the **Tiangong space station**: its core module Tianhe was launched
from Wenchang, Hainan, on 29 April 2021, and the design is "1 (Tianhe) + 1 (Wentian) + 1
(Mengtian) = 1 (Tiangong)": three modules built as one combined core, so that every
module and visiting craft acts in coordination and resources are used and optimised as
a whole.

### Strategic thinking

::: define Strategic thinking ability | 战略思维能力
The ability to look far ahead, take in the whole situation, and grasp the overall trend
and direction of development; it stresses thinking that is integral, overall and
long-term.
:::

It matters because it makes full use of people's conscious activity and concerns the
future direction and overall planning of society. To strengthen it: stand at the
forefront of the times and the height of the overall strategy; grasp essences and laws
through appearances; seize the key points while taking everything into account; stand
on the present while looking to the long term; know the national situation and the
world situation. Handle the part and the whole, the present and the long term, the key
and the non-key, and choose the most favourable strategy by weighing advantages and
disadvantages. The slide's case is the **Belt and Road Initiative**: Xi Jinping proposed
the Silk Road Economic Belt at Nazarbayev University in Kazakhstan on 7 September 2013
and the 21st-Century Maritime Silk Road in Indonesia's parliament on 3 October 2013; by
October 2021 China had signed 206 cooperation documents with 140 countries and 32
international organisations.

### Bottom-line thinking

Defined in Lesson 3.4: setting a minimum goal objectively and striving from that lowest
point for the best outcome, where the bottom line is the critical point of a thing's
qualitative change. Strengthening it is also an important guarantee against trouble
before it happens and a key to preventing and resisting risks. It requires holding to
principles and drawing and keeping bottom lines; studying risks and preventing them in
advance, so as to keep the strategic initiative; and a strong sense of potential danger
and of foresight. In short: be prepared for danger in times of peace, prepare for the
worst while striving for the best (凡事从最坏处着眼、向最好处努力), and fight only battles
you are prepared for. The slides' case is farmland: China has a large population and
little land, so Xi Jinping called for the strictest farmland protection, to "protect
farmland as we protect giant pandas", with the 1.8-billion-*mu* red line.

### Innovative thinking

::: define Innovative thinking ability | 创新思维能力
The ability to break through routine thinking: to break with superstition and outdated
rules, adapt to local conditions, press on in the face of difficulty and open up new
ground.
:::

In a knowledge economy, innovation has become the leading force and main source of
social progress. Strengthening it means breaking the inertia of blind faith in
experience, in books and in authority; getting rid of conservatism, rigid thinking,
formalism and inaction; and daring to open new directions. It requires the boldness
to be first, dropping outdated ideas, and carrying out the new development philosophy,
whose first element is innovation.

### Thinking in terms of the rule of law

::: define Rule-of-law thinking ability | 法治思维能力
The ability to understand things, judge right and wrong and solve problems on the basis
of the inherent features of the rule of law and belief in it.
:::

"When the rule of law flourishes, the country flourishes." Strengthening it means
believing in the law and living its spirit, learning, understanding, obeying and using
the law, and valuing the force of morality alongside it (法安天下、德润人心): thinking,
deciding and acting **within** the law, and using legal means to deepen reform, promote
development, resolve conflicts and keep stability.

::: callout exam Thinking abilities in multiple choice
Questions give a policy and ask which way of thinking it shows. "Big agriculture, big
food view: food from land and sea, plants, animals and microbes" is **systems**
thinking (the whole food supply as one system). "Prepare for the worst" and "red lines"
are **bottom-line** thinking. "Look far ahead, take the whole into account" is
**strategic**; "learn from history" is **historical**; "break with convention" is
**innovative**.
:::

## A closing question

The lecture ended with a 辨析题 that brings back a principle from Lesson 2.3.

::: example 辨析: "Act according to laws, and success will surely follow"
Judge the statement 按照规律办事，成功必然追随 and explain (10 points).
== Judge ==
**Wrong** (not agreed).
== The principle ==
Subjective initiative and objective laws are a dialectical unity (Lesson 2.3). On the
one hand, respecting objective laws is the precondition for using our initiative
correctly; on the other, only by fully using our initiative can we know and use
objective laws correctly.
== The conditions ==
Using initiative correctly needs three things: starting from reality; practice, its
fundamental path; and the necessary material conditions and means. Its methodology is
to carry forward the spirit of historical initiative. Following laws is necessary, but
not sufficient: without effort, practice and means, nothing happens.
== Model answer ==
错误（不赞成）。主观能动性和客观规律性是辩证统一的。一方面，尊重客观规律是正确发挥主观能动性
的前提；另一方面，只有充分发挥主观能动性，才能正确认识和利用客观规律。正确发挥人的主观能动性
有三个方面的前提和条件：第一，从实际出发是正确发挥人的主观能动性的前提；第二，实践是正确发挥
人的主观能动性的根本途径；第三，正确发挥人的主观能动性，还要依赖于一定的物质条件和物质手段。
要充分发挥历史主动精神。因此，在按照规律办事的前提下，要充分发挥人的主观能动性，才能有成功的
可能。
:::

## Lecture summary

The lecture's closing map has three branches:

- **Master the basic categories**: content and form; essence and appearance; cause and
  effect; necessity and contingency; reality and possibility.
- **Learn to use dialectical thinking methods**: induction and deduction; analysis and
  synthesis; abstract and concrete; logic and history.
- **Keep strengthening thinking abilities**: dialectical, historical, systems,
  strategic, bottom-line, innovative and rule-of-law thinking.

::: callout key The one thing to remember
Every pair of categories is a **unity of opposites**: opposed, unified, and (for cause
and effect, necessity and contingency, reality and possibility) turning into each other
under conditions; and each comes with its methodology. Exams want all three parts.
:::

::: cards Categories and dialectical thinking
Category (范畴), in Lenin's image :: The knots in the net of natural phenomena; stages in knowing the world (*Conspectus of Hegel's "Science of Logic"*).
The five pairs of basic categories :: Content and form; essence and appearance; cause and effect; necessity and contingency; reality and possibility.
Content and form: relation and methodology :: Content determines form, form reacts back; form is relatively independent. Stress content, oppose formalism, replace outdated forms.
Essence and appearance: relation and methodology :: Essence determines appearance, appearance expresses essence (true appearances directly, false ones in reverse). See the essence through appearances; 去粗取精、去伪存真.
Cause and effect: relation and methodology :: Cause before effect in one connection; they depend on each other and turn into each other. Grasp causal links; remove unfavourable causes.
Internal and external causes (内因与外因) :: Internal: the thing's inner contradiction, the basis, decides the direction. External: contradiction with other things, the condition, speeds up or slows down. External causes work through internal ones.
Necessity and contingency: relation :: Necessity shows itself through contingency; behind contingency there is necessity; they turn into each other.
Reality and possibility: methodology :: Stand on reality, look to the long term; prevent bad possibilities, create conditions for good ones.
Possibility vs impossibility :: A possibility has grounds and conditions in reality; an impossibility has none.
Objective and subjective dialectics :: The dialectics of the material world, and its reflection in thinking; united through practice; relation of reflected and reflecting.
The essence of materialist dialectics :: Critical and revolutionary (Marx, afterword to *Capital*, vol. 1).
The four pairs of dialectical thinking methods :: Induction and deduction; analysis and synthesis; abstract and concrete; logic and history.
The road from sensory concrete to concrete in thought :: Sensory concrete → abstract determinations → concrete in thought (具体—抽象—具体).
The seven thinking abilities :: Dialectical, historical, systems, strategic, bottom-line, innovative, rule-of-law.
The method of contradiction analysis in dialectical thinking :: Admit universality; analyse each concrete contradiction concretely; unite the two-point and key-point views.
:::
