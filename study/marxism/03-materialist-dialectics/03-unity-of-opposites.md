Lesson 3.2 said that things are connected and that they develop. This lesson asks
**why** they develop: what pushes a seed to become a plant, a society to change, a
student to learn. The course's answer is **contradiction**: every thing holds opposite
sides that both depend on each other and fight each other, and that tension moves it.
This is the law of the unity of opposites, which the course calls the **essence and
core** of materialist dialectics. It is the most examined part of the unit: the
definitions of identity and struggle, which is absolute and which relative, the
"two points and a focus" method, and the error in "a white horse is not a horse" all
come up again and again.

## Laws, and the three basic laws

Connection and development are not chaos. Things are connected in regular ways and
develop in regular ways; so the overall view of materialist dialectics (things are
connected, things develop) leads to the claim that **connection and development follow
laws**, and so to the basic laws of materialist dialectics. Engels: "辩证法不过是关于自然界、人类社会和思维的运动和发展的普遍规律的科学"
(dialectics is nothing more than the science of the general laws of motion and
development of nature, human society and thought; *Anti-Dühring*).

::: define Law | 规律
The inherent, essential, necessary and stable connection within the development of
things.

> 规律是事物发展中本身所固有的、本质的、必然的、稳定的联系。
:::

Each word matters. *Inherent*: a law belongs to things, it isn't imposed by us.
*Essential* and *necessary*: it is the deep connection, not a surface coincidence.
*Stable*: it repeats wherever its conditions hold. The ancient philosopher Xunzi said
the same about nature: "天行有常，不为尧存，不为桀亡。应之以治则吉，应之以乱则凶"
(Heaven's course is constant: it does not exist for the sage-king Yao, nor perish for
the tyrant Jie. Respond to it with order and good fortune follows; respond with
disorder and misfortune follows; *Xunzi*, "On Heaven"). Laws are objective, and we do
well by following them. Engels adds that the same dialectical laws that govern
seemingly accidental events in history work through countless changes in nature, and
run through the history of human thought, where people gradually become aware of them
(*Anti-Dühring*).

Engels also counted the laws. In *Dialectics of Nature* he wrote that the laws of
dialectics are abstracted from the history of nature and of human society, and "can be
reduced in the main to three": the law of the transformation of quantity into quality
and vice versa; the law of the interpenetration of opposites; and the law of the
negation of the negation.

| Law | What it reveals | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unity of opposites (对立统一规律) | The **source and motive force** of development (源泉和动力) | This lesson |
| Quantitative and qualitative change (量变质变规律) | The **form and state** of development (形式和状态) | Lesson 3.4 |
| Negation of the negation (否定之否定规律) | The **direction and path** of development (方向和道路) | Lesson 3.5 |

### Why this law is the core

The slides give three reasons why the law of the unity of opposites is **the essence
and core** (实质和核心) of materialist dialectics:

1. it reveals the **fundamental content** of universal connection and the **inner
   motive force** of change and development;
2. it is **the key** (一把钥匙) to understanding the other laws and categories of
   materialist dialectics;
3. it gives the **fundamental method** of knowing and changing the world:
   **contradiction analysis** (矛盾分析法).

Lenin put it in two sentences. "统一物之分为两个互相排斥的对立面以及它们之间的相互关系……是辩证法的实质"
(the splitting of a single whole into two mutually exclusive opposites and their
mutual relation is the essence of dialectics; *On the Question of Dialectics*,
《谈谈辩证法问题》). And: "可以把辩证法简要地规定为关于对立面的统一的学说。这样就会抓住辩证法的核心"
(dialectics can be briefly defined as the doctrine of the unity of opposites; this
grasps its core; "Summary of Dialectics", 《辩证法的要素》, in his notes on Hegel's
*Logic*).

## What contradiction is

The Chinese word 矛盾 comes from an old story (自相矛盾, in *Han Feizi*). A man selling a
spear and a shield boasted that his spear could pierce anything and that his shield
could stop anything. Someone asked: what happens if your spear strikes your shield? He
had no answer: the two claims can't both be true.

That is a **logical** contradiction, and dialectics does not deny the rule against it.
The course separates two meanings:

| Kind | What it says | Formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The law of contradiction in **formal logic** (形式逻辑的矛盾律) | In one argument, a statement and its denial can't both be true; thinking must not contradict itself | "Either–or" (非此即彼) |
| The law of contradiction in **dialectics** (辩证法的矛盾规律) | Real things contain opposite sides that both exclude and depend on each other | "Both–and" (亦此亦彼) |

Engels: dialectical thinking "doesn't recognise hard and fixed lines, the
unconditional, universally valid 'either–or!'; it lets the fixed metaphysical
differences pass into each other, and besides 'either–or!' recognises, in the right
place, 'both this and that!', and puts the opposites in relation" (*Dialectics of
Nature*).

::: callout pitfall Logical contradiction is not dialectical contradiction
"The spear can pierce everything and the shield can't be pierced" is a
**logical** contradiction: a mistake in thinking, to be removed. "Life contains death",
"production and consumption need each other" are **dialectical** contradictions: real
features of things. An exam option that says "dialectics allows self-contradictory
statements" is wrong. Dialectics and formal logic apply at different levels.
:::

::: define Contradiction | 矛盾
The philosophical category that reflects the relation of **unity of opposites** within
things and between things. In short, contradiction is the unity of opposites.

> 矛盾是反映事物内部和事物之间对立统一关系的哲学范畴。
:::

Chinese thinkers long saw this: 凡物皆有两端 (everything has two ends), 无独必有对
(nothing stands alone, everything has its opposite), 物生有两 (things are born in
pairs), 一分为二，合二而一 (one divides into two, two combine into one). Mao Zedong
applied it to history: "the changes in nature are mainly due to the development of the
contradictions inside nature; the changes in society are mainly due to the development
of the contradictions inside society: between the productive forces and the relations
of production, between classes, between the new and the old" (*On Contradiction*,
《矛盾论》, 1937).

Every contradiction has two **basic attributes** (基本属性): a **unifying** attribute,
called **identity** (同一性), and an **opposing** attribute, called **struggle**
(斗争性). Mao lists pairs where each side exists only with the other: "没有生，死就不见；没有死，生也不见。没有上，无所谓下；没有下，也无所谓上……没有资产阶级，就没有无产阶级；没有无产阶级，也就没有资产阶级。"

## Identity

::: define Identity of contradiction | 矛盾的同一性
The property and tendency of the opposite sides of a contradiction to **depend on each
other** and to **interpenetrate** (connect through to each other).

> 矛盾的同一性是指矛盾着的对立面相互依存、相互贯通的性质和趋势。
:::

Identity has two meanings:

1. **Mutual dependence**: each side is the condition of the other's existence
   (互为存在的前提). The *Daodejing* (ch. 2): "有无相生，难易相成，长短相形，高下相盈，音声相和，前后相随"
   (being and non-being produce each other, hard and easy complete each other, long and
   short shape each other, high and low lean on each other…). There is no "difficult"
   without "easy".
2. **Interpenetration**: the sides connect through to each other, and **under certain
   conditions can turn into each other** (在一定条件下可以相互转化). The *Daodejing*
   (ch. 58): "祸兮福之所倚，福兮祸之所伏" (misfortune is what good fortune leans on;
   good fortune is where misfortune hides).

::: example The old man of the frontier loses his horse (塞翁失马)
An old man living near the frontier lost his horse; it ran off to the nomads' land.
Neighbours came to console him. He said: "Who knows if this isn't good fortune?" Months
later the horse came back leading a fine nomad horse. Neighbours congratulated him. He
said: "Who knows if this isn't misfortune?" His son, fond of riding, fell from the new
horse and broke his leg. Neighbours consoled him. "Who knows if this isn't good
fortune?" A year later the nomads invaded; the able-bodied men were all called up, and
nine in ten died. The son, lame, was spared, and father and son survived.

== Which side of identity ==
The second: the opposites (fortune 福 and misfortune 祸) interpenetrate and, under
certain conditions, turn into each other.

== The conditions ==
Each turn has its condition: a horse that returns with a companion, a fall, a war.
Without the condition there is no transformation. Fortune doesn't become misfortune by
itself, "just because".

== The lesson ==
Don't treat good or bad luck as fixed. The slide credits the story to 《塞翁失马》; it
comes from the Han-dynasty *Huainanzi* (《淮南子·人间训》).
:::

The slides then ask: **does a concrete unity of opposites exist forever?** Answer:
**No, because of conditions.** Any particular pair of opposites holds together only
under definite conditions; when they change, that unity breaks up and a new one forms.

::: example Think it through: "三人行，必有我师焉"
The teacher's question: do concrete unities of contradiction exist forever? Take
Confucius' saying "三人行，必有我师焉" ("when three people walk together, one of them
is sure to be my teacher", *Analects*, 《论语·述而》).

== The contradiction ==
Teacher and student form a unity of opposites: each is a teacher only in relation to a
student, and the other way round (mutual dependence). They are also opposed: one knows,
the other doesn't yet.

== Identity is conditional ==
Who is the teacher depends on conditions: what is being learned, who knows more about
it. Among three people walking together, one is better at this, another at that; the
person I learn from today may learn from me tomorrow. As Han Yu (韩愈) put it in *On
Teachers* (《师说》), "弟子不必不如师，师不必贤于弟子".

== Transformation ==
When the conditions change (the student masters the subject, the topic changes), the
old teacher–student unity dissolves and the sides change places or form a new unity.
No particular unity of opposites is permanent; its identity is relative.

== Model answer ==
具体的矛盾统一体不会永远存在。矛盾的同一性是有条件的、相对的，矛盾双方在一定条件下相互依存，并在一定条件下相互转化。"三人行，必有我师焉"中，"师"与"生"是相互依存的矛盾双方，谁为师、谁为生取决于具体条件（所学内容、各自所长）；条件变化了，原有的师生统一体就会解体，双方可以相互转化，形成新的统一体。所以具体的矛盾统一体是暂时的、有条件的，我们要虚心向他人学习，用发展的眼光看待师生关系。
:::

## Struggle

::: define Struggle of contradiction | 矛盾的斗争性
The property and tendency of the opposite sides of a contradiction to **exclude each
other** and **pull apart** (separate from each other).

> 矛盾的斗争性是指矛盾着的对立面相互排斥、相互分离的性质和趋势。
:::

"Struggle" here is a philosophical word, wider than fighting: it covers the attraction
and repulsion of forces, the absorption and excretion of a cell, the rivalry of ideas,
competition in a market, and class struggle. It has **the widest range of forms**, in
nature, society and thought. Lenin: to know all the processes of the world in their
"self-movement", their spontaneous development and their living life, we must know
them **as a unity of opposites** (*On the Question of Dialectics*).

The form of struggle depends on the contradiction. The slides distinguish:

::: define Antagonistic and non-antagonistic contradictions | 对抗性矛盾与非对抗性矛盾
An **antagonistic** contradiction rests on a **fundamental conflict of interests**
between the two sides; it is resolved by external confrontation. A
**non-antagonistic** contradiction rests on **fundamentally shared interests**; it is
resolved by consultation and discussion, criticism and self-criticism.
:::

The slide's pictures: the **Paris Commune**, proclaimed on 28 March 1871 (an
antagonistic contradiction between classes, settled by revolution and armed
suppression); a brawl at a football match, a couple quarrelling over a child, and the
two poles of a compass needle (struggles within shared ties, or opposites inside one
whole).

::: callout pitfall Struggle is not always violent
"Struggle" (斗争性) is the general property of opposites to exclude each other. Only
antagonistic contradictions take the form of confrontation. Most contradictions
(between colleagues, between production and consumption, inside a family) are
non-antagonistic and are settled peacefully.
:::

## How identity and struggle relate

**They are bound together** (相辅相成):

- Identity can't exist without struggle: identity **contains difference and
  opposition** (同一是包含着差别和对立的同一). Confucius: "君子和而不同，小人同而不和"
  (the gentleman harmonises without being the same; the petty man is the same without
  harmonising; *Analects*, 《论语·子路》).
- Struggle can't exist without identity: struggle always takes identity as its
  premise and basis, and **lies within identity** (斗争性寓于同一性之中). The slide's
  cartoon shows two figures clinging to the same rope under one balloon and asks: "Does
  he dare to cut it?" Whatever they are fighting over, they hang from one lifeline;
  their struggle happens inside their dependence on each other.

Mao: "我们中国人常说：'相反相成'……'相反'就是说两个矛盾方面的互相排斥，或互相斗争。'相成'就是说在一定条件之下两个矛盾方面互相联结起来，获得了同一性。而斗争性即寓于同一性之中，没有斗争性就没有同一性。"

**They also differ:**

| Point of comparison | Identity (同一性) | Struggle (斗争性) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Mutual dependence and interpenetration | Mutual exclusion and separation |
| Status | **Relative**, conditional (相对的，有条件的) | **Absolute**, unconditional (绝对的，无条件的) |
| Parallel | The relativity of rest | The absoluteness of motion |
| Role | Keeps a thing relatively stable; sets the possibility and trend of transformation | Changes the balance of forces; decides the passage to a new unity |

Why is identity conditional? Mao: "为什么鸡蛋能够转化为鸡子，而石头就不能转化为鸡子呢？为什么战争与和平有同一性，而战争与石头却没有同一性呢？……没有别的，就是因为矛盾的同一性要在一定的必要的条件之下。缺乏一定的必要的条件，就没有任何的同一性。"
Any **particular** unity of opposites lasts only while its conditions last. Struggle,
by contrast, runs through every unity, from start to end, and through the change from
one unity to the next: it never stops. This matches Lesson 2.2: motion is absolute,
rest relative.

::: callout pitfall "Absolute struggle" doesn't mean "struggle is everything"
"Struggle is absolute" means it is unconditional and never stops; it does **not** mean
struggle alone matters or that identity is unimportant. Both together make a
contradiction move. Equally, "identity is relative" doesn't mean identity is unreal.
A 辨析题 favourite: "矛盾的同一性是绝对的" or "只有斗争性推动发展" → both wrong.
:::

## Their roles in development

**Identity's three roles:**

1. **Mutual dependence** is the premise of a thing's existence and development: each
   side develops on condition that the other does, and this keeps the thing
   **relatively stable**. A living thing passes through birth, growth, flourishing,
   ageing and death while remaining itself. Engels: every plant, animal and cell is, at
   every moment, identical with itself and yet different from itself, through absorbing
   and excreting matter, breathing, the forming and dying of cells (*Dialectics of
   Nature*).
2. **Interpenetration** lets each side **absorb from the other what helps it**. The
   slides' case (not shown in class): in Yellowstone National Park the wolves were
   killed off to protect the deer (elk). With no predator the deer multiplied, ate the
   saplings, wore out the pasture and grew weak. When wolves were brought back (1995),
   they took the old, weak and sick deer, kept numbers down, the vegetation recovered
   and the herds became healthier. Wolf and deer are each other's condition of
   existence.
3. **Transformation**: identity **sets the possibility and trend** of a thing's
   transformation. Why does an egg hatch into a chick but a stone never does? Not
   just because of warmth: the egg has the inner contradiction that makes a chick
   possible; the stone has none. External conditions only act through that.

**Struggle's two roles:**

1. Struggle **shifts the balance of forces** between the sides, preparing the
   conditions for the opposites to transform and for qualitative change. The slides'
   pairs: slave-owners and slaves, landlords and peasants, bourgeoisie and proletariat.
2. Struggle is **the decisive force** in the passage from one unity of opposites to
   another: the course's sequence of slave society → feudal society → capitalist
   society → communist society.

Together: "有条件的相对的同一性和无条件的绝对的斗争性相结合，构成了一切事物的矛盾运动" (Mao, *On
Contradiction*). Identity and struggle combined make up the movement of contradiction,
which drives change and development.

::: law The relation of identity and struggle | 矛盾的同一性和斗争性辩证关系原理
Identity and struggle are the two basic attributes of every contradiction. They are
bound together: identity contains struggle, struggle lies within identity. They
differ: identity is relative and conditional, struggle absolute and unconditional.
Conditional, relative identity combined with unconditional, absolute struggle makes up
the movement of contradiction, which drives the development of things.
:::

**Methodological requirements:**

1. **Grasp identity within struggle** (在斗争性中把握同一性): understand correctly the
   role of **harmony** in development.
2. **Grasp struggle within identity** (在同一性中把握斗争性): vigorously carry forward
   the **spirit of struggle** (发扬斗争精神).

Xi Jinping: "对待矛盾的正确态度，应该是直面矛盾，并运用矛盾相辅相成的特性，在解决矛盾的过程中推动事物发展。"
(*Dialectical materialism is the worldview and methodology of Chinese Communists*.)

::: define Harmony | 和谐
A special form in which contradiction shows itself: it embodies the mutual dependence,
mutual promotion and common development of the two sides.

> 和谐是矛盾的一种特殊表现形式，体现着矛盾双方的相互依存、相互促进、共同发展。
:::

Harmony is not the absence of contradiction but a way contradictions coexist. The late
Western Zhou thinker Shi Bo (史伯): "和实生物，同则不继。以他平他谓之和，故能丰长而物归之"
(harmony produces things; mere sameness cannot continue. Balancing one thing with a
different one is called harmony, so things grow and flourish; *Discourses of the
States*, 《国语·郑语》).

::: example Think it through: Plants vs. Zombies
The teacher's question: use the dialectical relation of identity and struggle to
analyse the relation between plants and zombies in the game *Plants vs. Zombies*
(植物大战僵尸).

== Struggle ==
Plants and zombies exclude each other: zombies advance to reach the house, plants
shoot, block and blow them up. Every level is this struggle, and it never stops while
the game is played: struggle is absolute.

== Identity ==
They also depend on each other. Without zombies the plants have nothing to do, and
there is no game; without plants the zombies simply win and the game ends. Each side
exists as a side of the game only because the other does. Their unity, one level, is
the condition of their struggle: struggle lies within identity.

== Development ==
The struggle pushes both sides on: tougher zombies (with buckets, pole-vaulting,
digging) force the player to find new plants and new layouts, and the new defences
call out new zombies. Each side absorbs from the struggle what drives its own
development.

== Conditions ==
Their identity is conditional: when one side wins, that level's unity ends, and a new
level brings a new unity with new opponents. No particular unity lasts forever.

== Model answer ==
矛盾的同一性和斗争性相辅相成：同一性不能脱离斗争性而存在，斗争性寓于同一性之中；同一性是相对的、有条件的，斗争性是绝对的、无条件的，二者结合构成事物的矛盾运动，推动事物发展。游戏中，植物与僵尸相互排斥、不断交战，体现了斗争性；同时二者相互依存、互为前提，没有僵尸就没有植物的"防守"，没有植物也就没有僵尸的"进攻"，二者共处于一个游戏关卡之中，体现了同一性。双方在斗争中相互促进，不断出现新的植物和新的僵尸，推动游戏发展；当一方胜出，这一关的矛盾统一体就解体，被新的统一体代替。因此，我们既要在斗争性中把握同一性，又要在同一性中把握斗争性。
:::

## Universality and particularity

People have long dreamed of a world without contradictions: the "Great Unity" society
(大同) of the *Book of Rites*; Laozi's "small state with few people" (小国寡民);
Zhuangzi's "age of perfect virtue" (至德之世); the *Weiliaozi*'s "the world as one
family, sharing cold and hunger"; the *Lüshi Chunqiu*'s "perfect public-mindedness"
(至公); the *Six Secret Teachings*' "the world shared, the same benefit and joy for
all"; Tao Yuanming's Peach Blossom Spring (世外桃源); Thomas More's *Utopia* (1516);
Tommaso Campanella's *The City of the Sun* (written 1602). Can such a world exist? The
course's answer: no, because contradiction is universal.

::: define Universality of contradiction | 矛盾的普遍性
Contradiction exists in **all things** (事事有矛盾), and it exists **throughout the
whole process of every thing's development**, from beginning to end (时时有矛盾).
:::

So the useful attitude is not to wish contradictions away but to find and solve them:
contradiction analysis. Xi Jinping: "问题是事物矛盾的表现形式，我们强调增强问题意识、坚持问题导向，就是承认矛盾的普遍性、客观性，就是要善于把认识和化解矛盾作为打开工作局面的突破口。"
A problem is how a contradiction shows itself.

::: define Particularity of contradiction | 矛盾的特殊性
Each contradiction, and each side of it, has its own features:

1. the contradictions of **different things** each have their own features;
2. the same thing has different features **at different processes and stages** of its
   development;
3. the many contradictions that make up a thing, and the different sides of each
   contradiction, differ in **nature, position and role**.
:::

Mao: the contradictions "of each form of motion in each process of development, each
side of each process's contradiction, each process at each of its stages, and each side
of the contradiction at each stage" all have their particularity (*On Contradiction*).

The slides sort particularity into three kinds:

| Particularity of… | What differs | Pairs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature** (性质) | What kind of contradiction it is | Fundamental and non-fundamental contradictions |
| **Position** (地位) | How much it matters in the whole | Principal and secondary contradictions; principal and secondary aspects |
| **Way of resolution** (解决方法) | How the struggle ends | One side overcomes the other; both perish; the two fuse |

The **fundamental contradiction** (根本矛盾) runs through a thing's whole process and
decides its nature; the others are non-fundamental. When the struggle of a
contradiction ends and the two sides reach unity, it does so in one of three forms:

1. **One side overcomes the other**: the new defeats the old.
2. **Both sides perish together** and are replaced by a new pair: the contradiction of
   slaves and slave-owners is replaced by that of peasants and landlords.
3. **The two sides fuse** into a new thing: over long development, the contradiction
   between city and countryside merges step by step into urban–rural integration
   (城乡一体化).

::: callout note A slip on the slide
The slide writes the third example as "城乡与农村的矛盾"; it means **城市与农村**, the city
and the countryside.
:::

### Principal and secondary

::: define Principal and secondary contradictions | 主要矛盾与次要矛盾
In a complex thing with many contradictions, the **principal contradiction** is the one
that holds the dominant position and plays the decisive role in its development; the
others, in a subordinate position without a decisive role, are **secondary
contradictions**. Under certain conditions they can change places.

> 主要矛盾是在复杂事物发展过程中处于支配地位、对事物发展起决定作用的矛盾。
:::

::: define Principal and secondary aspects of a contradiction | 矛盾的主要方面与次要方面
Within one contradiction, the **principal aspect** holds the dominant position and
plays the leading role; the **secondary aspect** is subordinate. **The nature of a
thing is decided mainly by the principal aspect of its principal contradiction.**
:::

::: callout pitfall Contradiction vs aspect
"Principal contradiction" compares **several contradictions** in one thing; "principal
aspect" compares **the two sides of one contradiction**. Don't swap them. A student's
studies may be the principal contradiction of this term; within it, their own effort
(not the textbook) may be the principal aspect.
:::

The clearest case is **China's principal social contradiction**. The 1981 resolution
named it as the contradiction between the people's ever-growing **material and
cultural needs** and **backward social production**. The 19th National Congress
(18 October 2017) declared that with socialism with Chinese characteristics entering a
new era, it "has become the contradiction between the people's ever-growing needs for a
**better life** and **unbalanced and inadequate development**":

> "中国特色社会主义进入新时代，我国社会主要矛盾已经转化为人民日益增长的美好生活需要和不平衡不充分的发展之间的矛盾。"

| Side | Before (1981) | Since 2017 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs | Material and cultural needs (物质文化需要) | Needs for a better life (美好生活需要) |
| Problem to solve | Backward social production (落后的社会生产) | Unbalanced and inadequate development (不平衡不充分的发展) |

The change illustrates the second point of particularity: the same thing (Chinese
society) has different principal contradictions at different stages.

::: law Two points and a focus | 两点论与重点论相统一
The unequal position and role of contradictions gives the method of combining
"**two points**" with "**a focus**":

- **Two points** (两点论): in studying the development of a complex thing, see both the
  principal contradiction and the secondary ones; in studying one contradiction, see
  both its principal and its secondary aspect.
- **A focus** (重点论): in analysing a complex thing, concentrate on its principal
  contradiction; in analysing one contradiction, concentrate on its principal aspect.

The two go together: seeing both sides without a focus is eclecticism (均衡论); a focus
without both sides is one-sidedness (一点论).
:::

### Universal and particular together

::: law The relation of universality and particularity | 矛盾的普遍性和特殊性辩证关系原理
The universality of contradiction is its **commonality** (共性), absolute and
unconditional; its particularity is its **individuality** (个性), relative and
conditional. They are an organic unity: **the common lies within the individual**
(共性寓于个性之中); there is no commonality apart from individuality, and no
individuality apart from commonality. Under certain conditions they change into each
other.
:::

A course classic: *a white horse is not a horse*.

::: thinker Gongsun Long | 公孙龙
dates: c. 320–250 BC
works: *Gongsun Longzi* (《公孙龙子》), including "On the White Horse" (《白马论》)
A thinker of the School of Names (名家) in the Warring States period, famous for the
paradox "白马非马" (a white horse is not a horse). The course holds that his argument
**cuts the common off from the individual**.
:::

::: example Think it through: what is wrong with "a white horse is not a horse"?
The teacher's question: use the dialectical relation of universality and particularity
and the excerpt from "On the White Horse" to say where the argument goes wrong.

> "马者，所以命形也；白者，所以命色也。命色者非命形也。故曰：'白马非马'。"……"求马，黄、黑马皆可致；求白马，黄、黑马不可致。"

== The argument ==
Gongsun Long says: "horse" names a shape; "white" names a colour; naming a colour is not
naming a shape, so "white horse" is not "horse". And: if you ask for a horse, a yellow
or black one will do; if you ask for a white horse, a yellow or black one won't. So
white horse and horse are different.

== What is right in it ==
He notices a real difference: "horse" (the general) and "white horse" (the particular)
are not identical concepts. The individual has features (whiteness) the general
doesn't name, and the general covers more than any individual.

== The error ==
He turns "different from" into "not belonging to". Commonality lies within
individuality: every white horse **is** a horse; the general "horse" exists only in
particular horses (white, yellow, black), and every particular horse has what is
common to horses. By setting the general against the particular, as if one excluded
the other, the argument **splits commonality from individuality** (割裂了共性和个性).

== Model answer ==
矛盾的普遍性即共性，矛盾的特殊性即个性，二者是辩证统一的：共性寓于个性之中，没有离开个性的共性，也没有离开共性的个性。"马"是共性，"白马"是个性。公孙龙看到了"马"与"白马"的区别，但他把二者的区别夸大为对立，否认了白马也是马、共性存在于个性之中，割裂了共性和个性的辩证关系，因而陷入诡辩。所以"白马非马论"是错误的。我们要坚持共性和个性的统一，既不能以个性否认共性，也不能以共性抹杀个性。
:::

**Methodological significance.** The slides close with Xi Jinping's speech marking the
200th anniversary of Marx's birth (4 May 2018): "社会主义并没有定于一尊、一成不变的套路，只有把科学社会主义基本原则同本国具体实际、历史文化传统、时代要求紧密结合起来，在实践中不断探索总结，才能把蓝图变为美好现实。"
The banner: the principle of universality and particularity is the **philosophical
basis** for combining the basic principles of Marxism with each country's concrete
reality, and the **philosophical foundation of socialism with Chinese
characteristics**. The universal principles (the common) exist only in concrete forms
(the individual): Marxism has to take a particular national form to work at all.

::: callout key The rule for applying any principle
Universal truths must be **combined with concrete conditions**: analyse the concrete
situation concretely (具体问题具体分析), which Lenin called the living soul of Marxism.
Copying a general model regardless of conditions ignores particularity; refusing any
general principle ignores universality.
:::

## Summary

The slides' summary: the law of the unity of opposites, also called **the law of
contradiction**, is the **fundamental law** (根本规律) of materialist dialectics, its
essence and core. Its basic content: **everything contains contradiction; the two
sides are both identical and in struggle, and this drives the movement and development
of things.** It reveals the **source and motive force** of development.

The slides end with **Chinese-style modernisation** (中国式现代化) and its five features
as an application of the law: modernisation of a **huge population**; of **common
prosperity** for all; of **material and spiritual civilisation in harmony**; of
**harmony between humanity and nature**; and of **peaceful development**. Each names a
contradiction to be handled (size and resources, efficiency and fairness, material
and spiritual life, people and nature, one's own development and the world's).

::: callout exam How this lesson is examined
- **Absolute vs relative**: struggle absolute and unconditional, identity relative and
  conditional. Traps reverse them.
- **Sayings → idea**: 相反相成, 有无相生 (identity: mutual dependence); 祸兮福之所倚,
  塞翁失马 (identity: transformation under conditions); 和实生物，同则不继, 和而不同
  (identity contains difference); 白马非马 (splits common and individual);
  具体问题具体分析 (particularity).
- **Two points and a focus**: "抓主要矛盾" is the focus; "统筹兼顾" is the two points.
  Both together.
- 辨析题: "矛盾就是自相矛盾" (wrong: logical vs dialectical); "和谐就是没有矛盾" (wrong:
  harmony is a form of contradiction); "具体的矛盾统一体永远存在" (wrong: identity is
  conditional).
:::
