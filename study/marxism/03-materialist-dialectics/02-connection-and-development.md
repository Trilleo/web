Lesson 3.1 said materialist dialectics looks at the world in connection and in
development. This lesson fills those two words in. They are the **overall features**
(总特征) of materialist dialectics: everything else in the unit (the three laws, the
pairs of categories) explains *how* things are connected and *how* they develop. The
exam asks for the four features of connection with what each requires of us, for the
definition of development, and above all for how to tell a **new thing** from something
that has merely appeared recently. Both of the teacher's discussion questions in this
lesson are answered here in exam form.

## What connection is

Start with a picture from the slides: a **hermit crab carrying sea anemones** on its
shell. The anemones' stinging tentacles protect the crab; the crab carries the anemones
to new food. They are two different animals, each with its own needs, yet each lives
better because of the other. That is a connection: not two things merely side by side,
but two things that act on each other.

Connections reach much further. The **2008 international financial crisis** began with
the abuse of financial derivatives in the United States. Because economies are tied
together by globalisation, it spread from the "virtual" economy of finance to the real
economy of factories and jobs, from the United States to other countries, and from the
economy to politics and society, until it became a worldwide crisis.

Chinese thought noticed this long ago. The slides show four sayings:

| Saying | Source | Idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 物无孤立之理 | Zhang Zai (张载), Northern Song | Nothing exists in isolation |
| 阴阳互补，相依相持 | The yin–yang tradition | Yin and yang complete and support each other |
| 五行变化，相生相克 | The five-elements (五行) tradition | The five elements generate and restrain each other |
| 天人一理，天地万物一体 | Yuan Xie (袁燮), Southern Song | Heaven and humans share one principle; all things are one body |

These are insights, but not yet a science of connection. Materialist dialectics makes
the idea precise.

::: define Connection | 联系
The mutual influence, mutual restriction and mutual interaction between different
things, phenomena and processes, and among the elements inside each of them.

> 联系是指不同的事物、现象和过程之间及其内部各要素之间的相互影响、相互制约、相互作用。
:::

Note two things in the definition. Connection is found both **between** things and
**inside** each thing (between its parts). And it is *mutual*: each side acts on the
other. The hermit crab picture also makes a point the slides write beside it:
**connection is the connection of things in contradiction** (联系是矛盾着的事物的联系).
The crab and the anemone are different, even opposed in their needs, and that is what
makes their connection a real relation and not a sameness. Lesson 3.3 develops this.

::: law The principle of universal connection | 普遍联系原理
Everything in the world is connected with other things, and the parts of everything
are connected with each other; the whole world is a single, interconnected whole.
Connection has four features: it is **objective, universal, diverse and conditional**.
The view of connection is one of the overall features of materialist dialectics
(联系的观点是唯物辩证法的总特征之一).
:::

Each feature has its own methodological requirement, so learn them as pairs:

| Feature | What it means | Methodological requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objectivity (客观性) | Connections belong to things themselves, independent of human will | Start from the connections things really have (从客观事物本身固有的联系出发去认识事物) |
| Universality (普遍性) | Everything is connected: inside things, between things, the whole world | Establish a view of wholeness, systems and openness (确立整体性、系统性、开放性的观念) |
| Diversity (多样性) | Connections are of many kinds | Analyse and grasp each thing's concrete connections (要善于分析和把握事物的具体联系) |
| Conditionality (条件性) | Every connection holds under definite conditions | Analyse and grasp the conditions of a thing's existence and development (注重分析和把握事物存在和发展的各种条件) |

## Connection is objective

::: define Objectivity of connection | 联系的客观性
Connections are inherent in things themselves and do not depend on human will
(事物的联系是事物本身所固有的、不以人的意志为转移的).
:::

People invent connections all the time. The slides call this **脑补** (internet slang
for "filling in the story in your head"): "whenever I see X, I think of Y". When an
official commits suicide, people assume he feared being caught; a woman official must
have traded favours; a temporary worker must be a scapegoat; a BMW in a crash must mean
a rich kid behaving badly; an official denial must be a cover-up. Each of these may
happen to be true in some case, but the link is supplied by the mind, not found in the
facts.

That is why the methodological requirement is to **start from the connections things
themselves have**. The slides add: holding to the objectivity of connection is holding
to **materialism** in the view of connection. Two errors deny it: **idealism**, which
lets the mind make up connections, and **empiricism** (经验主义), which treats one's
own limited experience as the rule.

::: example Think it through: "the people still awake this late are all single"
The teacher's question: is connection objective or subjective? Take the meme "以我的经验，这么晚没有睡的都是单身狗" ("in my experience, anyone still awake this late is single"). What is wrong with it?

== The principle ==
Connection is objective: it is inherent in things and doesn't depend on anyone's will
or feelings. So we must start from the connections that really exist, not from what we
imagine.

== The case ==
The speaker joins two facts, being awake late and being single, by "my experience".
But people stay up for work, study, a sick child, a time difference, insomnia; and
single people go to bed early too. No real, necessary connection ties the two together.
The link is made in the speaker's head and generalised from a few cases.

== The error ==
This is **empiricism**: personal sense experience is taken as the measure of things,
which denies the objectivity of connection. The same mistake lies behind all "脑补":
"whenever I see X, I think of Y".

== Model answer ==
联系具有客观性。事物的联系是事物本身所固有的，不以人的意志为转移，要求我们从事物本身固有的联系出发去认识事物。"这么晚没有睡的都是单身狗"把"晚睡"与"单身"这两个没有必然联系的现象，凭个人经验主观地联系在一起，是经验主义的表现，否认了联系的客观性。因此这一说法是错误的。我们应当实事求是，从客观事物的真实联系出发，而不能凭主观想象或片面经验"脑补"联系。
:::

::: callout pitfall "Objective" doesn't mean "unchangeable by people"
Connections don't depend on our will, but people can **create new connections** by
practice: a bridge connects two banks, the internet connects strangers. Those
connections are still objective once made: they exist whether or not anyone
believes in them. Objective means "not made up in the mind", not "untouched by
humans". (This is the "doubling" of the material world through practice, Lesson 2.2.)
:::

## Connection is universal

Universality has three levels, each with a picture on the slides:

- **Inside things.** The different parts and elements of anything are connected. The
  slide shows interlocking wooden brackets (斗拱) of a Chinese building: take one out and
  the structure weakens.
- **Between things.** Nothing exists alone; everything stands in some connection with
  other things. The slide shows a **temperate grassland food web**: grass, insects,
  spiders, insect-eating birds, frogs, mice, rabbits, snakes, owls and foxes. Remove the
  frogs and the insects multiply, the snakes go hungry, and the change spreads.
- **The whole world.** The world is a single interconnected whole (整个世界是相互联系的统一整体).

### Mediation

How can a butterfly in the Amazon be connected with a tornado in Texas? Not directly,
but through a chain of links: the flapping wings → changes in airflow, air pressure and
the movement of water vapour → the storm. Those middle links are mediation.

::: define Mediation | 中介
The intermediate link or bridge (中间环节、桥梁) through which things connect and act on
each other. Universal connection is realised through mediation (普遍联系通过"中介"来实现).
:::

Mediation explains why universal connection does not mean "everything is directly
linked to everything". Most connections are indirect, passed along chains of
intermediaries.

::: callout pitfall Universal does not mean "anything goes"
"Everything is connected" doesn't let you connect anything to anything you like.
Connections are universal **and** objective: you have to find the real chain of
mediation. Saying "everything is connected" to excuse a made-up link is the 脑补 error
again.
:::

## Connection is diverse

The material world is a unity of diversity, so the connections between things are of
many kinds. The slides give four pairs, each with an example:

| Kind of connection | Example from the slides |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct and indirect** (直接 / 间接) | 城门失火，殃及池鱼: water from the moat puts out the fire at the city gate (direct: fire–water), so the fish in the moat suffer; the fish are connected with the fire only indirectly, through the water |
| **Internal and external** (内部 / 外部) | Harbin Institute of Technology and the FAST radio telescope (天眼): the university's own spirit (internal) met the nation's major needs (external) and "resonated at the same frequency" (同频共振) |
| **Essential and non-essential** (本质 / 非本质) | 种瓜得瓜，种豆得豆: plant melons and get melons, plant beans and get beans (essential); how many melons a vine bears or peas a pod holds (non-essential) |
| **Necessary and accidental** (必然 / 偶然) | A ripe apple must fall to the ground (necessary connection between apple and Earth); that it lands on someone's head is accidental |

The slides call direct and indirect connection the most common forms, which together
make up the universal connection of things.

The FAST story deserves a word. FAST, the 500-metre radio telescope in Guizhou, is made
of six systems; its core, the active reflecting surface, was designed over more than
ten years by a team led by academician **Shen Shizhao** (沈世钊) of HIT, and HIT became
the first technical partner of the National Astronomical Observatories on the project.
Asked how the team overcame its great technical challenges, Shen smiled: "That's what
HIT people are like: when the country needs it, we keep at it until it's done." The
internal factor (the team's spirit) and the external one (the nation's need) together
made the result.

The methodological requirement: be good at **analysing each thing's concrete
connections**. Different connections play different roles: essential and necessary
connections decide what a thing is and where it is going, so they matter most, but the
others still count.

## Connection is conditional

::: define Condition | 条件
The sum of all the elements that are connected with a thing and affect its arising,
growth and dying away (同某一事物相互关联、对其生长灭亡产生影响的诸要素的总和).
Every connection is a connection under definite conditions.
:::

So, in knowing and changing the world, we must act **according to time, place and
conditions** (要善于以时间、地点、条件为转移). The slides give four points:

1. **Take initial conditions seriously.** The outcome of a process can depend extremely
   sensitively on its starting conditions; a tiny deviation at the start can lead to a
   huge difference at the end (the butterfly effect again). The slide's poster: "button
   the first button of life correctly" (扣好人生第一粒扣子). Values formed in youth are
   like the first button of a shirt: get it wrong and every button after it is wrong.
2. **Weigh the nature and number of conditions.** Sunzi: "多算胜，少算不胜，而况于无算乎！"
   (more calculation wins, less calculation loses, let alone none at all). Before acting,
   weigh what conditions you have, and how many.
3. **Conditions can be changed.** Unfavourable conditions can be turned into favourable
   ones, and then into good results. The slide shows oil workers hauling a drilling rig
   by hand, with the slogan "有条件要上，没有条件创造条件也要上" (if the conditions are
   there, go ahead; if not, create them and go ahead). The Daqing oilfield was discovered
   on **26 September 1959**, when the well Songji-3 (松基三井) struck industrial oil.
4. **Oppose both "conditions are everything" and "conditions don't matter".**

| View | What it says | Where it leads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conditions-only view (唯条件论) | Conditions are the root and basis of a thing's existence and development | Metaphysical, passive waiting: "nothing can be done until conditions are perfect" |
| Unconditional view (无条件论) | "Unconditional" really means "under any conditions whatever": as if no condition could stop us | Reckless action that exaggerates subjective initiative (夸大主观能动性的盲动) |

The right course lies between: respect the conditions as they are, and work to change
them. This is the same relation as between objective laws and subjective initiative
(Lesson 2.3).

::: example Think it through: *Once Upon a Time in the Middle East* (《欢迎来龙餐馆》)
The teacher's question: use the film's story to analyse the features of connection.
The film (directed by Wen Muye, 文牧野, starring Shen Teng, 沈腾) follows a Chinese
restaurant owner, Xu Fu (徐福), whose restaurant in a Middle Eastern country is drawn
into a war.

== Connection is universal ==
One small restaurant is tied to everything around it: the owner's personal fate, the
running of the shop, the conflict in the region, and in the end peace between peoples.
Nothing in the story stands alone.

== Connection is objective ==
The restaurant does not choose to be caught up in the crisis: the war reaches it
whether anyone wants it or not. The links between the conflict and the restaurant's
fate are real, not imagined.

== Connection is diverse ==
There are connections of many kinds: direct ones between Xu Fu and his customers;
indirect ones between the great powers' rivalry and the restaurant's fate, passed
through the war (a mediation); internal ones (how the restaurant is run) and external
ones (the political situation).

== Connection is conditional ==
War is the key condition. In peacetime the same restaurant, owner and customers stand
in quite different connections; the war changes all of them.

== Model answer ==
联系具有客观性、普遍性、多样性和条件性。影片中，个人命运、店铺经营、地区冲突乃至人类和平相互联系，体现了联系的普遍性；餐馆不以人的意志为转移地卷入战乱危机，体现了联系的客观性；徐福与食客之间的直接联系、大国博弈与餐馆命运之间的间接联系等，体现了联系的多样性；"战乱"这一关键条件改变了各种联系，体现了联系的条件性。因此，我们要从事物固有的联系出发，确立整体性、开放性观念，具体分析事物的联系，注重把握事物存在和发展的条件。
:::

## The systems view

The four features together ask us to analyse concrete connections, to establish a view
of **wholeness and openness**, to use systems thinking, and to study connections in
motion, not frozen. The slides illustrate it with the "Internet of Everything"
(万物互联): mobile devices now link people with people, people with devices and devices
with devices, more closely and instantly than ever; 5G cities make the web of connection
visible.

The Party's version of this is the **systems view** (系统观念). Xi Jinping's report to
the 20th National Congress of the Communist Party of China (16 October 2022):

> "必须坚持系统观念。万事万物是相互联系、相互依存的。只有用普遍联系的、全面系统的、发展变化的观点观察事物，才能把握事物发展规律。"

**Systems science**, which arose in the 1940s, shows the connected and developing
nature of the material world in scientific detail. But it is still a **specific
science**; it doesn't replace philosophy. The slide's map contrasts three methods:

| Method | What it looks at | The slide's sum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reductionism (还原论) | The microscopic: divide the object ever finer (molecules, quarks, DNA…) | 1 + 1 < 2: the parts miss the whole |
| Holism (整体论) | The macroscopic: cross-disciplinary, the properties of the whole | 1 + 1 = 2 |
| Systems theory (系统论) | Joins the macro and the micro | 1 + 1 ≫ 2: the whole is far more than its parts |

The map also sorts systems from simple to complex (simple systems, simple giant systems,
complex systems, complex giant systems, and special complex giant systems such as the
thinking human brain) and names the "integrated method" (综合集成法): people-centred,
combining human and machine, going from qualitative to quantitative. These are Qian
Xuesen's (钱学森) terms from Chinese systems science.

::: example Dujiangyan: a system that has worked for over two thousand years
Dujiangyan (都江堰), the irrigation system built in Sichuan under Li Bing (李冰) in the
Qin state, around 256 BC, still waters the Chengdu plain. How does it show the systems
view?

== The parts ==
Its headwork has three main parts: the **Fish Mouth** (鱼嘴) levee splits the Min River
into an inner and an outer channel; the **Flying Sand Weir** (飞沙堰) spills floodwater
and sand back to the outer river; the **Bottle-Neck Channel** (宝瓶口) lets a measured
flow into the irrigation network.

== The whole ==
No part works alone. The three are matched to each other, restrain each other and run
together, so that the system "divides four and six, evens out floods and droughts"
(分四六，平潦旱): it shares water between the channels by season, irrigates the fields
and turns away floods.

== The lesson ==
The slide calls it "a perfect union of natural conditions and human wisdom". The
builders worked with the river's own connections (its bends, its sediment) as one
whole, instead of fighting each problem separately. As the Qing writer Chen Danran
(陈澹然) put it: "惟自古不谋万世者不足谋一时，不谋全局者不足谋一域" (whoever does not
plan for all ages cannot plan for one time; whoever does not plan for the whole cannot
plan for one part).
:::

## Development

Change is the next idea. The Chinese idiom **沧海桑田** ("the blue sea becomes mulberry
fields") comes from Ge Hong's (葛洪) *Biographies of Immortals* (《神仙传》): in the time
of Emperor Huan of the Eastern Han, the immortal Magu (麻姑), looking eighteen or
nineteen, said she had already seen the Eastern Sea turn into mulberry fields three
times. Nature shows the same on a grand scale: the **Qinghai–Tibet Plateau** was once
ocean and has risen over tens of millions of years into the highest plateau on Earth.

The slide's lesson from the plateau: **the interconnection of things causes motion,
change and development** (事物的相互联系引起运动、变化和发展). Connection and
development are not two separate facts: things change *because* they act on each
other. Engels:

> "相互作用是事物的真正的终极原因。我们不能比对这种相互作用的认识追溯得更远了，因为在这之后没有什么要认识的东西了。"
> (Engels, *Dialectics of Nature*)

The slides draw it as connection → (through the unity of opposites) → development.

::: callout note The plateau's geology
The slide says the plateau's external cause is the attraction and repulsion between the
Earth and the Sun, and its internal cause the interaction of the "Tarim plate" and the
Indian plate. In the usual geological account, the plateau was raised mainly by the
**Indian plate colliding with the Eurasian plate**, beginning roughly 50 million years
ago. The philosophical point, that the plateau rose through the interaction of its own
parts, is the same.
:::

::: define Development | 发展
The upward, forward movement of things, from simple to complex and from lower to
higher. It is driven by the unity and struggle of opposites, and its **essence** is the
**arising of new things and the dying away of old things**.

> 发展是事物由简单到复杂、由低级到高级的变化趋势，其实质是新事物的产生和旧事物的灭亡。
:::

Two details from the slides: the new thing **matures inside** the old (it doesn't drop
from the sky), and the new thing **sublates** (扬弃) the old: it discards what is
outdated and keeps what is still of value (the full idea of sublation is in Lesson
3.5). Engels gives life as the model of development through contradiction:

> "生命也是存在于物体和过程本身中的不断地自行产生并自行解决的矛盾；矛盾一停止，生命也就停止，死亡就到来。"
> (Engels, *Anti-Dühring*)

::: callout pitfall Motion, change, development
Motion (运动) is any change at all (Lesson 2.2). **Development** is a particular
direction of change: forward and upward, the new replacing the old. A thing that only
grows bigger, moves elsewhere or goes round in a circle changes without developing.
That is exactly the metaphysical view of "development" in Lesson 3.1's table.
:::

The slides' map of development:

| Aspect | What the course says |
| --- | --- |
| Essence (实质) | The arising of new things and the dying away of old things |
| Causes (原因) | The unity and struggle of opposites; the new inherits the rational elements of the old and adds new content; in society, the new fits the fundamental interests and demands of the masses |
| Form (形式) | A process: in form, an alternation of duration in time and extension in space; in content, renewal of the forms of motion, states, functions and relations |
| Scope (范围) | Nature, human society and human knowledge |

## New things and old things

::: define New thing and old thing | 新事物与旧事物
A **new thing** is one that fits the forward direction of history and has a great
future (合乎历史前进方向、具有远大前途的东西). An **old thing** is one that has lost
its historical necessity and is dying away (丧失历史必然性、日趋灭亡的东西).
:::

The definition says nothing about **when** a thing appeared. The basic test is
**whether it fits the necessary trend of historical development**
(区别的根本标志：是否符合历史发展的必然趋势). The slides list recent phenomena to judge:
high-tech cheating, social life gone virtual and atomised, people who never leave home
(宅男、宅女), campus loans, excessive "innovation" in financial instruments. All are
recent; none, in the course's view, is a "new thing" in the philosophical sense. And
the test isn't authority either: an expert, an elder, a teacher or a parent can call
something new or outdated, but their word doesn't decide it; the trend of history does.

::: law The invincibility of new things | 新事物不可战胜
New things are bound to defeat old things. A new thing may look weak for a while, but
whatever difficulties and twists it meets, it will in the end overcome the old thing,
however strong the old one looks. Reasons:

1. a new thing has new elements, structure and functions, and so is **better adapted**
   to the changed environment and conditions;
2. it sublates the old thing: it keeps the old thing's rational elements and adds **new
   content** the old one cannot hold;
3. in society, it fits the **fundamental interests and demands of the masses**, and so
   wins their support.
:::

**Methodological requirement:** tell new from old by whether a thing fits the necessary
trend of history, not by when it appeared or who praises it; support and protect new
things; and keep faith in them through temporary setbacks, since the road is twisting
but the direction is forward.

The slides set two sayings side by side. Hegel wrote "凡是现实的都是合理的，凡是合理的都是现实的"
(what is actual is rational, and what is rational is actual; *Philosophy of Right*,
1821). Engels drew out its revolutionary side: "凡是现存的，都一定要灭亡" (all that
exists deserves to perish). For Hegel, "actual" (现实) means what is necessary, not
whatever happens to exist; something that has lost its necessity loses its claim to be
"actual" and must give way. So a thing that merely exists is not thereby justified
forever. The human-evolution staircase and a seedling pushing through the soil on the
same slide make the point visually.

::: example Think it through: is everything newly appeared a new thing?
The teacher's question: from the definitions of new and old things, what is the
standard for telling them apart? Is "something that has newly appeared" the same as a
"new thing"?

== The definitions ==
A new thing fits the forward direction of history and has a great future; an old thing
has lost its historical necessity and is dying away. Neither definition mentions time
of appearance.

== The standard ==
The fundamental mark of difference is **whether a thing fits the necessary trend of
historical development**. Time of appearance, novelty of form, popularity or the
opinions of authorities are not the test.

== Applying it ==
Campus loans and high-tech cheating appeared recently, but they harm students and
society and go against the direction of development: they are not new things. Some
newly appeared things are revivals of old ones in new clothes. Conversely, a new
thing can look weak and be rejected at first: the new-democratic revolution (below)
was small for years.

== Model answer ==
新事物是指合乎历史前进方向、具有远大前途的东西；旧事物是指丧失历史必然性、日趋灭亡的东西。区分新旧事物的根本标志，在于是否符合历史发展的必然趋势，而不在于出现时间的先后，也不在于形式是否新奇、权威人物如何评价。新出现的事物不一定是新事物：如高科技作弊、校园贷等虽然新近出现，却违背历史发展趋势，不是新事物。因此，我们要用历史发展的必然趋势来判断新旧事物，支持和保护真正的新生事物。
:::

::: example Why the new-democratic revolution won
The slides' case study: read the victory of the new-democratic revolution (1919–1949)
as an instance of the invincibility of new things.

== The old thing ==
Imperialism and feudal forces, represented by the Kuomintang reactionaries. For a long
time they were far stronger: they held state power, armies and economic resources.

== The twists ==
The temporary advantage of the old showed in real setbacks: the defeat of the Great
Revolution (1927), the sacrifice of Party members and revolutionary masses, the loss of
the fifth counter-campaign against "encirclement and suppression" (1934), and the forced
Long March (1934–36). This is the **tortuousness** (曲折性) of development.

== The new thing ==
The forces of the new-democratic revolution, led by the Communist Party of China. At
first weak, they represented the direction of social development, fitted the laws of
history and the fundamental interests of the great majority of the people.

== The outcome ==
After more than twenty years of hard and twisting struggle they overthrew the "three
great mountains" (imperialism, feudalism and bureaucrat-capitalism) and founded the
People's Republic of China in 1949. This is the **progressiveness** (前进性) of
development: the new thing's final victory.
:::

## The world as a collection of processes

Engels sums up the developmental view in one sentence:

> "一个伟大的基本思想，即认为世界不是既成事物的集合体，而是过程的集合体，其中各个似乎稳定的事物同它们在我们头脑中的思想映象即概念一样都处在生成和灭亡的不断变化中，在这种变化中，尽管有种种表面的偶然性，尽管有种种暂时的倒退，前进的发展终究会实现……"
> (Engels, *Ludwig Feuerbach and the End of Classical German Philosophy*)

A river, a city, a person, a theory: each looks like a finished object, but each is a
stretch of a process, coming into being and passing away.

::: law The world is a collection of processes | 世界是过程的集合体
The world is not a collection of ready-made things but a collection of processes. Four
points:

1. the development of nature is a process;
2. the development of human society is a process;
3. every truth and every piece of knowledge is concrete, belonging to a particular
   process;
4. every opposition has only a relative meaning and can only be understood inside a
   particular process.
:::

**Methodological requirement:** when we come to know a thing, we must understand it
not only as a **system** (a whole) but also as a **dynamic process**; we must study not
only the relations among its elements and parts, but also the relations among the
stages of its development. And we must clearly oppose four wrong views that deny the
world is a process:

| Wrong view | What it claims | Why it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical cyclism (历史循环论) | History goes round in circles, repeating itself | Denies forward development |
| The "end of history" (历史终结论) | History has reached its final form (in Francis Fukuyama's version, Western liberal democracy) | Puts an end point on a process |
| Dogmatism (教条主义) | Fixed formulas apply everywhere, whatever the process | Ignores that truth is concrete, tied to a process |
| The eternal-truth view (永恒真理观) | Some truths hold unchanged forever | Ignores that knowledge, too, develops |

## Lecture summary

The slides end the lecture with a map:

- **The opposition of dialectics and metaphysics** (Lesson 3.1): dialectics looks at
  things in connection, development and comprehensively, and admits contradiction;
  metaphysics looks at them in isolation, statically and one-sidedly, and denies it.
  Materialist dialectics is the unity of **objective and subjective dialectics**, and
  is in essence **critical and revolutionary** (both in Lesson 3.6).
- **Universal connection**: what connection is; its four features (objectivity,
  universality, diversity, conditionality).
- **Eternal development**: universal connection is the premise of development; the
  unity and struggle of opposites is its cause; the arising of the new and the dying of
  the old is its essence. The world is a collection of processes, and the wrong views
  that deny this must be clearly opposed.

::: callout exam How this lesson is examined
- **Four features + four requirements** of connection: learn them as pairs (the table
  under the principle of universal connection).
- 辨析题: "新出现的事物就是新事物" → wrong: the test is whether it fits the necessary
  trend of history. "联系是普遍的，所以任何事物之间都有联系，可以随意联系" → wrong:
  connection is also objective and conditional.
- Sayings: 城门失火，殃及池鱼 (indirect connection through mediation); 种瓜得瓜 (essential
  connection); 沧海桑田, 沉舟侧畔千帆过 (development, new replacing old); 不谋全局者，不足谋一域
  (the whole, systems view).
- 唯条件论 vs 无条件论: the first waits passively, the second acts blindly.
:::

::: cards Connection and development
Connection (联系) :: The mutual influence, restriction and interaction between things, phenomena and processes, and among the elements inside each
Four features of connection :: Objectivity, universality, diversity, conditionality (客观性、普遍性、多样性、条件性)
Objectivity of connection requires… :: Starting from the connections things themselves have (从客观事物本身固有的联系出发)
Universality of connection requires… :: A view of wholeness, systems and openness (整体性、系统性、开放性)
Diversity of connection requires… :: Analysing and grasping each thing's concrete connections
Conditionality of connection requires… :: Analysing the conditions of existence and development; acting according to time, place and conditions
Mediation (中介) :: The intermediate link or bridge through which things connect; universal connection is realised through it
Four pairs of kinds of connection :: Direct/indirect, internal/external, essential/non-essential, necessary/accidental
城门失火，殃及池鱼 shows… :: Indirect connection: the fish are linked to the fire only through the water (a mediation)
Conditions-only view vs unconditional view :: 唯条件论: conditions are everything, so wait passively; 无条件论: act as if conditions don't matter, blind action
Development (发展) :: The upward, forward movement from simple to complex, lower to higher; its essence is the arising of the new and the dying away of the old
New thing (新事物) :: What fits the forward direction of history and has a great future
Fundamental test of new vs old :: Whether it fits the necessary trend of historical development, not when it appeared
Why new things are invincible :: Better adapted (new structure and functions); sublate the old and add new content; fit the people's fundamental interests
"世界不是既成事物的集合体，而是过程的集合体" :: Engels, *Ludwig Feuerbach and the End of Classical German Philosophy*
Four wrong views denying the world as process :: Historical cyclism, the end of history, dogmatism, the eternal-truth view
:::
