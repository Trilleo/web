This course starts with **heat**. A gas looks calm, but it is a swarm of molecules
flying about at hundreds of metres per second; kinetic theory explains pressure and
temperature from that swarm. Thermodynamics then follows energy as it moves between
heat and work: the **first law** keeps the books, heat engines and refrigerators put
it to use, and the **second law** says which way things can go, with entropy as its
measure. The second half turns to **light**, starting with what makes two beams able
to interfere.

Every lesson starts from the idea, then the laws and formulas, then worked examples
with real numbers. Play with the diagrams and graphs — move a slider and watch a
curve change — and finish with the quiz. Chinese terms sit beside the English ones,
so the lessons match the slides and the exam.

The course is still being taught: lessons follow the lectures up to 16 October 2026,
and new ones are added as the term goes on.
